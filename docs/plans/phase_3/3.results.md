# Phase 3 results: retrieval through the concept space

> Produced by T19. Every figure below is read from an on-disk artifact named by the configuration
> that produced it. Nothing here is recomputed for the report.

## Verdict

**The concept space retrieves, retrieves far worse than either baseline, and contributes nothing to a
fused system.** Its fusion weight was fitted on dev over the declared eleven-point grid and came back
at `w = 1.0` - all the weight on dense, none on the concept space. System B is therefore dense, metric
for metric, at every budget on both splits.

**The dense+BM25 control, built by the same code and fitted by the same procedure, does improve on
dense**: +3.9 points of Full Support on test at 2,048 tokens. That is the number that makes this
phase's verdict attributable rather than merely disappointing. The headroom a hybrid can exploit was
real and available; a cheap lexical signal took it and the concept space did not. Without HU-5's
control, "System B ties dense" would have been uninterpretable.

**One result points somewhere.** Conceptual-only retrieval answers 5 dev questions dense does not, and
**4 of those 5 are among the 72 that defeat both baselines** - the multi-hop failures this project
exists for. The complementary signal exists. What does not exist is a way to extract it by a convex
combination of scores: at any weight loud enough to contribute those 4, the concept space loses more
questions than it gains. Phase 4 does not average scores, and that is now a sharper question than it
was before this phase ran.

One finding about how the question enters the space, and it holds in all eight cells:

**The unclipped projection wins everywhere, by between 0.9 and 18.1 points.** `projection_full` is the
highest figure in every (K, view) pair, and the other two arms lose to it in all eight. Between
themselves they swap - sparse coding beats the truncated projection at K = 2,048 in both views and
loses to it at the other three K - so the ranking of the two losing arms is not a finding, and only
their common defeat is.

Both of them lose the same thing, by different routes. **The truncation** was the principled arm:
Phase 2 calibrated `X` to 8-16 active concepts per unit, so a top-16 query describes the question at
the resolution the units are described at. **Sparse coding** was the symmetric one: code the query
under the same non-negative L1 penalty the paragraphs were coded with, so that query and unit are
described the same way. Both keep a handful of concepts - the ones that best rebuild the query vector -
and set every other coordinate to zero. The plain projection keeps all of them, including the weak
ones, and wins. What a question needs is evidently not a compact description of itself but every
concept it touches at all, however faintly: the weak coordinates still meet the units' rows, and a
zero cannot.

The two shapes are the result of this phase:

- **System B's curve is monotone increasing and flat at the top.** There is no interior optimum to
  find. Every point of conceptual weight costs recall, until the last tenth costs nothing because the
  conceptual contribution is already inside the noise.
- **The control's curve has a genuine interior optimum**, 0.9308 at `w = 0.5`, above both endpoints
  (0.8592 for BM25 alone, 0.9017 for dense alone). Two signals that are each individually worse than
  the mixture: the textbook shape of complementarity, measured on the same 600 questions by the same
  code.
- **RRF, the parameter-free reference, behaves exactly as its mechanics predict.** It reads ranks and
  gives both components an equal vote, so it is catastrophic for System B (0.7408, far below dense
  alone) and nearly optimal for the control (0.9208 against the fitted 0.9308). The weighted scheme
  earned its one degree of freedom here, by 0.1608 for System B and 0.0100 for the control.

### Would another K have changed the verdict? No.

The space decision was taken inside what 600 questions can resolve, so the branch that was not taken
is a fair question. The System B fusion was therefore fitted on **all four spaces**, each on its own
best arm and damped as its own damping decision would have had it - by the same function, over the
same grid, on dev. A robustness reading taken after the freeze: it decides nothing, and test is not
involved.

| K | Weight chosen | Weighted | RRF | Dense alone (`w = 1.0`) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **512** (selected) | 1.0 | 0.9017 | 0.7408 | 0.9017 |
| 1,024 | 1.0 | 0.9017 | 0.7658 | 0.9017 |
| 2,048 | **0.9** | **0.9025** | 0.7825 | 0.9017 |
| 4,096 | 1.0 | 0.9017 | 0.7217 | 0.9017 |

**Three of the four fit at `w = 1.0`, and the fourth is indistinguishable from it.** K = 2,048 is the
only space whose curve peaks anywhere but the endpoint, and it peaks 0.0008 above dense alone - half a
question in 600, against a standard error of 0.0122 at that figure. It is a fifteenth of the noise.

So the structural tie-break that kept K = 512 over K = 2,048 decided **which space Phase 4 inherits**,
and nothing about this phase's verdict. Had it gone the other way, the fitted weight would have been
0.9 instead of 1.0 and System B would still have been dense to three decimals. That is worth knowing
precisely because the tie-break was applied inside the noise: the one decision this phase could not
resolve on evidence turns out not to have been load-bearing.

## The damping variant, on all four spaces

The declared rarity weight `w_j = log(1 + N / (1 + df_j))` was the **decision** on the selected space
only (D8 takes it there). Reported here on all four spaces, on each space's own best arm, as HU-6 asks
- a reading on dev, not a choice, and taken after the freeze:

| K | Hub share | Undamped | Damped (`idf`) | Delta |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **512** | 7.2% | 0.5017 | **0.5450** | **+0.0433** |
| 1,024 | 7.0% | 0.4108 | 0.4775 | +0.0667 |
| 2,048 | 66.7% | 0.4825 | 0.5300 | +0.0475 |
| 4,096 | 99.4% | 0.4617 | 0.4383 | **-0.0233** |

**It helps where no hub exists, which makes it a finding about rarity weighting and not about the
hub.** HU-6 wrote that case down before the number was seen: the variant was motivated by the atom
that activates on 66.7% of the corpus at K = 2,048 and 99.4% at K = 4,096, and it helps most at
K = 1,024, where the largest concept covers 7.0% of the pool. Demoting common concepts is simply a
better way to weight this space, hub or no hub.

**And it stops helping exactly where the hub is worst.** At K = 4,096 the weighting *costs* 2.3
points. The reason is visible in the structural column: 2,610 of those 4,096 concepts are supported by
fewer than 30 units. A weight that rises as support falls is, in that space, mostly a weight on
concepts the corpus barely uses - so it amplifies the thinnest evidence rather than the most specific.
Rarity weighting and dilution pull in opposite directions, and past some K the dilution wins.

No concept was pruned, merged or removed anywhere in this phase. The damping multiplies the query
vector; `X` and the atoms are the Phase 2 artifacts, byte for byte.

### What carried the five questions dense loses

The five dev questions conceptual-only answers and dense does not, with the three concepts the damped
query vector activates most. The third slot is frequently noise - *"Covey's 7 Habits franchise"* has
nothing to do with a gym owner's birthday - so the pattern is in the first two:

| Question | Top concepts |
|---|---|
| "What man, born in 1963, was instrumental in bringing the Boston Red Sox to the stadium City of Palms Park in Fort Myers, Florida?" | *Florida Cities and Lakes* · *Baseball players, teams, and records* |
| "Where did the opera in which Iva Pacetti starred as Elena first premier?" | *Italian opera figures and institutions* · *Opera factual descriptions* |
| "John Stoltenberg is the managing editor of the magazine that focuses on what?" | *Magazine profile summaries* · *Political news/media outlets and figures* |
| "At what height does State Route 160 start?" | *Highway route descriptions* · *Roads and neighborhood boundaries* |
| "What is the owner of Straight Blast Gym Ireland's birthday?" | *Irish biographical and local-heritage entries* · *Combat sports fighters and events by weight class/title* |

**Every one of them is a conjunction of two topics**, and in four of the five the two concepts are
about different things: a Florida place *and* baseball; an Irish biography *and* combat sports; a
magazine *and* politics. That is what the representation can express and a single embedding cannot: a
dense query is one point and has to land near one paragraph, while a concept vector can ask for the
paragraph that is about *both* of two subjects at once - which is the shape of a bridge.

It is five questions out of 600, from a system that loses 284 others, and the inference is a
hypothesis rather than a result. But it is a hypothesis with names attached, and it says where to
point Phase 4: not at "retrieve better in concept space", but at the conjunctions.

## What these numbers are not

- **They are not a verdict on the hypothesis of §69.** This phase measured one hop. The failure mode
  the project exists for - the retriever finds the entity the question names and never reaches the
  bridge entity it does not - has no one-hop remedy in any space, and Phase 1 isolated it before this
  phase began. System B landing on dense leaves §69 entirely open.
- **They are not a verdict on sparse concept representations in general.** One corpus, one embedding
  model, one induction method, one set of four K values, one benchmark. `sparse_coding` losing to a
  plain projection is a finding about *this* dictionary at *these* penalties, not about sparse coding.
- **They do not measure Type C questions**, the ones where satellite knowledge would have to emerge
  from the corpus rather than from an annotator. HotpotQA's hops are laid out by hand. That gap is
  declared in the master plan and is Phase 6 material.
- **The 4 recovered questions are 4 questions.** Out of 600, on one split, from a system that loses 284
  others. They are a reason to look, not a result.
