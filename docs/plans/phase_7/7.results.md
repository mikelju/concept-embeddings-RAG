## Verdict

**Yes. `data/phase7/selection.json` → `selected: "gliner"`, and the single held-out run keeps
83.5% of what the Claude extraction bought, at roughly one thousandth of the projected extraction
cost.**

| Test (1,400), Full Support @2,048 | Value | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Dense | 0.8250 = 1,155 / 1,400 | Phase 6, `run-dense-test-20260917T215214+0000.json` |
| Dense + BM25 | 0.8643 = 1,210 / 1,400 | Phase 6, `run-hybrid-bm25-test-20260917T215216+0000.json` |
| Dense + Entity Hop (**Claude**) | 0.8900 = 1,246 / 1,400 | Phase 6, `run-hybrid-entity-hop-test-20260917T215231+0000.json` |
| **Dense + Entity Hop (GLiNER)** | **0.8793 = 1,231 / 1,400** | **Phase 7, `data/phase7/test.json`** |

The retention arithmetic, in whole questions and in points:

```text
Claude gain over Dense   = 1246 - 1155 = 91 questions   = 0.8900 - 0.8250 = 0.0650
GLiNER gain over Dense   = 1231 - 1155 = 76 questions   = 0.8793 - 0.8250 = 0.0543
retention                = 76 / 91 = 0.8352            = 0.0543 / 0.0650 = 0.8352
```

**GLiNER recovers 76 of the 91 questions Claude added, 83.5%.** It also beats the Dense + BM25
control by 21 questions (+1.50 points) and sits 15 questions below Claude (−1.07 points).

The three headline findings, each argued below: the **cost collapse** is real and measured; the
**dev figure was more pessimistic than test**, which is the safe direction; and the phase turned up
a **three-way trade-off between quality, structure and cost** that was not the question it asked.

## The one question, and the answer

> Can the expensive Claude-based entity extraction be replaced by a cheap or local extractor
> without materially losing the Entity Hop retrieval gain?

**It can.** Both local candidates cleared the preregistered retention bar (517 of 600 dev questions)
and the economic bar; the rule selected GLiNER; its held-out figure keeps 83.5% of the gain. Nothing
else in the retrieval system changed — same pool (`unit_set_hash = 101f564fdcca620c`), same
`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` at revision `5c38ec7c…`, same `normalization-v1`, same `EntityHopStage`
with P1 only, one hop, read depth 10, rarity `log(1 + N / (1 + df))`. Only the reader changed.

## Extraction, measured

Both passes ran on **rented RunPod compute**, which plan decision D14 approves and which is what
made the phase answerable at all. From both manifests → `hardware`: 1× NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090
(24 GB), `Linux-6.8.0-124-generic-x86_64-with-glibc2.39` (Ubuntu 24.04, driver CUDA UMD 13.3),

## Dev, and the selection rule as applied

Every candidate refitted its fusion weight on the 600 dev questions with the inherited objective
(gold recall @2,048, `FUSION_WEIGHT_GRID` plus RRF, Phase 3 tie-breaks). Sources:
`data/phase7/{gliner,spacy}/dev.json` → `fit`, `metrics`.

| Dev (600), Full Support @2,048 | Value | Questions | Fitted fusion |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Dense (inherited) | 0.8117 | 487 | — |
| Dense + BM25 (inherited) | 0.8633 | 518 | `weighted`, dense 0.5 / bm25 0.5 |
| Dense + Entity Hop, Claude (inherited) | 0.8783 | 527 | `weighted`, dense 0.7 / entity-hop 0.3 |
| **Dense + Entity Hop, GLiNER** | **0.8633** | **518** | `weighted`, **dense 0.7 / entity-hop 0.3** |
| **Dense + Entity Hop, spaCy** | **0.8750** | **525** | `weighted`, **dense 0.8 / entity-hop 0.2** |

The bar was computed once and frozen in `config.py` before any candidate ran; the retention
arithmetic beneath it is in whole questions:

```text
bar          = 0.8117 + 0.75 * (0.8783 - 0.8117) = 0.8617  =  517 of 600 dev questions
Claude gain  = 527 - 487 = 40 questions
GLiNER       = 518 - 487 = 31  ->  31 / 40 = 0.775   (77.5%)
spaCy        = 525 - 487 = 38  ->  38 / 40 = 0.950   (95.0%)
```

Both pass the retention bar — GLiNER by one question, spaCy by eight — and both pass the economic
bar: no per-paragraph third-party API call, 0.00 USD software cost, projected FullWiki wall clock
under 48 h, infrastructure spend under 30 USD (`selection.json` → `candidates[].economics`).

**The rule then ranks by the lowest projected FullWiki cost and time** (`rule.ranking`: `"lowest
projected FullWiki usd, then hours, then higher dev full support"`). GLiNER's 6.34 USD / 8.56 h
beats spaCy's 11.08 USD / 14.97 h, so the ranking is `["gliner", "spacy"]` and the recorded reason
reads:

> "gliner cleared the retention bar and the economic bar, and ranked first on projected FullWiki
> cost and time among the candidates that did"

### The Dense + BM25 dev line, read honestly

`selection.json` → `candidates[0].bm25_dev_line` records `below_line: false` for GLiNER. It is
`false` **only because the two figures are exactly equal**: GLiNER supported 518 of 600 dev
questions, and Dense + BM25 supports 518 of 600 — the identical count, not a margin. One question
fewer and GLiNER would have sat below a free lexical complement.

The `note` string in that field — *"a reported qualification, never a rejection: a free lexical
complement did at least as well on dev"* — is **fixed text describing what the field means**,
emitted for every candidate whether or not it applies. It is not a verdict on GLiNER. For GLiNER,
however, it happens to hold literally: on dev, a free lexical complement did *at least as well*,
exactly as well.

## The dev/test discrepancy, stated honestly

On dev, GLiNER landed **exactly on the Dense + BM25 line**: 518 of 600 against 518 of 600. On the
held-out split that did **not** reproduce — GLiNER beat Dense + BM25 by 21 questions, +1.50 points:

| Split | GLiNER retention of Claude's gain | GLiNER vs Dense + BM25 |
|---|---:|---:|
| dev (600) | 31 / 40 = **77.5%** | 518 vs 518, a tie |
| test (1,400) | 76 / 91 = **83.5%** | 1,231 vs 1,210, **+21 questions** |

**Dev was the more pessimistic of the two.** For a selection rule that is the safe direction to
err in: the rule admitted a candidate on the weaker of the two readings and the held-out split then
returned a stronger one. Had it gone the other way — a flattering dev figure and a weaker test
figure — the selection would have been made on a number that did not hold.

This is a single observation on a single split with no interval attached; it is not evidence that
GLiNER is *reliably* better than BM25 by 21 questions. What the phase claims is the recorded
figure: 0.8793 on test, 83.5% of Claude's gain, selected on dev without ever reading test.

## The trade-off Phase 7 actually found

The phase asked one question and answered it. It also turned up something it did not ask for: the
three extractors do not line up on a single axis of "quality for money". They trade **quality**,
**representational structure** and **cost** against each other.

| | Claude | spaCy | GLiNER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality (dev retention) | reference, 100% | **95.0%** (38/40) | 77.5% (31/40) |
| Dev Full Support @2,048 | 0.8783 (527) | **0.8750 (525)** | 0.8633 (518) |
| Labels | 5 categories | 11 | 5 |
| Distinct nodes | 96,416 | 97,189 | **81,617** |
| Nodes per paragraph, mean / median | — | 9.45 / 8 | **6.93 / 6** |
| Positive hop candidates, mean / median | — | 1,526 / 493 | **161 / 40** |
| Largest hub (paragraphs on one node) | — | 4,085 | **1,186** |
| Cost, 19,366 paragraphs | 25.9848 USD measured | 0.05 USD | **0.03 USD** |
| Cost, 5M, projected | ~6,700 USD | 11.08 USD | **6.34 USD** |

Structure figures from `selection.json` → `candidates[].index` and `candidates[].hop`. Read plainly:
**Claude** is the highest quality and prohibitively expensive at FullWiki scale; **spaCy** buys
nearly all of Claude's quality for nothing but pays for it with a much **denser, less scalable
representation** — 11 labels rather than 5, a median of 493 candidates per hop rather than 40, a
largest hub of 4,085 paragraphs rather than 1,186; **GLiNER** gives up some quality for a far more
**contained** structure that is the cheapest and fastest to scale.

Two smaller measured facts point the same way. spaCy's fit trusted its hop *less* (dense 0.8 /
entity-hop 0.2, against GLiNER's 0.7 / 0.3), and spaCy's hop alone is the weaker ranker — at the
grid point where the fused system is entity-hop only, dev gold recall @2,048 is 0.1075 for spaCy
against 0.1492 for GLiNER (`dev.json` → `fit.curve["0.0"]`). spaCy proposes far more candidates and
converts fewer of them: 552 units introduced into the fused dev context against GLiNER's 917
(`hop.introduced_context_units`).

### The four statements this phase records

- **Best dev retrieval among the cheap extractors:** **spaCy**, 525 / 600, retaining 95% of
  Claude's dev gain.
- **Protocol-selected extractor:** **GLiNER**, 518 / 600, selected by the preregistered cost-and-time
  ordering among the candidates that passed both bars.
- **Why the selection is not overridden after the fact:** overriding it *after observing dev* would
  invalidate the preregistered selection rule, which is the only thing that makes the held-out
  figure mean anything. Beyond the protocol, spaCy creates substantially larger candidate sets and
  hubs.
- **Implication for Phase 8:** Phase 8 inherits **GLiNER**, so that **Strong Dense is the only
  changed variable**. If Phase 8 changed the Dense retriever *and* the extractor at once, no
  improvement and no degradation could be attributed to either. spaCy remains a candidate for a
  later scale-and-representation experiment.

### One open question, left open

Does spaCy's dev advantage over GLiNER survive at FullWiki scale, or does it collapse under
candidate explosion and hub growth? A median of 493 candidates per hop on 19,366 paragraphs is a
different proposition on five million. **This is a future question, not Phase 7 work and not an
active phase.** It is recorded so it is not lost; writing it down does not promote it into Phase 8
or 9.

## Security

Targeted review of the three declared surfaces, catalogued in
[`docs/security/README.md`](../../security/README.md#phase-7--2026-09-18-targeted-review-three-surfaces-only).
**No Critical and no High.** `pip-audit` over the environment with the `phase7` group installed
reports no known vulnerabilities; the pinned download was checked on the real files and contains no
pickle archive; GLiNER takes its safetensors branch and the pickle fallback it never reaches is
`weights_only=True`. Two Low rows are open: **SEC-031**, the extraction manifest records no digest
of the weight file it loaded; **SEC-032**, `spacy.load` executes publisher code by construction.
One observation, **OBS-012**, on the transitive footprint `gliner` pulls through `transformers`.

The review predates the passes, and the passes opened no new surface: rented compute executed the
same pinned code, downloaded the same allow-listed files and wrote the same artifacts.

**SEC-031 is deferred rather than fixed (2026-09-19).** Hashing `model.safetensors` into the
manifest is cheap, but doing it now would rewrite a measured, frozen artifact to add provenance that
was not recorded when the run happened. The weight digest is to be recorded prospectively, before
the next corpus-scale extraction with this extractor; Phase 8 reuses this extraction and does not
reopen it. Phase 7 stays reproducible to the pinned repository revision without binding its manifest
to the exact weight-file bytes.

**The targeted review satisfies the audit planned for this phase.** The spec settled a three-surface
review instead of a full `/8-auditar` pass before implementation began, S6 ran it, and the
`/7-verificar` pass of 2026-09-19 found no material gap against the spec. No separate audit is
outstanding for Phase 7.

## Provenance and reproducibility

Two stages, and the digest chain that binds each artifact to the one before it:

```text
cer cheap-extract --extractor {gliner,spacy}  ->  data/phase7/<id>/{records.jsonl.gz, extraction.json}
cer cheap-eval                                ->  data/phase7/<id>/{nodes-*, run-*-dev-*.json, dev.json}
                                                  data/phase7/selection.json
cer cheap-eval --test                         ->  data/phase7/gliner/run-*-test-*.json, test.json

extraction 840b4f78325d5c33…  ->  node index 0aec5c32440ff600…  ->  dev b8540db72ea474af…
  ->  selection 002d3bc484581f47…  ->  held-out run aaa1539723c34ca9…
```

`test.json` carries `dev_digest`, `selection_digest` and `run_digest`, so the held-out figure cannot
be detached from the dev reading and the selection that authorized it. The configuration digests
`2f7864661b8ce7ff` (GLiNER) and `1e450b748cfc2d83` (spaCy) stand in for Phase 5's prompt digest:
they answer *which extractor configuration produced these records*. `cheap-eval` refuses a further
candidate once `selection.json` exists; `cheap-eval --test` refuses without a selection, on a null
selection, and on a second run.

Inherited reference figures are held equal to `config.PHASE_7_REFERENCE_DEV_FULL_SUPPORT` and
`PHASE_7_REFERENCE_HELD_OUT_FULL_SUPPORT` by a data-dependent test, so no Phase 6 number here was
typed from memory, and no Phase 5 or Phase 6 artifact, digest pin or expected count was written to
or invalidated. The only figures not stored in an artifact are the retention percentages and
question counts (share × split size, and differences of those counts) and the cost ratios (division
of the figures in §"Cost, recorded precisely"); everything else is quoted with its artifact and
field.

## Conclusion

**Phase 7 is complete, and its recorded selection is `gliner`
(`data/phase7/selection.json` → `selected`).** A local zero-shot NER model, run once over the
19,366-paragraph pool on a rented RTX 4090 in two minutes for an attributable 0.03 USD, produces an
entity index whose one-hop, query-blind Entity Hop keeps **83.5%** of what a 25.9848 USD
Claude extraction bought on the held-out split — and beats the free lexical control it was allowed
to merely tie. spaCy kept more of the gain on dev (95%) and was not selected, because the rule that
made the held-out figure meaningful was written before the figure existed and ranks on projected
FullWiki cost and time; spaCy also builds a markedly denser index, with hubs three times larger and
candidate sets an order of magnitude wider. Phase 8 therefore inherits GLiNER and changes one thing:
the Dense retriever. Nothing here establishes that GLiNER reads entities *well*, that its advantage
over BM25 is stable beyond this single test run, or that either extractor's structure survives at
five million paragraphs — the last of those is the question Phase 9 exists to ask.
