## Problem and objective

**Problem.** The project's positive result rests on a small encoder. Phase 6 measured, at Full
Support @2,048 on the 1,400 test questions, `Dense 0.8250`, `Dense + BM25 0.8643`,
`Dense + Entity Hop 0.8900` — a **+6.50-point** gain over Dense. Phase 7 then showed the entity
representation can be built cheaply, with GLiNER keeping 83.5% of that gain (`0.8793`). Every one of
those numbers was produced with `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`: **33.4M parameters, 384 dimensions**, a
model whose published MTEB retrieval average is 51.68 across 15 tasks.

Two explanations of the Phase 6 gain remain open, and the roadmap names them (§5, *Interpretation A*
and *Interpretation B*):

- **A — structural relatedness is genuinely complementary.** Shared-entity structure encodes
  something semantic similarity does not express, and even a much stronger Dense retriever should
  still benefit from it.
- **B — Entity Hop is repairing a weak encoder.** Most of the +6.5 points is compensation for
  `bge-small`'s limitations, and a modern strong retriever would absorb it.

Nothing measured so far distinguishes them. Until it does, the project cannot claim a general
retrieval principle, and Phase 9 would be scaling a mechanism whose value is unestablished.

**Objective.** Answer one question:

> **Does Entity Hop still add useful complementary signal when the Dense retriever itself is
> substantially stronger than `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`?**

Success is a written answer with three numbers on the held-out split — `Strong Dense`,
`Strong Dense + BM25`, `Strong Dense + Entity Hop` — read beside the inherited BGE-small figures,
plus a statement of which of the three outcomes in §"Interpreting the result" was observed. **All
three outcomes, including "the effect disappears", are successful completions of this phase.** A
negative result here is a publishable finding, not a failure to be engineered away.

## What changes: the Dense retriever — APPROVED

The master plan is explicit: *"Choose one modern strong open Dense model… Do not create a model
leaderboard."* So the model is chosen **a priori**, from published evidence and practical
constraints, and **fixed in this spec and in `config.py` before any Phase 8 number exists** — the
same discipline as Phase 7 decision 10. Choosing it by comparing dev scores across several models
would be the leaderboard the plan forbids, and would also spend the dev split on a question the
phase is not asking.

### Measured facts about the corpus that constrain the choice

Computed from `data/token_counts.json` (19,366 units, `bge-small` tokenizer) while writing this spec:

```text
mean   124.42 tokens      p90   206
median 112               p99   355
max  1,850

paragraphs over 512 tokens =  36  (0.186%)
paragraphs over 384 tokens = 131  (0.676%)
```

The indexing unit is the whole paragraph and is never split. A model with a 512-token window
therefore **truncates 36 paragraphs**; a long-context model truncates none. The effect is small but
real, and it is a fact this spec declares rather than one the results quietly inherit. (Caveat: these
counts are under `bge-small`'s tokenizer; another model's tokenizer would give slightly different
numbers. The order of magnitude is what matters.)

### The approved model

**`Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B`**, approved by the author. Four reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It actually tests the question.** Phase 8 exists to distinguish Interpretation A from B, and
   that requires Dense to be *substantially* stronger. `bge-large-en-v1.5` is the same family, the
   same era and the same 512-token window, +2.61 MTEB retrieval points over `bge-small`. If Entity
   Hop's gain survived that, it would be weak evidence for A; if it shrank, we could not tell whether
   the encoder or the ceiling caused it. A generational jump gives the hypothesis a real chance to
   fail.
2. **No new dependency and no remote code.** The pinned `transformers 5.16.1` and
   `sentence-transformers 6.0.1` already satisfy it, and it needs no `trust_remote_code=True`.
   `gte-large-en-v1.5` does, which means the model load executes code fetched from the Hub — directly
   against `CLAUDE.md`'s "avoid executable serialization for untrusted data" and an `/8-auditar`
   trigger this phase would rather not create.
3. **It removes the truncation confound.** 32K context means zero paragraphs are cut, so no part of
   any difference can be attributed to Dense having read less of the corpus than the Entity Hop did.
4. **It keeps Phase 9 possible.** Phase 8 selects the Dense model Phase 9 carries to ~5M paragraphs.
   0.6B / 1024-dim is heavier than `bge-small` but stays a realistic FullWiki embedding job; a 4B or
   8B Qwen3 variant (2,560 / 4,096 dims) would make Phase 9 an expensive proposition for a gain that
   is not what Phase 8 is measuring. **This is also why the approved model is 0.6B rather than the
   largest available model** — "substantially stronger" is the requirement, "strongest possible" is
   not.

Two costs of the choice, stated plainly: it needs a GPU (the approved rented path, Decision 9), and
it is **asymmetric** — see Restriction R2 and Decision 3.

**How it is pinned.** Full **1024 dimensions, no MRL truncation**. The revision is not left to
implementation — it is an experimental decision of this spec, and it is:

```text
model     Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B
revision  97b0c614be4d77ee51c0cef4e5f07c00f9eb65b3
```

Resolved from the Hugging Face Hub API on 2026-09-19 (`lastModified 2026-04-20T02:45:58Z`) and
written here rather than into the plan, so that the model identity behind every Phase 8 number is
fixed by the approved spec. `config.py` carries this exact 40-character SHA, never a branch name;
the `resolved_revision` the Hub actually serves is recorded beside the embedding artifact and must
equal it.

Read from that revision and confirmed while writing this spec: `config.json` → `hidden_size 1024`
(the 1024-dim claim) and `max_position_embeddings 32768`; `model.safetensors` is the only weight
file. Weights are loaded from **`safetensors`** and their **SHA-256 is recorded** (R7).
**`trust_remote_code` is not used.** The effective `max_seq_length` is read from the loaded model and
recorded rather than copied from the model card (Decision 6).

## What is measured

Three systems, nothing else:

```text
Strong Dense
Strong Dense + BM25
Strong Dense + Entity Hop   (GLiNER index, inherited)
```

**Retrieval** — dev for fitting, test once for the final reading:

- Full Support at all four budgets, **headline at 2,048**;
- Gold Recall at all four budgets;
- Recall@2 / @5 / @10;
- the fitted fusion scheme and weight for each hybrid, and the dev curve over the grid;
- mean query latency per system, for comparison with Phase 6's 0.891 / 1.508 / 10.447 ms/query.

**The five roadmap questions**, answered from those numbers:

1. how much Strong Dense improves over the BGE-small baseline;
2. whether BM25 still adds complementary signal;
3. whether Entity Hop still adds complementary signal;
4. whether Entity Hop still compares favourably with the lexical complement;
5. how much of the original gain survives, expressed **in whole questions as well as points**
   (Phase 7's habit: `1,231 − 1,155 = 76 of 91` is easier to argue about than `+0.0543`).

**Entity Hop behaviour under the new Dense**, because P1 changes when Dense changes and that is the
mechanism:

- positive candidates per question `P(q)`: mean, median, share of questions with zero;
- `p1`s with no entity node;
- units the hop introduces into the fused context that Dense did not supply
  (`evaluation/entity_diagnostics.py` already records this; Phase 7 reused it in one call);
- how often `p1` under Strong Dense differs from `p1` under `bge-small` — the cheapest available
  diagnosis of *why* the hop's contribution moved.

**Cost and scale**, so Phase 9 inherits a measurement rather than a guess:

- corpus embedding wall clock and throughput (paragraphs/s) with the hardware named;
- question embedding time;
- embedding cache size on disk;
- monetary cost actually incurred: the real charge for the rented session (Decision 9), recorded
  as Phase 7 recorded its 0.03 USD;
- **a linear projection to 5,000,000 paragraphs, labelled as a projection everywhere it appears**,
  with its arithmetic shown, exactly as Phase 7 did (`method: "linear from measured throughput"`).

No figure may be estimated and reported as measured. A figure that does not exist is declared as a
gap.

## How dev decides, and what test reports

Deliberately simple. **Phase 8 is a comparison, not a preregistered non-inferiority experiment.**
Phase 6 needed a superiority test, a validity test, a non-inferiority margin and a frozen decision
state because it was making a *replacement* claim. Phase 8 asks whether an effect survives a changed
condition; three numbers beside three inherited numbers answer that. The Phase 6 apparatus is
explicitly **not** reproduced.

### The protocol

1. The **Dense model, its revision, its dimension and its query handling are fixed in `config.py`
   before the first Phase 8 number exists.** A different model or variant measured later is a
   separately declared candidate in a later phase, never a silent swap and never a retune after
   seeing a figure.
2. Corpus and question embeddings are computed under the new model and cached **under new cache
   keys**, one for the corpus and one for the questions (Restriction R3).
3. On the **600 dev questions**: fit the fusion weight for `Strong Dense + BM25` and for
   `Strong Dense + Entity Hop`, each with the inherited objective (Gold Recall @2,048),
   `FUSION_WEIGHT_GRID`, RRF measured on the same questions, Phase 3 tie-breaks. Read dev Full
   Support @2,048 for all three systems.
4. **One test pass**, all three systems, after the dev fits are written — **unless the stop rule
   below fired**, in which case the held-out split is not opened at all. Test figures are never read
   while anything is being fitted or chosen.
5. Report, interpret, write `8.results.md`.

### What "held out" means here, precisely

The 1,400 test questions are held out **with respect to every Phase 8 choice** — the fusion weights —
and that is the whole of the claim. They are **not** globally unseen: Phases 6 and 7 already measured
systems on exactly these questions, and those figures are inherited and quoted. This changes nothing
about the protocol: every Phase 8 choice is made on dev, and test is read once.

### A pre-declared stop rule

If `Strong Dense` alone does **not** beat the inherited `Dense` dev figure of
**`0.8117 = 487 / 600`**, the phase's premise has failed: the chosen model is not stronger on this
corpus. The rule is settled and leaves no discretion — **report the dev result and STOP, before the
held-out split is opened.** No other encoder is tried inside Phase 8: swapping models after seeing
dev is choosing a model on dev performance, the leaderboard the master plan forbids and the same
error Phase 7's record was written to prevent. Declaring a different model is work for a later phase
or an openly written deviation, never a silent retry inside this one. (Decision 4.)

## Interpreting the result

All three outcomes are informative and all three close the phase. The roadmap (§5) and master plan
already name them:

| Outcome | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Entity Hop remains strongly positive** | Interpretation A. Structural relatedness supplies retrieval information a much stronger semantic retriever still misses. The strongest possible support for the project's second research line. |
| **The effect is smaller but still positive** | Both mechanisms were present in Phase 6: Entity Hop partly compensated for a weak encoder *and* carried real complementary signal. Phase 9 remains worth running; the claim is narrower. |
| **The effect disappears** | Interpretation B. The Phase 6 gain was largely a property of the smaller encoder rather than a general retrieval principle. This is a genuine, bounded, publishable negative result — the same shape as the Phase 4 closure, and just as valuable. |

Two rules govern the reading, and neither is negotiable:

- **A weak result is never rescued by adding complexity.** No canonicalization, no relations, no
  extra hops, no extra seeds, no query-aware filtering, no reranker, no second extractor, no model
  swap. If the effect shrinks or disappears, that is the finding.
- **A comparison against BM25 under the new Dense is descriptive.** Phase 6's replacement claim was
  preregistered; Phase 8's BM25 column is context, reported with its question counts, not a new
  non-inferiority verdict.

### `Phase8Embedding` (one per phase)

`model`, `revision` (the pinned commit `97b0c614be4d77ee51c0cef4e5f07c00f9eb65b3`) and
`resolved_revision` (what the Hub actually served), `weights_file` (`model.safetensors`) and

**Why the weight digest is here.** Phase 7's security review raised exactly this gap as **SEC-031**:
a manifest that names a revision but not the bytes cannot prove which weights produced its numbers.
That finding is deferred for Phase 7 rather than fixed, because rewriting a measured, frozen artifact
to add provenance nobody recorded at execution time is not worth doing. Phase 8 is the prospective
side of that same decision: it records the digest **as it runs**, so no later phase has to choose
between an incomplete record and a retroactive edit. **No Phase 7 artifact is modified.**

**Two cache keys, deliberately.** The query prompt affects queries only, so it enters
`question_cache_key` and **not** the corpus key: a query-only parameter has no business invalidating
19,366 paragraph embeddings. Both keys are recorded, so a later reader can tell which encoding
produced which vectors.

**Invariants:** both keys differ from every existing key under `data/cache/`; no existing embedding
artifact is modified or deleted; `unit_set_hash == 101f564fdcca620c`; `resolved_revision` equals the
pinned `revision`.

### `Phase8Dev` (one per phase)

Per system: the fitted scheme and weight (hybrids only), the dev curve over the grid, metrics at all
four budgets, hop-behaviour figures, run file name and digest. Written **before** the test pass.

### `Phase8Test` (one per phase)

The single held-out pass: one `RunResult` per system from the unchanged harness, plus the inherited
BGE-small dev/test figures quoted beside them **with their source files named**, plus the surviving-
gain arithmetic in questions and in points.

**Integrity, Phase 7 shape, no more.** A digest chain linking: embedding artifact → GLiNER node index
(pinned digest) → dev fit → held-out run, so a test figure cannot be detached from the configuration
and the dev fit that authorized it. That is the smallest mechanism that answers "which model and
which index produced this number". No Phase 6 freeze/supersede protocol.

## Restrictions

**R1 — The context-budget ruler does not move. This is the largest integrity risk in the phase.**
`config.TOKENIZER_ID` is currently defined as `= EMBEDDING_MODEL`, and `TokenCounter` defaults to
`(TOKENIZER_ID, EMBEDDING_REVISION)`. `cer embed` writes a **single flat file**,
`data/token_counts.json`, and `_cheap_inputs` refuses to run without it. So changing
`EMBEDDING_MODEL` naively would (a) silently re-tokenize the whole corpus with a different tokenizer
and (b) **overwrite the one file that defines the fixed context budget behind every Phase 1–7 result**.
The budget is a shared ruler, not an absolute truth; if it moves, no Phase 8 number is comparable to
any inherited number, and the inherited numbers themselves stop being reproducible from the
repository.

**Settled:** the budget tokenizer stays **`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` at the revision Phases 1–7 used**,
whatever the Dense model becomes. **Changing Dense MUST NOT rewrite or regenerate
`data/token_counts.json`.** The implementation must **separate the embedding model from the budget
tokenizer semantically**: they are two different configuration items that have merely shared one
constant until now, and `TOKENIZER_ID` must stop being an alias of `EMBEDDING_MODEL`. (Decision 2.)

**R2 — Query handling is a declared configuration item, not a free parameter.** Neither
`embeddings/backend.py` nor `embeddings/cache.py` has any notion of an instruction prefix: documents
are encoded from `unit.indexable_text` and questions from `question.question`, identically.
Qwen3-Embedding is asymmetric, and measuring it without its query prompt would handicap it — biasing
the phase toward Interpretation A for the wrong reason.

**Settled — one source of truth, and it is this spec.** The **expected exact value** of the query
prompt is:

```text
Instruct: Given a web search query, retrieve relevant passages that answer the query\nQuery:
```

where `\n` is a single newline character and there is **no trailing space**. This is the value
`config.py` carries.

The implementation **reads `model.prompts["query"]` from the loaded model and asserts EXACT string
equality against that value. On any mismatch it aborts before producing a single embedding** — a
silently different prompt would change every query vector while leaving the artifact's provenance
looking correct. Encoding may go through `prompt_name="query"`; what is recorded in the artifact and
what enters `question_cache_key` is the **exact resolved string**, so the recorded provenance is
always what was actually used rather than what was expected.

**Documents remain unprefixed** (the pinned revision stores `"document": ""`). The instruction is
**not** customized or tuned for HotpotQA: a prompt fitted to this benchmark would be a
hyperparameter chosen on our own data, which this phase does not do. (Decision 3.)

**R3 — A different model produces a different cache, never an overwrite.** `cache_key` is
`sha1(model|revision|corpus_hash|normalized)`, so a new model already writes a new file
(`data/cache/embeddings-<key>.npz`) and the two existing BGE-small artifacts
(`embeddings-a28365b7ed90ed10`, `embeddings-bacd74e7f468f0d4`) are untouched by construction. The same
must hold for the question caches under `data/cache/questions/`. **Do not delete any cache**
(`CLAUDE.md`: ASK FIRST).

**Settled, on where the query prompt belongs:** it **must enter `question_cache_key`**, or two
different query encodings would collide under one key. It **must not** enter the corpus cache key —
documents are encoded without it, so letting a query-only parameter invalidate 19,366 paragraph
embeddings would buy nothing. Both keys are recorded in the embedding artifact. (Decision 5.)

**R4 — Phase 5/6/7 artifacts are never invalidated.** `data/extraction/`, `data/nodes/`,
`data/replacement/`, `data/phase7/`, `config.PINNED_*`, `EXPECTED_*` and the `extract` / `nodes` /
`navigate` / `replace-*` / `cheap-*` stages stay exactly as they are. Phase 8 **reads**
`data/phase7/gliner/` and **writes nothing into it**. The existing suite must still pass unchanged.

**R5 — Platform.** The local machine is Windows ARM64; the rented Linux x86_64 GPU path (RunPod, RTX
4090) is approved, proven and used by Phase 7, with `pyproject.toml` resolving `torch` from
`pytorch-cu126` there and `pytorch-cpu` here. A scientifically appropriate model is **not** rejected
because the laptop runs it slowly. The hardware actually used is recorded beside every timing.

**R6 — Dependencies.** The approved model needs none: the lock already holds
`transformers 5.16.1` and `sentence-transformers 6.0.1`. **If implementation discovers that it does
require a new or changed dependency, that is a separate explicit approval** under `CLAUDE.md` — not
granted by this spec.

**R7 — Model identity is external input.** The model is pinned to the **exact commit revision
`97b0c614be4d77ee51c0cef4e5f07c00f9eb65b3`, never a branch**, fixed in `config.py` before any
measurement, and the revision the Hub actually served (`resolved_revision`) is recorded and must
equal it — exactly as `EMBEDDING_REVISION` already does. Load **`safetensors`**.
**`trust_remote_code` is not used** — the approved model does not need it, and enabling it would be a
materially different security surface requiring its own approval (see §Security).

**Record `weights_sha256`.** The SHA-256 of `model.safetensors`, computed over the bytes actually
downloaded and loaded, goes into the embedding artifact beside the revision. A revision names a
commit; only the digest binds the artifact to the weights that produced it. This is the prospective
answer to Phase 7's **SEC-031**, applied here at the run that writes the artifact rather than
retrofitted afterwards.

**Record the effective `max_seq_length`.** The figures disagree, which is the point: the model card
advertises a 32K window, `config.json` at the pinned revision declares `max_position_embeddings
32768`, and `tokenizer_config.json` declares `model_max_length 131072`. The pinned revision ships
**no** `sentence_bert_config.json`, so `sentence-transformers` derives the limit at load time.
Nothing is taken on trust: the run reads what the loaded model actually resolves and records it,
because the "truncates nothing" argument depends on it. Every one of those figures is far above the
pool's longest paragraph (1,850 `bge-small` tokens), so the conclusion is not in doubt — only the
recorded number is. (Decision 6.)

**R8 — Discipline.** Dev/test separation is operational, not aspirational: the runner refuses test
questions in any fitting path, as `check_dev_only` already does. Explicit seed. Deterministic
tie-breaking. ASCII only in `print()`, logging and Python string literals. English everywhere.
Secrets stay in `.env` — this phase needs no API key at all.

## Anti-goals (what this phase does NOT do)

- **NO model leaderboard.** One Dense model, fixed before measuring. Not a sweep, not a comparison of
  encoders, not a second model added mid-phase because the first disappointed.
- **NO change of entity extractor.** GLiNER is inherited unchanged. That spaCy scored higher on
  Phase 7 dev does **not** reopen it: the choice was made by a rule frozen before any candidate ran,
  and overriding it after observing dev would destroy the meaning of the held-out figure. Re-opening
  the extractor belongs to the deferred scale-and-representation experiment.
- **NO canonicalization, alias resolution, relations, triples, attributes, PageRank or any other
  graph propagation.**
- **NO query-aware Entity Hop, NO multiple seeds, NO second hop, NO change to read depth.**
- **NO `Strong Dense + BM25 + Entity`** three-way hybrid.
- **NO reranker, cross-encoder or LLM anywhere in the retrieval loop.**
- **NO change** to the corpus, indexing unit, splits, normalization v1, node index shape or the
  context-budget ruler.
- **NO FullWiki corpus** — that is Phase 9.
- **NO chunking.** The paragraph stays whole even if the chosen model's window truncates a few.
- **NO Phase 6-style apparatus:** no freeze/supersede protocol, no deviation branches, no decision
  table, no non-inferiority margin, no preregistered state machine, no `8.tasks.md`.
- **NO new statistical test — no McNemar, no bootstrap, no interval.** Question counts, deltas and
  the per-question outcomes the harness already records answer this phase's question. A test that was
  not preregistered would dress a comparison up as a decision procedure.
- **NO re-measuring** the Phase 6/7 BGE-small runs.
- **NO tuning of anything against a test figure, ever.**
- **NO rescuing a weak result with added complexity.**

## Tests this phase actually needs

Result-changing code only. Five areas, not a suite:

- the new embedding configuration produces **distinct corpus and question cache keys** that resolve
  to no existing artifact, and the pinned revision is checked against the resolved one;
- the loaded GLiNER node index matches its pinned digest and the frozen pool's unit ids, and the
  runner refuses any other index;
- the runner refuses test questions during fitting, and refuses a node index built over another pool;
- **the query prompt is covered**: the equality check against the spec's expected value **aborts
  before any embedding** on a mismatch; documents are encoded **without** the prompt and queries
  **with** it; it enters `question_cache_key` and **not** the corpus cache key; and the string
  recorded in the artifact is the exact resolved one;
- **the budget ruler is pinned**: `TOKENIZER_ID` resolves to `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` at the
  Phase 1–7 revision independently of `EMBEDDING_MODEL`, so changing Dense cannot move the context
  budget or rewrite `data/token_counts.json`;
- the existing suite, `ruff check .` and `mypy src` still pass unchanged.

## Security

Phase 8 has **one** `/8-auditar` trigger, and it is a narrow one: a **new model download**. There is no
external untrusted input, no subprocess execution, no credentials, no network API beyond the Hub
fetch, and — under the approved model — no new dependency and no remote code execution.

The proportionate response is therefore a **short targeted review of model provenance only**: pinned
revision `97b0c614be4d77ee51c0cef4e5f07c00f9eb65b3`, `resolved_revision` recorded and equal to it,
`weights_sha256` of `model.safetensors` recorded over the bytes actually loaded, `safetensors`
loaded, `trust_remote_code` not used. Not a full audit.

Recording the weight digest closes, **prospectively and for Phase 8 only**, the gap Phase 7
catalogued as **SEC-031**. Phase 7's own artifacts are not touched: that finding stays deferred there
precisely so a measured, frozen artifact is not rewritten after the fact.

**The approved model does not require `trust_remote_code=True`, and the flag is not used.** That was
one of the reasons `Alibaba-NLP/gte-large-en-v1.5` was not chosen: it does require it, which would
mean code fetched from the Hub executing at model load. If anything in implementation appears to need
that flag, it is a second, materially different surface and must be reviewed and accepted explicitly
rather than absorbed silently.

## Declared gaps

Stated rather than filled. None of these is invented, estimated or worked around.

- **G1 — Published model scores are not comparable to one another.** `bge-small` 62.17 / retrieval
  51.68 and `bge-large` 64.23 / retrieval 54.29 come from MTEB's 56-task English set, and
  `gte-large-en-v1.5`'s 65.39 is the same-style 56-task average. Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B's figures come
  from the **MTEB v2-era tables**: multilingual Mean(Task) 64.33, Retrieval 64.64, Pair
  Classification 80.83; English v2 Mean(Task) 70.70, Retrieval 61.83. That is a different benchmark
  on a different scale, and **those numbers cannot be read against the older BGE/GTE averages** — the
  larger figures are not, by themselves, evidence that Qwen3 retrieves better on this corpus. They
  are used to rank plausibility before choosing, never as evidence. **The only number this project
  will trust is its own measured Full Support on the frozen pool.**
- **G2 — No measured embedding cost exists for any candidate on this corpus.** Phase 8 measures its
  own; nothing is projected from Phase 7's GLiNER throughput, which measured a different model doing
  a different job.
- **G3 — Truncation counts are tokenizer-dependent, and moot for the approved model.** The
  36-paragraph figure is under `bge-small`'s tokenizer and mattered only for candidates with a
  512-token window. Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B's window exceeds the pool's longest paragraph by a wide
  margin, so **no paragraph is truncated** and no part of any Phase 8 difference can be attributed to
  Dense having read less of the corpus than the Entity Hop did. The resolved `max_seq_length` is
  recorded anyway (Decision 6), so this is verified rather than assumed.
- **G4 — Phase 5's Claude extraction wall-clock time was never recorded** (inherited gap, Phase 7
  §Declared gaps). Irrelevant to Phase 8, carried forward so it is not lost.
- **G5 — NER quality is still unmeasured.** Phase 7 made it an explicit anti-goal and Phase 8 does not
  reopen it. Any Phase 8 result is conditional on whatever GLiNER actually read.
- **G6 — Phase 8 measures one Dense model on one corpus.** It cannot establish that the outcome
  generalizes to other strong encoders, to other multi-hop datasets, or to FullWiki scale.
