## Why it did not help: a dev diagnostic (exploratory)

Computed after the gate from the recorded dev lists, at the author's request, to explain the
result. Scripts and output: `data/phase12/closing-diagnostic/`, not versioned (sha256
`diagnostic.py` `ca06e632…86b5f8`, `diagnostic.txt` `e23683fe…ab94d`). Nothing was fitted or
selected on these figures. They describe the 4,310 gold paragraphs Dense leaves outside its top
10 on dev. A gold paragraph's *bridge entities* are the P1 entity nodes it also holds.

### 1. The funnel: restricting the seed set never lifts the first-5 count above the unrestricted hop's

In the depth-100 list / first 5 / median rank:

| config | in depth-100 list | in first 5 | median rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `s=1, x=no` | 1,032 | 379 | 11 |
| `s=1, x=yes` | 984 | 375 | 10 |
| `s=2, x=no` | 1,530 | 573 | 10 |
| `s=2, x=yes` | 1,412 | 512 | 10 |
| **`s=all, x=no`** | **1,665** | **590** | **11** |
| `s=all, x=yes` | 1,515 | 522 | 11 |

Every restricted configuration reaches and ranks fewer gold paragraphs than the unrestricted hop.
Restricting by `s` trades reach for nothing; excluding by `x` costs reach for the same reason the
dev fit shows: some of the entities it drops are the bridge.

### 2. The sentence choice is mostly right but too coarse

Of the 4,310 gold paragraphs, **2,651 share an entity with P1** (bridge-sharing). Restricted to
those:

- for **66.1 %** (1,751 of 2,651), the P1 sentence the relevance ranking puts first holds a
  bridge entity; it is ranked 2nd for 25.0 % (662), 3rd or lower for 6.7 % (178), and unlocated
  for 2.3 % (60);
- the ranking puts P1's own first sentence first in **85.9 %** (4,927 of 5,734) of the P1s with
  at least 2 candidate sentences — the similarity that made Dense pick P1 already sits there;
- the bridge is in P1's first sentence in **63.7 %** (1,650 of 2,591) of the located cases (21.0 %
  in the second, 8.8 % in the third, 6.5 % later).
- **Even a right choice does not help.** Of the 1,751 gold paragraphs whose bridge sentence ranks
  first, 950 are in both the `s=1` and the `s=all` lists; for those 950 the median rank goes
  9 → 10 and the first-5 count 368 → 372. The chosen sentence still holds a median of several
  seeds, so the bridge keeps competing with its co-located entities and their ties.
- **A wrong choice (about a third) drops the bridge outright.** Of the all-seed hop's 590 first-5
  gold paragraphs, 301 fall out of `s=1`'s first 5: 163 whose bridge was not in the top-ranked
  sentence, and 138 whose bridge *was* in it but lost score that the unrestricted hop shared
  through the other sentences.

### 3. The exclusion removes bridges

For **26.4 %** (699 of 2,651) of the bridge-sharing gold paragraphs, a bridge entity is named in
the question — comparison questions ("which is taller, X or Y") name both sides of the hop. This
is consistent with every `x = yes` configuration scoring below its `x = no` twin in the dev fit
above.

### 4. Oracle upper bound (uses gold; not a system)

A hop seeded only by each gold paragraph's own bridge entities — an oracle, since it reads the
answer to know which entity to follow — puts **2,055** of the 2,651 bridge-sharing gold
paragraphs in its depth-100 list and **1,131 in its first 5** (median rank 4), against 1,665 /
590 for the all-seed hop. A perfect entity choice would nearly double what the hop places in its
first 5.

### Reading

An interpretation, not a measurement: Phase 11's hypothesis survives in a sharper form.

- Which entity is followed matters: that is what the oracle shows.
- Question-to-sentence similarity cannot single that entity out on its own. The right sentence
  usually holds several entities, and the sentence most similar to the question is usually P1's
  lead sentence, where the similarity that made Dense pick P1 already lies — it does not
  additionally isolate the bridge among what that sentence names.
- Picking the entity needs its role relative to what the question asks, which is what a typed
  relation (roadmap 8.2) would supply. An entity-level signal without relations — for example a
  score over a window around the mention, rejected in spec decision D1 for adding a width
  parameter to fit — remains untested.
- This is a reading of dev diagnostics. It claims nothing about relations or any GraphRAG system
  as measured.

## Limitations

1. **The diagnostic is post hoc.** It was designed after the gate had fired, on the same dev
   questions the fit used. It describes the mechanism; it does not test an alternative.
2. **Dev has now been fitted by Phases 10, 11 and 12.** The 7,405 validation questions remain
   valid for selection, not as held-out evidence.
3. **The sentence vectors were encoded on the laptop CPU**, while the dev question vectors came
   from Phase 9's rented-GPU run. The numeric difference between the two runs was not measured
   and is expected to be negligible; Phase 11 recorded the analogous CPU/GPU gap for its own
   question encodings without finding it material.
4. **2.7 % of P1 entity nodes are unlocated** — present in the corpus index but not found bounded
   by non-word characters in any sentence of their own P1, most likely because they occur only in
   the title.
5. **Latency was not measured for P12**, because no held-out pass ran; the sentence-encoding cost
   the spec's D8 asked for has no figure.
6. **The oracle (diagnostic section 4) uses gold** to choose the seed entity and is an upper
   bound on what any purely structural, query-time seed choice could reach; it is not a system
   and was not evaluated as one.

## Next research decision

Phase 12 closes with `DEV_STOP`. P10-C (Dense + BM25 + the query-blind Entity Hop) stays the
line's best system, and `test-11` stays reserved and unopened.

The diagnostic sharpens, rather than reopens, the question Phase 11 left. Choosing the bridge
entity by question-to-sentence similarity recovers it in the majority of cases where a sentence
ranking is even meaningful (66.1 % first-choice accuracy among bridge-sharing gold, section 2
above) — yet Full Support does not move, because the chosen sentence still names several
entities that tie under the unchanged rarity score, and because a third of the time the wrong
sentence is chosen and the bridge is dropped outright. The oracle shows the ceiling this line of
attack is chasing: seeding only the true bridge would put nearly twice as much gold in the hop's
first 5. Reaching that ceiling needs the entity's *role* relative to the question, which a
sentence-similarity score does not carry. Two directions address it, in the order the roadmap
already set:

- **relations (roadmap 8.2):** a typed edge such as `Titanic —directed by→ James Cameron` would
  single out the bridge among P1's entities directly, rather than ranking a sentence and hoping
  one entity dominates it;
- **entity linking or canonicalization (roadmap 8.1):** a separate, cheaper concern — merging
  raw forms of the same entity — that does not by itself supply a role and was not tested here.

Which direction comes next is the author's decision.
