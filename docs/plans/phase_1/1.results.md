**These are not directly comparable to published HotpotQA figures**, and saying otherwise
would be dishonest. The standard *distractor* setting ranks 10 candidates per question;
*fullwiki* searches 5.2M paragraphs. This setup sits in between: a unified pool of 19,366
paragraphs. Harder than distractor, far easier than fullwiki. The numbers are internally
comparable, which is what they exist for.

## Reading of the results

**Dense beats BM25 everywhere**, by 6 to 16 points of Full Support depending on the budget.
The gap is widest at the tightest budget (512 tokens: 0.654 vs 0.492), which makes sense —
with room for only four or five paragraphs, ranking quality is everything. BM25 closes much
of the gap at 4096 tokens, where nearly everything fits anyway.

Precision falls as the budget grows, and that is arithmetic rather than degradation: only
2 of the ~33 units included at 4096 tokens can be gold, so precision is capped at about 0.06.

**BM25 is 75x faster** (0.4 ms vs 30.7 ms per query). Almost all of the dense cost is
embedding the query itself on this CPU, not the similarity search.

## The finding that matters for Phase 4

Failure analysis on the dev split at 2048 tokens:

| Outcome | Questions | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Both systems succeed | 393 | 65.5% |
| Only dense succeeds | 94 | 15.7% |
| Only BM25 succeeds | 41 | 6.8% |
| **Both fail** | **72** | **12.0%** |

Two things follow.

**The systems are complementary.** BM25 rescues 6.8% of questions that dense loses, so a
hybrid ceiling sits near 88% against dense's 81.2%. That is the margin Phase 3 is aiming at.

**The 12% that both fail look exactly like the case this project was built for.** Three of
them, verbatim:

| Question | Found | Missing (dense rank) |
|---|---|---|
| "What show does the host of The 2011 Teen Choice Awards ceremony currently star on?" | 2011 Teen Choice Awards | **Kaley Cuoco** (35) |
| "Mariana Kovacevic used horse placenta treatment to heal the captain of what team?" | Mariana Kovacevic | **Asamoah Gyan** (not in top-100) |
| "What year was the writer of the blog Preposterous Universe born?" | Cosmic Variance (blog) | **Sean M. Carroll** (20) |

The pattern is identical in all three: **the system finds the entity the question names, and
misses the bridge entity the question does not name.** The question never says "Kaley Cuoco";
it says "the host". A one-shot retriever has no way to get there, because the query contains
no signal for the second hop.

But the first retrieved paragraph does. It names the host. That is precisely the mechanism
Phase 4 proposes: let the concepts found in the retrieved chunks drive a second round of
retrieval. This dataset gives us 72 dev questions where the baseline provably cannot reach
the answer and the information needed to reach it is sitting in the chunk it already
retrieved.

**A caveat worth keeping.** This is a hypothesis-friendly reading of a small sample, not
evidence. Some of the 12% will fail for reasons expansion cannot fix — the missing paragraph
being genuinely unreachable, or the question being ambiguous. The honest statement is that
the failure mode has the right shape, and Phase 4 will find out whether the mechanism
actually closes it.

## Cost of the run

| Stage | Time |
|---|---|
| `fetch` (7,405 questions, paged) | ~4 min |
| `build` (subset, split, pool) | ~9 s |
| `embed` (19,366 units + tokenization) | **36 min 10 s** |
| `evaluate` (2 systems x 2 splits) | 1 min 9 s |

The embedding stage is the bottleneck, and it is slower than expected: BGE-small over 19k
short paragraphs would take 3-5 minutes on an x86 CPU with MKL. The `win_arm64` build of
PyTorch appears to lack the optimized kernels it has elsewhere. It is a one-off cost —
results are cached and reused — but if Phase 5 requires reindexing with several models, an
ONNX Runtime CPU backend is the fix worth trying first: typically 2-4x faster on small
encoders, in fp32, so the vectors stay comparable. The NPU is not the answer, since it would
require int8 quantization and that changes the vectors.
