# Spec Phase 16: the frozen systems on MultiHop-RAG, a corpus that is not Wikipedia

> Functional spec: WHAT is measured and WHY. The HOW goes in the plan (`16.0_*.md`).
> Status: **draft, pending the author's approval.** Settled by the author on 2026-09-30, before
> any Phase 16 measurement, and recorded in `16.pre_spec_notes.md`: the corpus is MultiHop-RAG;
> the unit is the article's own paragraph, boilerplate included; a fact that straddles a break
> maps to the paragraph holding its first sentence; the label is decided by P14 against P10-B;
> the splits by gold count and by query type are reported and decide nothing; the hop's gains are
> tallied by same article or another article; the corpus size is a declared limitation; nothing
> is fitted and no LLM is used anywhere. A change to any rule below is a written deviation
> `16.Y_*.md`.

---

## Problem and objective

**Problem.** Every result of this line, including Phase 15's transfer to MuSiQue, was measured on
Wikipedia text: short, self-contained paragraphs whose first paragraph names the article's
entity. The aim of the line is a set of strategies that serve any private corpus, and a private
corpus is rarely Wikipedia. Phase 15 changed how the questions were built; nothing has yet changed
the kind of text.

MultiHop-RAG (Tang & Yang 2024) is a corpus of 609 English news articles published after the
LLMs' cut-offs, with 2,556 queries whose evidence is marked as sentences copied from the
articles. Its text is long-form journalism with its own paragraph breaks, quotes, datelines and
boilerplate. Every evidence sentence is found verbatim in its article (measured 2026-09-30), so
gold paragraphs are locatable and Full Support is well defined.

**Question.**

> With every weight and parameter frozen at its HotpotQA value, do the Entity Hop systems
> (P10-C and P14) still retrieve the full multi-hop evidence more often than Dense + BM25 on a
> corpus of news articles cut into the authors' own paragraphs?

**Objective.** One pass, nothing fitted. The four frozen systems are measured once on the
2,255 answerable MultiHop-RAG queries, over a corpus of every paragraph of the 609 articles.

This phase adds no retriever and changes none. It changes the kind of text, and only that.

## Decisions

### D1 — The corpus

- **Source.** Hugging Face `yixuantt/MultiHopRAG`, revision
  `71ac0d0bd1f951d2d6b70311f7d2ae404e1ffa82` (last modified 2024-01-30, licence ODC-BY), files
  `corpus.json` (6,785,567 bytes, SHA-256 `20b61b5b…4d28f`) and `MultiHopRAG.json`
  (5,171,312 bytes, SHA-256 `03cfb492…bff`), measured 2026-09-30. The plan pins the download by
  revision, bytes and SHA-256 and checks the counts below. The GitHub repository's `dataset/`
  folder no longer serves the files (404 on 2026-09-30) and is not a source.
- **Articles.** 609, all titles and bodies distinct; fields `title, author, body, url, source,
  category, published_at` (measured). The paper kept only articles of at least 1,024 tokens.
- **Indexing unit.** The body of every article is split at its newline breaks; every
  non-empty piece is a unit, with the article's title as its title. Measured: 28,711 newline
  paragraphs, median 28 words, p90 80, maximum 1,285; 29 exceed 384 words (GLiNER's window;
  handled by the Phase 9 windowing rule, counted and declared, as in Phase 15). No fixed-size
  window and no segmenter: the phase tests another kind of text, not another way of cutting
  text (author decision 3, pre-spec notes).
- **Boilerplate is kept (author decision 1).** 4,538 of the newline paragraphs have five words
  or fewer (`Advertisement` two hundred times, section labels, dates, photo credits). They are
  indexed as they come, counted and declared in the corpus manifest; no minimum length is
  applied. No gold paragraph is that short (minimum 12 words, measured).
- **Unit identity.** The unit id is the Phase 1 content id over title and text, so a piece
  that repeats verbatim inside the same article (`Advertisement` under the same title) collapses
  to one unit. The corpus manifest declares both counts: newline paragraphs read and distinct
  units written. The declared corpus size is the number of distinct units.
- **Article metadata.** Each unit keeps its article's index in `corpus.json`, `url`, `source`,
  `category` and `published_at`, so that D8 can tell P1's article from any other. None of it is
  indexed: `indexable_text` stays title plus paragraph.
- **Size.** Under 30,000 units against MuSiQue's 101,962 and FullWiki's 5,233,329. This phase
  tests another kind of text, not scale (limitation 1).

### D2 — The questions and the gold

- **Queries.** 2,556 in `MultiHopRAG.json`, fields `query, answer, question_type,
  evidence_list`. The 301 `null` queries have no evidence and the answer "insufficient
  information": they are not measurable by Full Support and are excluded from the measurement,
  as MuSiQue's unanswerable questions were. They serve one purpose only: the live-path check
  (D4).
- **Answerable queries.** 2,255: comparison 856, inference 816, temporal 583 (measured). Every
  query with a non-empty `evidence_list` is answerable; the plan checks these counts.
- **Gold.** Each evidence piece carries a `fact`, a sentence copied verbatim from the article
  it names. Gold is the set of corpus units that contain the facts:
  - a fact that lies inside one newline paragraph maps to that unit (6,055 of 6,084 facts,
    measured);
  - a fact that straddles a paragraph break maps to the unit holding its first sentence
    (29 facts, measured; author decision 2). The rule is fixed here and applied by code; the
    plan records which facts it touched;
  - two facts inside the same unit count once. Measured after deduplication: 1,079 queries
    with 2 gold units, 780 with 3, 396 with 4; 962 distinct gold units in all.
- Any fact whose article or text is not found stays in the denominator as a miss (Phase 9's
  sentinel id); any unmapped gold at all is reported, and more than 1 % of the facts stops the
  phase (`DATA_STOP`). The measurement of 2026-09-30 found none.
- **Full Support** means every gold unit of the query is inside the 2,048-token context: 2, 3
  or 4 of them.
- The `answer` field is never read for retrieval, fitting or diagnosis.

### D3 — Nothing is fitted

No weight, `α`, depth, threshold, label set or prompt is chosen on MultiHop-RAG. The four
systems run with the values in the table above. There is no dev set in this phase and no grid.
No LLM is used anywhere: not to segment, not to extract, not to retrieve, not to judge.

### D4 — Integrity before the pass

- **Code identity.** On HotpotQA dev, from the recorded dev lists, the code used in this phase
  reproduces P10-C at 4,801 / 7,405 and P14 at 5,224 / 7,405 exactly, before it touches
  MultiHop-RAG. A miss stops the phase.
- **Extractor identity.** The GLiNER configuration digest on the pod equals the Phase 9 pod
  digest (Phase 15 measured them equal). A different digest stops the phase and goes to the
  author.
- **Live path.** The pass runs the live retrievers; before it, they run on the 301 `null`
  queries, only to check that they complete and that the run and ranking records are well
  formed. These queries have no gold, so no metric of them exists to compute, print or store.
  MultiHop-RAG has no train split; the `null` queries are the only questions outside the
  measurement.

### D5 — Primary endpoint and label

Full Support @2,048 on the 2,255 answerable queries, **P14 against P10-B**: one two-sided exact
McNemar test (binomial on the discordant queries, p = 1/2), α = 0.05.

```text
TRANSFER_SUPPORTED      wins > losses and p < 0.05
TRANSFER_REGRESSION     losses > wins and p < 0.05
TRANSFER_NOT_SUPPORTED  otherwise
DATA_STOP               D2 or D4
```

P10-B is the primary control, as in Phase 15: the question is whether the structural signal is
worth having at all on text that is not Wikipedia. The label is written by code. The pass runs
once, after the author's explicit authorization, and refuses a second start. The result is
reported as it is, whatever the label (author decision 2 in the pre-spec notes).

### D6 — Secondary comparisons, preregistered

Same metric and test. None can change the label.

- **P14 against P10-C:** does ordering the hop's candidates by the question still help?
- **P10-C against P10-B:** does the query-blind hop still help?
- **P10-B against P10-A:** the reference gain of BM25, for scale.

Every result states both comparisons of the line: against P10-C and against P10-B.

**The progression, side by side.** The ladder Dense → Dense + BM25 → P10-C → P14 as Full
Support @2,048 and as the gain of each step over the one before, on MultiHop-RAG beside the same
four systems on HotpotQA `test-11` (Phase 14, measured: 57.04 %, 61.58 %, 65.70 %, 70.60 %) and
on MuSiQue validation (Phase 15, measured: Dense 2,417-question figures in `15.results.md`;
P10-B 524, P10-C 669, P14 761). The three columns differ in corpus, questions and size, so they
compare the direction and order of the gains, not their size.

### D7 — By number of gold units and by query type, reported, deciding nothing

Every system's Full Support and gold recall @2,048, and the four paired comparisons of D5 and
D6, split two ways:

- by queries with 2, 3 and 4 gold units (1,079 / 780 / 396);
- by query type: comparison (856), inference (816), temporal (583).

Hypotheses, not measurements: the hop makes one step from one seed, so its gain is expected to
shrink as the gold count grows, as it did on MuSiQue; comparison queries ask two sources about
the same entity, so the shared-entity hop is expected to be strongest there. Neither hypothesis
changes the label.

### D8 — What the hop reaches, and from which article, reported, deciding nothing

From the rankings the pass stores, as in Phase 15: for each system, how many gold units outside
Dense's first 10 each component lists; whether P1 is a gold unit; the hop's candidate-set sizes;
and the split of won and lost queries by the Dense rank of each changed unit (1-10, 11-100,
beyond 100).

**New, the same-article tally (author decision 4).** Every unit of an article carries the
article's title in `indexable_text`, so all its paragraphs share the title's entities and the hop
from P1 can reach the rest of P1's article at little cost. For P10-C and P14 against P10-B, each
gold unit that enters the 2,048-token context through the hop is tallied as coming from P1's own
article or from another article; the tally is given for won queries and for all queries, over
all and by query type. It says whether the hop's gain on this corpus is cross-document evidence
or the rest of the same article. Also reported, for context: the fraction of queries whose gold
units all lie in one article (measured before the pass: 168 queries have two facts from the
same article) and the fraction where P1's article holds at least one gold unit.

### D9 — Hits@4 and Hits@10 over units, reported, not comparable

MultiHop-RAG's published figures are Hits@k and MRR@10 over 256-token chunks, with the best
reported pipeline at Hits@4 0.6625 and Hits@10 0.7467 (paper, Table 5). For each system, the
report gives Hits@4 and Hits@10 over the fused ranking, where Hits@k is the fraction of gold
units, over all queries, that appear in the first k units. It is computed over whole
paragraphs, a different unit and a different corpus cut, so the figures are stated beside the
paper's with the differences named, never as a comparison of systems. They decide nothing.

### D10 — Weights refitted on MultiHop-RAG, an optimistic ceiling

After the pass and the label, the stored component rankings are re-fused over Phase 14's
330-point grid (5 `α` × 66 weight triples) and the best point is reported beside the frozen
one. Fitted and measured on the same queries, it is an **upper bound, exploratory**: how much is
lost by not being able to fit, never a system's result.

### D11 — Latency, reported, not gated

`retrieve` wall time per query on the laptop, as in Phases 10-15, with the order in which the
systems ran and the working set before each. The corpus is under 30,000 units, so the figures do
not compare with earlier phases.

## Anti-objectives

Phase 16 does **not**:

- add or change a retriever, a component, a weight or `α`;
- add hops or seeds for the 3- and 4-gold queries;
- segment the articles by any rule other than their own newline breaks: no fixed windows, no
  sentence-embedding boundaries, no learned or LLM chunker;
- drop, merge or rewrite any unit: no minimum length, no boilerplate filter;
- use the `answer` field, the `null` queries as a measurement, or the paper's chunk-level
  figures as a baseline;
- change the encoder, the extractor, its labels or the indexing unit rule;
- run the query reformulation (Phase 17) or another corpus (QASPER, LegalBench-RAG);
- report the refitted ceiling (D10) or Hits@k (D9) as a system's result or as a comparison
  with the paper;
- compare its absolute figures with published MultiHop-RAG figures without stating the
  differences in unit, corpus cut and metric.

## Limitations, declared in advance

1. **A tiny corpus (author decision 5).** Under 30,000 units from 609 articles. Dense alone
   reaches further than at scale, candidate sets are small, and `N` and `df` come from a corpus
   where one entity recurs across the paragraphs of a single article. A gain or its absence
   here says nothing about scale.
2. **The title is in every unit.** Under the Phase 9 rule, each paragraph is indexed as title
   plus text, so all paragraphs of an article share the title's entities and terms. This helps
   every system reach the rest of P1's article and is why D8 tallies same-article gains. The
   rule is kept because changing it would confound the kind of text with the indexing rule.
3. **Boilerplate units are in the index.** 4,538 units of five words or fewer are noise for BM25
   and the entity index; they carry the article title, so they are not empty to either. Their
   effect is not measured separately in this phase.
4. **The questions were written by an LLM.** MultiHop-RAG's queries paraphrase GPT-4 claims
   built from extracted facts; their wording may sit closer to the evidence than a user's
   question would.
5. **One hop against 2-4 gold units.** The systems were designed for two-paragraph evidence.
   D7 reports the split; a loss on 3- and 4-gold queries is a limit of the design.
6. **A frozen transfer is a lower bound on what fitting could give**, and D10's ceiling an
   upper bound. The honest figure for a corpus without labels is the frozen one.
7. **English news of one quarter of 2023, six categories.** One kind of non-Wikipedia text, not
   generality; QASPER and LegalBench-RAG are the next corpora in the roadmap.
