## 1. Result

**Terminal state: `TRANSFER_REGRESSION`.**

Source: `data/phase16/outcome.json`, written mechanically by `cer p16-outcome` from the four run
files; `stop_reasons` is empty.

Primary endpoint (D5): Full Support @2,048 tokens on the 2,255 answerable MultiHop-RAG queries,
**P14 against P10-B**, every weight and `α` frozen at its HotpotQA value.

| System | Full Support @2,048 | Share | Label |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| P10-B Dense + BM25 (0.5 / 0.5, frozen) | **587 / 2,255** | **26.03 %** | measured |
| P14 Dense + BM25 + relevance-ordered hop (`α = 0.75`, 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2, frozen) | 513 / 2,255 | 22.75 % | measured |
| Difference | −74 | **−3.28 pp** | derived (−3.28 recorded) |

```text
exact two-sided McNemar: wins 31, losses 105, ties 2,119   p = 1.3e-10   (alpha = 0.05)
```

Both comparisons of the line, at the primary budget (measured, `outcome.json`):

- **against P10-B** (the community reference): P14 −3.28 pp (31 wins / 105 losses,
  p = 1.3e-10); P10-C −2.88 pp (25 / 90, p = 8.4e-10);
- **against P10-C** (the project's bar before P14): P14 −0.40 pp (24 wins / 33 losses,
  p = 0.29, not significant).

The answer to the phase question: **no.** With nothing refitted, both Entity Hop systems retrieve
the full multi-hop evidence **less** often than Dense + BM25 on MultiHop-RAG's news articles, and
the loss is significant. The relevance ordering neither helps nor hurts against the query-blind
hop here. The ladder Dense → Dense + BM25 → P10-C → P14 rises only at its first step (BM25:
+11.04 pp, the largest BM25 step the line has measured) and falls at the two hop steps, where it
rose on HotpotQA `test-11` and on MuSiQue.

**This is a negative result of the line, reported as it is (author decision 2).** It bounds the
claim of Phases 9-15: the frozen HotpotQA configuration of the Entity Hop is not a general-purpose
complement to Dense + BM25; on one non-Wikipedia corpus of news paragraphs it costs questions.

### Limitations

Declared in the spec in advance (1-7), then found in the run (8-12).

1. **A tiny corpus.** 27,989 units from 609 articles, 187 times fewer than FullWiki (derived:
   5,233,329 / 27,989). `N` and `df` come from a corpus where one entity recurs across the
   paragraphs of an article and a few hubs (`nfl` in 7.18 % of units) run through many articles.
   A loss here says nothing about scale.
2. **The title is in every unit.** All paragraphs of an article share the title's entities and
   terms. The same-article tally shows the hop's gains are mostly from other articles; the effect
   of the rule on what the hop *lists* is not measured.
3. **Boilerplate units are in the index.** 4,538 paragraphs of five words or fewer carry the
   article title; their effect is not measured separately.
4. **The questions were written by an LLM.** MultiHop-RAG's queries paraphrase GPT-4 claims built
   from extracted facts; their wording may sit closer to the evidence than a user's question
   would. BM25's unusually large step (+11.04 pp) is consistent with that, and does not prove it
   (interpretation).
5. **One hop against 2-4 gold units.** D7 puts most of the net loss (55 of 74 questions) in the
   2-gold queries; the design's limit on longer chains is not what explains this result.
6. **A frozen transfer is a lower bound on what fitting could give, and D10's ceiling an upper
   bound.** Here the ceiling itself sets the hop's weight to 0.
7. **English news of one quarter of 2023, six categories.** One kind of non-Wikipedia text, not
   generality; QASPER and LegalBench-RAG are the next corpora in the roadmap.
8. **The seed is rarely a gold unit.** P1 is gold in 17.07 % of queries (57.14 % on MuSiQue,
   77.4 % on HotpotQA dev). Every hop of this line starts from P1; this corpus violates the
   premise more than any measured before (section 12).
9. **Deviation 16.1.** 29 queries cite 6 sentences printed twice; the other paragraph was in a

10. **The HotpotQA and MuSiQue columns are not like-for-like controls.** Different corpora,

11. **Absolute figures are not compared with the literature.** D9 names three differences from

## 12. Interpretation: why the hop loses here

**Everything in this section is interpretation**: hypotheses read from the measurements above,
each with the evidence for it and what would test it. None is established; none was fitted or
selected on; the diagnostics that would separate them were not run.

**H1. The seed is usually the wrong paragraph of the right article.** The hop assumes P1 is a gold
paragraph whose entities point to the next one. On MultiHop-RAG, P1 is gold in 17.07 % of
queries, yet P1's article holds a gold unit in 62.44 %. Evidence: section 8 (P1 table); Dense
alone is the weakest first rung of the three columns (14.99 %) and nearly blind on inference
queries (3.43 %). Reading: with news cut into short paragraphs that all carry the article's title,
Dense reaches the right article but ranks a non-evidence paragraph first, and a hop from that
paragraph's entities follows the article's general cast rather than the fact's bridge. Test not
run: the hop's Full Support split by whether P1 is gold.

**H2. On this corpus the frozen weights take weight from the strongest signal.** BM25
weighs more here than on either other benchmark of the line: the BM25 step is +11.04 pp (276 wins / 27 losses);
BM25 alone (632, exploratory) beats Dense + BM25 (587); the best refitted point is Dense 0.3 /
BM25 0.7 / hop 0. The frozen P10-C and P14 weights cut BM25 from 0.5 to 0.3. At least 59 of the
114 gold units P14 lost against P10-B were in P10-B's context through BM25 alone, and the fused hop
lists hold fewer gold units beyond Dense's top 10 than P10-B's. In the refit curve, the hop at 0.2
taken from Dense beats P10-B (613-617) while the hop at 0.2 taken from BM25 loses (513-522).
Reading: much of the regression is the weight the HotpotQA fit moved from BM25 to the hop, on a
corpus where lexical overlap carries the evidence (limitation 4). This is read from an in-sample
refit and does not show that some fixed weight would transfer; the frozen figure is the result.

**H3. Entity sharing is weak evidence in a small, hub-dense news corpus.** The candidate set is a
larger share of the corpus (median 1.08 % against 0.23 % on MuSiQue and 0.11 % on HotpotQA dev),
fewer entity nodes are singletons (65.32 % against 79.75 % on MuSiQue), and a few hubs (`nfl`,
`amazon`, `apple`, `sunday night football`, `black friday`, `week 14`) run through hundreds of
paragraphs. Reading: sharing an entity with P1 separates the gold paragraph from the rest less
well than on Wikipedia, where a paragraph is *about* its title entity. Test not run: the rank of
gold units in the hop list against their shared-entity DF.

**What the measurements argue against.**

- *"The hop only fetches the rest of P1's article."* The same-article tally says the hop's few
  gains are about two thirds cross-article (P14, won: 21 of 29 from another article). The title
  rule may still crowd the hop's list with P1's article (H1, limitation 2), but that is not
  measured.
- *"The hop fails because the queries need 3-4 paragraphs."* 74.32 % of the net loss (55 of 74) is on 2-gold
  queries, the group where MuSiQue's gain lived.
- *"Comparison queries suit a shared-entity hop."* They carry the largest loss (D7).
- *"The relevance ordering is what breaks."* P14 and P10-C do not differ significantly
  (24 / 33, p = 0.29); the query-blind hop loses on its own (25 / 90).

**What is not shown.**

- Nothing was fitted: this is one frozen pass. It does not show that the hop cannot help on news
  with weights or a seed chosen for news; D10 is in-sample and its best point drops the hop.
- One corpus, one kind of text, one cut (newline paragraphs with the title prefixed), under
  30,000 units. It does not show that the hop fails on every non-Wikipedia corpus, nor at scale.
- Which of H1-H3 carries the loss, and how much each does, is not measured.
- Whether a stronger Dense, a seed other than P1, or no title prefix would change the result is not
  measured; each would be a new, specified experiment.

**What this bounds.** Phases 9-15 established the Entity Hop's gain on two Wikipedia benchmarks
whose paragraphs are *about* one entity and whose first Dense paragraph is usually evidence. This
phase shows that the frozen HotpotQA configuration does not carry to news paragraphs, where both
conditions fail, and that there Dense + BM25 is the better system. **P14 remains the best system
of the line on Wikipedia benchmarks; it is not a default for an arbitrary private corpus.**
