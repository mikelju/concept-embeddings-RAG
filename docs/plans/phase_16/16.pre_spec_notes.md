# Phase 16 — notes before the spec: MultiHop-RAG as the non-Wikipedia corpus

> Prepared 2026-09-30 for `/4-especificar`, at the author's request, after Phase 15 closed
> `TRANSFER_SUPPORTED` (PR #22, closing PR #23). Nothing here is a spec rule; it records what the
> data and the literature say so the spec can decide with the facts in view. Labels: **measured**
> (read from the files on 2026-09-30), **paper** (Tang & Yang 2024, arXiv 2401.15391, read through
> ar5iv), **interpretation**.

## Author decisions already taken (2026-09-30)

1. The corpus is MultiHop-RAG; the pre-spec work checks that every question has locatable gold.
2. Results are reported as they are, by number of supporting paragraphs, as in Phase 15: where the
   line works (2 paragraphs) and where it does not (3-4), so that the conditions under which the
   strategy helps are characterized, not flattered.
3. The indexing unit is a real problem for a non-Wikipedia corpus. No LLM is used to segment. The
   literature on segmentation without an LLM is surveyed (below), but the decision for this phase
   should not confound "another kind of text" with "another way of cutting text".

### Corpus

- 609 news articles, titles and bodies all distinct; fields `title, author, body, url, source,
  category, published_at`. Dates 2023-09-26 to 2023-12-25 (chosen by the paper to sit after the
  LLM cut-offs). Categories: sports 211, technology 172, entertainment 114, business 81, science
  21, health 10. Top sources: Sporting News 101, TechCrunch 97, The Verge 45, Polygon 44.
- 1,063,319 words in all; median 1,298 words per article, maximum 12,387. The paper kept only
  articles of at least 1,024 tokens (paper, §3.1).
- **Every body keeps its paragraph breaks**: newlines per body min 6, median 66, max 962, none
  without. Splitting on newlines gives **28,711 paragraphs**: median 28 words, p90 80, maximum
  1,285; 29 paragraphs exceed 384 words (GLiNER's window).
- **4,538 of those paragraphs have 5 words or fewer**: boilerplate such as `Advertisement` (200
  times), `Why we like it`, `(All times Eastern)`, `Photograph: Amazon`, dates, section labels.
  No gold paragraph is that short (minimum gold paragraph: 12 words).

### Questions

- 2,556 queries; fields `query, answer, question_type, evidence_list`. Types: comparison 856,
  inference 816, temporal 583, **null 301**. Null queries have no evidence and the answer
  "insufficient information": they are not measurable by Full Support and would be excluded, as
  MuSiQue's unanswerable questions were.
- **2,255 answerable queries**, evidence pieces per query: 2 → 1,079; 3 → 778; 4 → 398. By
  type: comparison 2 / 3 only (658 / 198); temporal 2 / 3 (318 / 265); inference 2 / 3 / 4
  (103 / 315 / 398).
- Each evidence piece is a `fact`: a sentence copied from the article, with the article's title
  and metadata. **All 6,084 facts are found verbatim in the body of the article they name**;
  6,055 lie inside exactly one newline-delimited paragraph and 29 cross a paragraph break (a
  sentence that continues after a newline). Mapping each fact to the paragraph that contains it
  (for the 29, the paragraph holding its first sentence, to be fixed by the spec) gives:
  - gold paragraphs per answerable query, after deduplication: 2 → 1,079; 3 → 780; 4 → 396
    (two queries have two facts in one paragraph);
  - **962 distinct gold paragraphs**, median 56.5 words, p90 117, maximum 966;
  - gold spread over distinct articles per query: 2 → 1,169; 3 → 774; 4 → 312 (168 queries have
    two evidence pieces from the same article).
- The paper's own evidence was produced by "a trained language model" that extracts factual
  sentences, then paraphrased by GPT-4 into claims that were assembled into queries (paper, §3.1);
  the `fact` field is the original sentence, which is why the verbatim match holds.

### What the paper did, for context (paper)

Chunks of 256 tokens, no overlap stated (§4.1); retrieval judged at the chunk level against the
ground-truth evidence; best reported pipeline voyage-02 + bge-reranker-large: MRR@10 0.586,
Hits@4 0.6625, Hits@10 0.7467 (Table 5). None of that compares with Full Support @2,048 over
whole paragraphs; it is not a baseline for Phase 16.

## What this means for the spec (interpretation)

**Gold is measurable.** Full Support is well defined: the paragraphs that contain the facts. The
mapping is exact for 99.5 % of the facts and needs one declared rule for the 29 that straddle a
break.

**The indexing unit can stay the same rule as Phases 9-15**: one paragraph of the source text, as
its author wrote it, delimited by the source's own breaks. That keeps the phase about the kind of
text, not about segmentation. Two things the spec must decide explicitly:

1. **Boilerplate paragraphs.** 4,538 units of five words or fewer are noise for BM25 and the entity
   index (`Advertisement` two hundred times). Options: keep them (the honest "as is" corpus; they
   cost little because they carry no entities and match no query), or drop units under a declared
   minimum length, recorded and counted, never touched after the label. Dropping nothing is the
   simplest and the most reproducible; the spec should say which and why.
2. **Long paragraphs.** 29 units over 384 words, as in MuSiQue (56 there); the same declaration.

**The corpus is tiny.** 28,711 units against MuSiQue's 101,962 and FullWiki's 5.2 M: Dense alone
will reach further, candidate sets will be small, and the hop's `N` and `df` come from a corpus
where one entity (a team, a company) recurs across many paragraphs of the same article. This is
the limitation to declare in advance, and a reason to look at D8-style diagnostics: whether the
hop's gains come from other articles or from the same article as P1.

**Question types.** Comparison queries ask about two articles reporting the same thing (two
sources, same entity); temporal ones about order of events; inference ones chain facts. The hop
by shared entity is plausibly strongest on comparison queries (same entity across sources). The
spec should preregister the primary endpoint over all 2,255 answerable queries (P14 vs P10-B, as in
Phase 15) and report the split by number of gold paragraphs and by query type, deciding nothing.

**No fitting, again.** Same frozen weights, `α`, depth, extractor and encoder. The GLiNER labels
(person, organization, location, work of art, event) were chosen for Wikipedia; on news they
still apply, but the spec should declare that the label set is frozen too.

**Cost (projection).** 28,711 units: GLiNER under 3 minutes on the Phase 9 GPU class, BGE seconds.
The whole extraction may even fit on the laptop CPU (measured 1.12 units/s in Phase 15's smoke:
about 7 hours), but the configuration digest would differ from Phase 9's (deviation 11.1), so a
rented GPU remains the clean path; under 0.5 USD in all.

## Segmentation without an LLM: what the literature does

Read for the author's question ("what do the strongest entity-based RAG systems do to cut text,
without an LLM?"). Summary, with sources:

- **Graph-RAG systems do not segment cleverly.** HippoRAG takes the benchmarks' own passages as
  given and runs OpenIE on each ([paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.14831)); GraphRAG
  and LightRAG cut fixed token windows (about 1,200 tokens with 100 overlap is the common setting)
  before entity extraction ([survey of graph RAG settings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05690)).
  The segmentation is not where these systems put their effort.
- **Semantic chunking without an LLM** exists and is cheap: boundaries where the cosine distance
  between consecutive sentence embeddings jumps (the "SemanticChunker" family), or
  [Max-Min semantic chunking](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-025-09638-7)
  (dynamic clustering of consecutive sentences by embedding similarity). Sequence-labelling
  segmenters over sentence encoders are the classical alternative.
- **The evidence that it pays is weak.** Vectara's NAACL 2025 study
  ([Is Semantic Chunking Worth the Computational Cost?](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.114/))
  found that fixed windows of about 200 words match or beat semantic chunking on real documents,
  with gains only on stitched, topic-diverse synthetic sets; later comparisons repeat the picture
  ([2026 evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00881v1),
  [systematic investigation](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06976)). Learned chunkers
  ([MoC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09600), [QChunker](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11650)) use LLMs
  or LLM supervision and are out of this line's budget by design.
- **LLM-free graph retrieval** is a current topic ([LiteSemRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16350)
  builds its graph from token embeddings without an LLM), but its abstract does not say how it
  segments; nothing there changes the picture.

**Reading for this project (interpretation):** for text that comes with its own paragraphs (news,
documentation, regulations, articles), the author's paragraph is the unit the literature itself
falls back to, and no LLM-free segmenter has shown a reliable gain over it. Segmentation becomes a
real question only for text without structure (transcripts, OCR, flat PDFs), and there the cheap
answer in the literature is a sentence-embedding boundary rule, not an LLM. That is a separate
phase, if ever, with its own question; Phase 16 should not carry it.
