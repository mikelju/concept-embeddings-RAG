# 4.1 — Closing the research line: concepts induced from pooled embeddings

Date: 2026-09-15. A deviation of Phase 4 in the sense of the master plan's convention: an off-plan
adjustment that the Phase 4 result made necessary.

## Why this is a deviation and not Phase 5

The master plan scheduled Phase 5 as the comparative evaluation of *this* method — full bench,
ablations, cost, a second benchmark and a verdict. Phase 4 ended with a negative result whose
mechanism is measured ([`4.results.md`](4.results.md)), and every lever the plan left to Phase 5
would measure in more detail an operationalization the evidence already rules out. So the line
closes here, beside the phase whose result closed it, and **Phase 5 stays free for the next
experiment** (see [`0_master_plan.md`](../0_master_plan.md)).

This document is documentary only. It introduces no retrieval method, benchmark, model, sweep or
metric, and **no test-split result is recomputed or replaced**: every test figure below is read from
a versioned result file, listed in §11.

It supersedes the first version of the closure (`phase_5/5.spec.md`, `5.0_research_closure.md` and
`5.results.md`), whose content is folded in here; §12 checks its acceptance criteria.

## 1. What was tested

- HotpotQA distractor validation, 2,000 questions (600 dev / 1,400 test), a unified pool of
  **19,366 paragraphs**;
- dense retrieval with `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` (384 dimensions);
- concept spaces induced by non-negative sparse coding **of the pooled paragraph embeddings**,
  K = 512, 1,024, 2,048 and 4,096 (Phase 2); K = 512 selected on dev (Phase 3);
- conceptual retrieval, and dense + conceptual score fusion — System B (Phase 3);
- diffusion over the paragraph × concept matrix with partial restart, seeded from dense (System C)
  or from conceptual retrieval (the isolating arm) (Phase 4);
- measurement at a fixed context-token budget, Full Support as the figure that decides.

## 2. The result on test

Full Support and gold recall at the 2,048-token budget, 1,400 test questions. All four budgets and
the dev split are in [`4.results.md`](4.results.md).

| System | Gold recall | Full Support |
|---|---:|---:|
| Concepts alone | 0.5604 | 0.3550 |
| Expansion, conceptual seed | 0.5450 | 0.3379 |
| BM25 | 0.8775 | 0.7593 |
| Dense | 0.9082 | 0.8250 |
| System B: dense + concepts | 0.9082 | 0.8250 |
| System C: diffusion, dense seed | 0.9057 | 0.8214 |
| Control: dense + BM25 | **0.9314** | **0.8643** |

System B's fusion weight was fitted on dev at `w = 1.0`: all of it on dense, so System B *is* dense
at every budget. System C tracks dense to within 0.0036 Full Support at every budget, inside what
1,400 questions resolve, and loses to the control at every budget by 2.4 to 4.3 points. The
conceptual seed is below conceptual-only at every budget: expanding the weaker seed makes it weaker.

## 3. Why the expansion did not expand

At every restart in the declared grid (0.2–0.8) **the walk returns exactly the seed's 100
paragraphs, reordered**: zero new paragraphs in the final top-100, on every dev question sampled.
Three measurements explain it (all in [`4.results.md`](4.results.md) §"Why no new unit is ever
promoted"):

- **The walk floods rather than hops.** All 19,366 paragraphs carry mass from the first round.
- **The seed's mass is nearly flat.** After normalization the lightest of the 100 seed paragraphs
  holds 0.00951 and the heaviest 0.01320, against 0.01 for a uniform split.
- **The propagation never concentrates.** The heaviest non-seed paragraph receives 0.00104 of the
  propagated mass.

At `restart = 0.8` the lightest seed paragraph is floored at 0.00761 while the best non-seed
paragraph reaches 0.00021 — a factor of 36. A new paragraph could only enter at **`restart <
0.0986`**, below the grid's floor. The one point measured below it, `restart = 0.0`, collapses to
0.0117 Full Support. Whether anything useful survives between 0 and 0.10 was not measured.

So System C is a re-ranker of dense's top-100. On dev it gains 5 questions dense misses and loses 6
dense had, and recovers 3 of the 72 questions that defeat both baselines (System B: 0; the control:
6). One of those three is recovered by nothing else in the project.

**This does not show that no graph or diffusion formulation can expand retrieval.** It shows that
this operator, on this matrix, seeded this way, cannot.

## 4. Cost

Mean latency per query on test, read from the result files: dense 0.90 ms, control 1.38 ms,
**System C 3.85 ms — 4.3x dense and 2.8x the control, for a result below both.** §73 of the source
document asks for any gain to be reported with its price; there is no gain, and this is the price.

## 5. Second-hop diagnostic — exploratory, dev only

**Status.** Run after Phase 4 was frozen and measured. It selects nothing, never reads test, and is
not a Phase 4 result. It is quoted because it answers the question §3 leaves open: *if a hop could
reach new paragraphs, would a hop through concepts point at the right one?*

**Protocol.** On the 600 dev questions, a reader has read dense's top-10. A gold paragraph outside
that top-10 is *missing*: **158 missing paragraphs across 152 questions, all of them bridge
questions**. Each hop starts from the best dense paragraph `p1`, ranks every paragraph not already
read, and the figure is the share of missing paragraphs found in its top-10 and top-100.

| Second hop from `p1` | Hit@10 | Hit@100 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Continue reading the dense list | 0.297 | 0.696 |
| BM25 on the question | **0.449** | 0.722 |
| Dense neighbours of `p1` | 0.392 | 0.652 |
| BM25 with `p1`'s text as query ("follow the names") | 0.443 | **0.741** |
| Concept hop, K = 512 (the selected space), all concepts | 0.076 | 0.367 |
| Concept hop, K = 4,096 (best concept variant), all concepts | 0.108 | 0.430 |

**The concept hop is the weakest second hop at every K**, below simply reading further down the
dense list. Describing each paragraph by only its 1, 2 or 4 strongest concepts never raised either
hit rate at any K (at hit@10 it ties at K = 1,024 and K = 2,048 with four concepts, and falls
everywhere else). The best concept variant is also the least selective: at K = 4,096 a hop from
`p1` reaches on average 99.4% of the pool — the same share of the corpus that Phase 2's most general
atom at that K activates.

Regenerate with `uv run python scripts/second_hop_diagnostic.py` → `data/diagnostics/second_hop-dev.json`,
which records the dictionary keys, pool hash and model it ran under. Nothing in it is stochastic.

## 6. What the evidence rules out

Within the tested formulation and ranges, further tuning is not a promising default:

- K = 512–4,096 did not make conceptual retrieval competitive with dense;
- the three query operators and both views of `X` produced no useful complementarity;
- score-level fusion selected dense alone;
- rarity damping helped conceptual retrieval (+0.043 on dev) without changing any verdict;
- diffusion with the declared restart and normalization grid introduced no new candidate;
- describing paragraphs by fewer concepts made the second hop worse, not better.

## 7. What remains open

Three questions this work does **not** answer, stated as the closure's source asked them to be:

1. **Concepts extracted from the text** — by an LLM, NER or term extraction — rather than
   directions found in a pooled embedding.
2. **A representation finer than one pooled embedding per paragraph** — token-level or
   multi-vector.
3. **A benchmark built for satellite and thematic context** (Type C in §72), rather than
   HotpotQA's entity bridges.

And, narrower, levers this line left untested:

- `restart` between 0 and 0.10, a sharper or deeper seed, a candidate set that reserves budget for
  new paragraphs, or intersection-style operators;
- entity graphs with personalized propagation;
- concept pruning and a dictionary without the non-negativity constraint;
- a stronger dense model or a reranker as the baseline;
- response-level metrics.

## 9. Verdict

### Supported negative conclusion

> On the tested HotpotQA setup, a concept space produced by sparse dictionary learning over pooled
> dense paragraph embeddings did not provide measurable complementary retrieval value over the
> dense baseline — by direct conceptual retrieval, by score fusion, or by the tested diffusion-based
> expansion. A dense + BM25 control was clearly stronger.

### Explicit non-conclusion

This does **not** refute the hypothesis of §69, that a corpus can be represented through
interpretable concepts discovered from the corpus and that retrieval can expand through them. The
tested implementation changed what "concept" means: dictionary atoms derived from dense paragraph
embeddings — a sparse recoding of the very vector the dense baseline already uses, and therefore
structurally unlikely to carry information that vector lacks — rather than concepts extracted from
the text itself.

On the second hypothesis — whether the representation improves contextual recall over dense-only
RAG, especially for multi-hop and satellite questions — the answer is **no for multi-hop questions
of HotpotQA's kind (Type B), for this representation**, and **not measured for satellite questions
(Type C)**, which no standard benchmark provides.

The benchmark also differs from the setting the proposal described: HotpotQA is heterogeneous, and
its bridge questions are built around an entity named in one paragraph and described in another —
consistent with BM25 on `p1`'s text being the strongest second hop at depth 100 in §5.

## 10. Decision for the next block

The current formulation is closed as a negative result. The next block does not tune its parameters.

The highest-information next experiment is a **cheap pilot with text-derived entities and concepts**
on a small set of bridge failures, with the dense and BM25 setup held fixed, asking one question
first: *can explicit text-derived nodes recover a missing gold paragraph through the context already
retrieved, where the pooled-embedding concept space could not?* It is Phase 5 of the revised master
plan and is not yet specified.

Two cautions carried forward. If entity nodes win, the pilot shows that names are good navigation
nodes, which is not yet the concept hypothesis — the design has to be able to tell the two apart.
And the founding decisions this pilot would change (the concept engine, the concept embedding) are
revised in the master plan only after they are verified, as that table requires.
