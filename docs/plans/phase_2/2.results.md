## Sparsity and reconstruction

| K | Coding `alpha` | Active/unit mean | median | p5 | p95 | max | `nnz` | Reconstruction error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 0.20281 | **15.63** | 15 | 9 | 23 | 37 | 302,754 | 0.4007 |
| 1,024 | 0.29699 | **9.23** | 9 | 4 | 15 | 26 | 178,699 | 0.5386 |
| 2,048 | 0.29699 | **10.01** | 10 | 5 | 15 | 25 | 193,910 | 0.5236 |
| 4,096 | 0.29699 | **8.26** | 8 | 4 | 13 | 21 | 159,919 | 0.4871 |

**All four spaces clear the rejection floor by a wide margin**, and all four land inside the 8-16
target band. The one-hot regime that disqualified clustering does not appear at any K: even the
5th percentile unit carries 4 active concepts. Co-activation exists, which is the precondition
Phase 4 needs and the only thing this table actually establishes.

Two caveats the table cannot carry on its own:

- **The reconstruction errors are not comparable across K.** `alpha` is not a constant here: it is
  searched per K until the achieved sparsity lands in the band. K = 512 shows the lowest error
  (0.4007) not because 512 atoms reconstruct better than 4,096, but because its calibrated penalty
  is the loosest (0.20281 against 0.29699) and it is therefore allowed almost twice as many active
  concepts per unit. Comparing reconstruction across K would require refitting all four at a fixed
  `alpha`, which was not done and is not planned.
- **The sweep console output and the artifacts disagree slightly, and the artifacts are right.**
  The `[INFO] coding alpha ... -> N active concepts per unit` lines report 15.84 / 9.40 / 10.01 /
  8.27; those come from the 2,000-unit bisection sample inside `calibrate_coding_alpha`. The values
  above are measured over all 19,366 units. Use the artifacts.

## Coverage: dead atoms, orphan units, deduplication

| K | Dead atoms (0 units) | Orphan units | Atoms with <3 units | <=5 units | <=10 units | Units/concept median | max |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 549 | 1,403 |
| 1,024 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 141 | 1,356 |
| 2,048 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 12 | 65 | **12,914** |
| 4,096 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 97 | 637 | 22 | **19,253** |

**No dead atoms and no orphan units at any K.** Every atom is used by at least one unit and every
unit is reached by at least one concept, so no paragraph is unreachable through the concept space.

The dead-atom threshold is `DEAD_ATOM_MIN_UNITS = 1`, which means an atom used by a single unit
counts as alive. That is a defensible definition and it is also why the clean zero column hides
what the next three columns show: at K = 4,096 there are 7 atoms activated by fewer than 3 units
and 637 activated by 10 or fewer -- 15.6% of the dictionary is supported by evidence too thin to
call a concept. At K = 512 and K = 1,024 that tail does not exist at all.

**Deduplication merged nothing, at any K.** All four merge logs record `merged_fraction 0.0`,
zero groups and `finding: null`: not one pair of atoms reached cosine 0.90, not even with 4,096
atoms over 19,366 units. The 20% merge budget was never touched. This is a real result and not a
bug -- the guard exists to catch a K so large that the dictionary starts duplicating itself, and
that failure mode did not occur in this sweep. It does mean the deduplication stage is, on this
corpus, an assertion that passed rather than a transformation that did anything.

## Quality against the null baseline

Null measured on this same pool: **0.4292 +/- 0.0073** (200 random sets of 25 units, seed 42).
A raw cosine is unreadable without it: two unrelated paragraphs of this corpus score 0.43, not 0.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 0.5541 | 0.5498 | 0.4739 | 0.7556 | 16.99 | 3.04 | 0.579 | 0.202 | 0.192 | 0 | 0 |
| 1,024 | 0.5470 | 0.5394 | 0.4761 | 0.7255 | 16.03 | 2.48 | 0.548 | 0.526 | 0.500 | 0 | 0 |
| 2,048 | 0.5373 | 0.5291 | 0.4772 | 0.7455 | 14.71 | 3.55 | 0.520 | 0.729 | 0.763 | 0 | 0 |
| 4,096 | 0.5351 | 0.5275 | 0.4805 | 0.7951 | 14.40 | **-1.49** | 0.508 | 0.922 | 1.000 | **3** | **7** |

Coherence sits well above the null everywhere: the *worst* concept of K = 512 is still 3 standard
deviations above what randomness scores here, and the mean concept is 14 to 17 above. The units
that activate a concept do have something in common, at every K tested.

The trend is monotone and shallow: coherence, atom alignment and mean z all decay as K grows
(0.554 -> 0.535, 0.579 -> 0.508, 17.0 -> 14.4). The decay is small in absolute terms; what changes
qualitatively is the tail, not the mean. K = 4,096 is the first and only K that produces concepts
scoring *below* random.

**`top_mass_share` is the diagnostic that separates the four spaces**, and it moves far harder than
coherence does: 0.202 -> 0.526 -> 0.729 -> 0.922, with a median of exactly 1.000 at K = 4,096. A
median of 1.0 means the typical concept has all of its weight inside its top 25 units, which is
another way of stating the units/concept median of 22. At K = 4,096 the dictionary has largely
stopped describing the corpus and started indexing it.

## The finding that matters for Phase 4: one concept swallows the corpus

Neither the coherence statistics nor the dead/orphan counts flag this, and it is the most
consequential thing in the sweep.

| K | Largest concept | Units it activates | Share of pool | Share of all `nnz` | Second largest |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | `c450` | 1,403 | 7.2% | 0.5% | 1,378 (7.1%) |
| 1,024 | `c28` | 1,356 | 7.0% | 0.8% | 1,055 (5.4%) |
| 2,048 | **`c0`** | **12,914** | **66.7%** | 6.7% | 1,006 (5.2%) |
| 4,096 | **`c0`** | **19,253** | **99.4%** | 12.0% | 1,327 (6.9%) |

At K = 512 and K = 1,024 the distribution has no hub: no concept reaches 10% of the pool, and the
largest sits within a factor of 1.02 (K = 512) and 1.29 (K = 1,024) of the second. From K = 2,048 a
single atom appears that is active on two thirds of the corpus -- 12.8 times the next concept -- and
at K = 4,096 on essentially all of it, 14.5 times the next and holding an eighth of every non-zero
in the matrix.

**This atom passes every quality check.**

| | Coherence | z | Atom alignment | `top_mass_share` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `c0` at K = 2,048 | 0.5643 | **+18.38** | 0.6371 | **0.0091** |
| `c0` at K = 4,096 | 0.5040 | **+10.18** | 0.6517 | **0.0028** |

Its coherence is *above* the mean of its own dictionary, because coherence is measured over the top
25 units by weight and those 25 really are alike. Read through coherence alone, `c0` looks like one
of the better concepts in the space. The number that catches it is `top_mass_share`: 0.0028 against
a 5th percentile of 0.558 at the same K. The over-generality diagnostic caught the one over-general
concept, and the coherence diagnostic did not -- which is the argument for HU-7 carrying three
scores rather than one, stated here as a measured case instead of a design intention.

For Phase 4 this is a warning, not a verdict. Diffusion over `X` travels along shared concepts; an
atom shared by 99.4% of units is an edge from everything to everything, and expansion through it
would reach the whole corpus in one hop while looking like it used the concept space. Whether that
actually degrades retrieval is a Phase 3/4 measurement, and nothing here settles it. What this
phase can say is that the substrate is not uniform across K, and that a K chosen on recall alone
would be chosen without knowing this.

## Reading the concepts below the null (K = 4,096)

T17 requires these to be read, not counted. All three sit at the opposite extreme from `c0`: thin
support, `top_mass_share` of exactly 1.0, and evidence with no shared subject.

`c239` is the clearest case: a 1994 sitcom season, a Long Island village, a 1947 Disney package

real use for, not a concept about cinema.

Seven further concepts are **undefined**, activated by fewer than the 3 units the score requires:
`c487`, `c623`, `c1738`, `c2674`, `c3563`, `c3605`, `c3957` (1 or 2 units each). They are reported
as undefined rather than as zero, and they are not pruned: nothing in this phase removes a concept.

Read together, the two extremes are the same phenomenon. At K = 4,096 the dictionary has more atoms
than the corpus has distinguishable topics, so capacity leaks in both directions -- a handful of
atoms are supported by almost nothing, and one absorbs everything that fits nowhere else. K = 2,048
shows the beginning of the same shape (`c0` at 66.7%) without the below-null tail; K = 512 and
K = 1,024 show neither.

### The labels corroborate the coherence score, and were not shown it

The model saw ten paragraphs per concept and nothing else -- no coherence value, no z, no rank, no
other concept. It was asked to name what those paragraphs have in common. In 511 of the 2,048 names
(25.0%) it answered, in effect, *nothing*: the name describes the **document format** rather than a
subject -- "Wikipedia-style factual stub entries", "Miscellaneous Wikipedia infobox summaries",
"Generic Wikipedia stub entries". Sorted by coherence, those names are not spread evenly:

| Band of 100 concepts, by coherence | Names describing format rather than subject |
|---|---:|
| 100 worst | **84** |
| 100 around the median | 12 |
| 100 best | **4** |

That is an independent signal agreeing with the geometric one. Coherence is a mean pairwise cosine
over the top 25 units; the label is a different model reading ten paragraphs. Nothing connects them
in the code, and they rank the same concepts the same way. This is the closest thing this phase has
to external validation of HU-7's scoring, and it is the reason the labelling stage earns its cost
even though retrieval never reads its output.

The count itself is a **lexical probe, not a semantic judgement**. It will miss a vacuous name
phrased differently and can flag a legitimately Wikipedia-about-Wikipedia concept, so read the
25.0% as an order of magnitude and the 84/12/4 gradient as the finding. The probe is stated in
full rather than described, because a number in this report has to be reproducible from the
artifacts on disk:

### The hub, named

The atom that activates on 66.7% of the corpus was labelled without the model being told anything
about its size:

> **`c0` -- "Wikipedia-style entity overview"**
> *"Each paragraph is an encyclopedic introductory summary defining a named entity (era, newspaper,
> nation, football club, place, ethnic group, or person) with its origin, naming history, and key

Shown ten of its 12,914 units, the model named it by **format**, not by topic -- and it named the

conclusion the `top_mass_share` of 0.0091 reaches by a completely different route, and it sharpens
the Phase 4 warning above: `c0` is not a concept that happens to be large, it is the corpus's
house style wearing a concept's clothes.

## Why it happens: K divides a fixed budget of evidence

Three findings above were recorded as separate observations: the hub at K >= 2,048, the below-null
tail at K = 4,096, and the 511 concepts at K = 2,048 named by document format. They have one cause,
and that cause has a dose-response curve in K.

Calibrating `alpha` per K holds the activation budget roughly constant -- every space lands inside
the 8-16 band by construction -- so the corpus contributes about the same total number of non-zeros
whatever K is. **Raising K therefore adds no evidence. It divides the same evidence into thinner
slices.**

| K | Active concepts/unit | Mean units per concept | Median | Concepts with <30 units | Largest concept: share of pool | of mass |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 15.63 | 591.3 | 549 | 0 (0.0%) | 7.2% | 0.8% |
| 1,024 | 9.23 | 174.5 | 141 | 40 (3.9%) | 7.0% | 0.7% |
| 2,048 | 10.01 | 94.7 | 65 | 261 (12.7%) | **66.7%** | 5.2% |
| 4,096 | 8.26 | 39.0 | 22 | **2,610 (63.7%)** | **99.4%** | **23.5%** |

The last column is measured on the **row-normalized** view, not on raw weights: the `raw` artifact
states in its own `weight_semantics` that raw coefficients are not comparable across units, because
a unit explained by fewer atoms carries larger ones. Each unit is therefore given a total attention
of 1 before summing, and the column reads as the share of the corpus's whole attention that one
atom holds.

That share tells something the unit count does not. At K = 4,096, `c0` holds 23.5% of all attention
against a 12.0% share of `nnz`: it does not merely appear in nearly every unit, it carries about
twice the weight its raw count suggests. At K = 2,048 the same concept is the reverse -- 66.7% of
units but 5.2% of mass against 6.7% of `nnz` -- a weak participant in very many units. **The hub
does not only grow with K, it hardens.**

The two failure modes are one displaced capacity seen from opposite ends. By K = 4,096 the median
concept rests on 22 units: fewer than the 25 that `coherence` is computed over, and barely twice
the 10 paragraphs shown to the labelling model. Meanwhile one atom absorbs everything those thin
atoms cannot explain.

### The link is measured, not assumed

At K = 2,048, support and coherence rank the concepts almost identically: Spearman rho = **0.7741**
across all 2,048. The format-describing names fall exactly where that predicts. Their median
support is 40 units against 77 for the rest, and the fraction rises monotonically as support falls:

| Units supporting the concept | Concepts | Named by format rather than by subject |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fewer than 30 | 261 | **52.1%** |
| 30 to 64 | 747 | 35.3% |
| 65 or more (the pool median and above) | 1,040 | **10.7%** |

The chain therefore runs: K up -> support per concept down -> coherence down -> the labelling
model, shown ten paragraphs and no number, names a document format because no subject is left to
name. Every link is measured on this corpus, and the last was measured by a reader with access to
none of the others.

**What this does not license.** Only K = 2,048 is labelled, so the format-name fraction exists at
exactly one K. The thin-concept counts and the hub shares are measured at all four, but the step
from those to "the format fraction is worse at K = 4,096" is an inference, not a measurement.
Labelling the other three spaces would cost roughly what this one did and was not authorised; the
gap is declared, not filled. The 30-unit line is likewise a disclosed choice, not a fitted
threshold: it is the point below which the 10 units shown to the labeller stop being a sample of
the concept and become most of it.

### What Phase 3 should take from this

Nothing here selects a K and nothing here prunes a concept: decision D11 stands, and no parameter
of a recorded experiment was touched. What it adds is that recall on the development split is not
the only axis along which these four spaces differ, and that the axis recall cannot see is the one
Phase 4 depends on. Diffusion travels along shared concepts, and an atom shared by 99.4% of the
pool is an edge from everything to everything.

Two consequences, to be written into the Phase 3 spec before it runs rather than after:

- **Report the hub share and the thin-concept count beside dev recall for each K.** Both are
  properties of `X` measured on the corpus, never on the evaluation split, so reporting them costs
  nothing in contamination terms and prevents a K being chosen without knowing this.
- **If the selected K carries a hub, mass damping belongs in Phase 4 as a declared ablation** --
  weighting a concept in the diffusion by its rarity, which is a retrieval parameter that leaves
  the dictionary untouched and is measured with and against. Declared in advance it is evidence;
  improvised once the numbers disappoint it is a repair, and would not be reportable as either.

What is *not* proposed is filtering concepts by their labels. A concept's embedding is its atom and
retrieval never reads a name; selecting concepts by an LLM's wording would put a non-reproducible
judgement inside the retrieval path through the back door. Any future removal must key on a
measured, regenerable property -- support, mass -- and not on a name.

Every figure in this section is regenerated by:

```python
# uv run --no-sync python dilution.py, from the repository root.
import json
import re
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
from scipy import sparse, stats

d = Path("data/concepts")


def load(key):
    z = np.load(d / f"matrix-{key}-raw.npz", allow_pickle=False)
    return sparse.csr_matrix((z["data"], z["indices"], z["indptr"]), shape=tuple(z["shape"]))


def order(p):
    return json.loads(p.read_text())["n_concepts"]


for f in sorted(d.glob("diagnostics-*.json"), key=order):
    g = json.loads(f.read_text("utf-8"))
    X = load(g["dictionary_key"])
    sup = np.diff(X.tocsc().indptr)
    rs = np.asarray(X.sum(axis=1)).ravel()
    m = np.asarray((sparse.diags(1 / np.where(rs, rs, 1)) @ X).sum(axis=0)).ravel()  # normalized
    print(
        f"{g['n_concepts']:5d} {g['active_per_unit']['mean']:6.2f} {sup.mean():7.1f} "
        f"{np.median(sup):5.0f} {(sup < 30).sum():5d} {100 * (sup < 30).mean():5.1f} "
        f"{100 * sup.max() / X.shape[0]:5.1f} {100 * m.max() / m.sum():5.1f}"
    )

K = "2fd69f5a413a6922"
X = load(K)
sup = np.diff(X.tocsc().indptr)
coh = json.loads((d / f"diagnostics-{K}.json").read_text("utf-8"))["quality"]["coherence"]
coh = np.array([coh[str(i)] for i in range(len(sup))])
lab = json.loads((d / f"labels-{K}.json").read_text("utf-8"))["labels"]
P = re.compile(
    r"wikipedia|stub|encyclopedi|miscellaneous|infobox|generic|assorted|"
    r"factual (summar|entr|articl|blurb|descript)|mixed",
    re.I,
)
fmt = np.array([bool(P.search(lab[str(i)]["name"])) for i in range(len(sup))])
print(
    "spearman",
    round(stats.spearmanr(sup, coh).statistic, 4),
    "| median support fmt",
    int(np.median(sup[fmt])),
    "vs",
    int(np.median(sup[~fmt])),
)
for lo, hi in ((0, 30), (30, 65), (65, 1 << 30)):
    b = (sup >= lo) & (sup < hi)
    print(f"[{lo},{hi if hi < 1 << 30 else '+'}) n={b.sum():5d} fmt={100 * fmt[b].mean():5.1f}%")
```

It prints one row per K -- K, active/unit, mean support, median support, count and percentage
under 30 units, hub share of pool, hub share of mass -- then the Spearman correlation, the two
median supports, and the three support bands.

- **The K = 4,096 time is not a measurement.** It equals the calendar interval 09:24:50 -> 12:52:13

  elapsed calendar time, not compute time. It is recorded here as a **declared gap**: the real cost

- **The `[WARN]` about the 7,200 s budget is cosmetic.** The guard is evaluated *after* each K
  completes, so it fired once the last K was already done and cut nothing. The budget did not
  protect the run; the sweep exceeded it by 85% and finished anyway because K = 4,096 was last.
  If a future sweep puts a large K anywhere but last, this guard will not stop that either.

Read the **11.73**, not the 7.83 the artifact reports. `cost.actual_usd` inside

served from disk, having been paid for in the runs that died. The figure above is the sum over all
2,048 cached answers, which is what actually left the account. A call that failed was never billed,

**Decision D8 forecast ~8.1 USD; the real spend is 45% above it.** It assumed ~1,500 input
tokens per concept;
the measured figure is 2,420, because ten full Wikipedia lead paragraphs are longer than the
estimate assumed. The code's own pre-run estimator, which uses the real evidence, printed 12.22 USD
and was accurate to 4%. The user authorised the resume against the corrected figure, not against
D8's.

**The elapsed time of the labelling is not a measurement.** The run was interrupted three times by
causes outside the code: an `APIConnectionError` at 102 concepts when the machine suspended, then
`anthropic.OverloadedError` (HTTP 529) at 684, twice within three minutes. The disk cache made each
restart free, so the spend above is clean while the wall time is not, and no wall time is reported
for this stage. The second 529 killed the relaunch on its **first uncached call**, which falsified

evidence to raise the SDK retry budget from 2 to `MAX_RETRIES = 8`, and the run then completed the
remaining 1,364 concepts without a single failure. The labels are unaffected either way: a retry
count is not a request parameter, so it cannot change an answer.

## What these numbers are not

**None of this is evidence for or against the hypothesis of the project.** Nothing on this page
touches retrieval, no question was answered, and no metric here is comparable to the Phase 1
baselines. A concept space with excellent coherence can retrieve worse than BM25, and this phase
is deliberately incapable of noticing.

What the phase does establish, and the whole of it:

1. Non-negative sparse coding produces a genuinely sparse, genuinely co-activating `X` on this
   corpus at all four K -- the regime that disqualified clustering does not appear.
2. The concepts are not random: coherence runs 14 to 17 standard deviations above a null measured
   on this same pool.
3. The four spaces are not equivalent, and they differ most where a mean does not look: in the hub
   concept at K >= 2,048 and in the thin tail at K = 4,096.
4. The coherence score measures something a second, independent reader agrees with: of the 100
   worst-coherence concepts at K = 2,048, 84 were named by their document format rather than by
   any subject, against 4 of the 100 best.
5. Those differences have one measured cause. Calibrating `alpha` per K fixes the activation
   budget, so K divides a constant amount of evidence: the median concept falls from 549
   supporting units at K = 512 to 22 at K = 4,096, and support ranks the concepts almost as
   coherence does (Spearman rho = 0.7741 at K = 2,048).

No K is selected, no concept is pruned, no parameter was tuned against the evaluation set, and the
test split was not looked at in this phase, not even descriptively.
