## Why it did not help: a dev diagnostic (exploratory)

Computed after the gate from the stored dev lists, at the author's request, to explain the
result. Scripts and outputs: `data/phase11/closing-diagnostic/` (`funnel.txt` sha256
`f321b4ba…`, `ties.txt` sha256 `781b00bf…`). Nothing was fitted or selected on these figures.
They describe the 4,310 gold paragraphs that Dense leaves outside its top 10 on dev.

### 1. Reach is not the bottleneck; selection is

| | Gold paragraphs |
|---|---:|
| Outside Dense's top 10 | 4,310 |
| In the uncapped P1 hop's depth-100 list | 1,665 |
| … among its first 5 | 590 |
| … median position when listed | 11 |
| In any entity list (P1 at any cap, or the question hop) | 2,237 |

The hop often reaches the right paragraph and then buries it.

### 2. The rarity weight is correct but coarse: ties

A candidate scores the sum of `log(1 + N / (1 + DF))` over the entities it shares with P1. The
weight belongs to the **entity**, so every paragraph sharing the same entity with P1 gets exactly
the same score:

- 1,290 of the 1,665 listed gold paragraphs **tie** with at least one other candidate (median 5
  tied, 4 strictly above);
- a typical 100-candidate list holds a median of **7 distinct scores**;
- ties are broken by unit id, a hash, so the order inside a tie is arbitrary with respect to the
  question.

This is also why the DF cap changes little. A hub already weighed almost nothing, so removing it
shortens the list but barely moves the top of it.

### 3. The question hop duplicates BM25

Of the 421 gold paragraphs the question hop places in its first 5, **247 are already in BM25's top
10** (337 in its top 100). BM25 matches the question's words, and the question's entities are its
proper names. The P1 hop is different: of its 590 first-5 gold paragraphs, only 158 are in BM25's
top 10. This is why the P1 hop added value over Dense + BM25 in Phase 10, and the question hop does
not here.

### Reading

The hop's ranking has no information about **which of P1's entities the question needs**, that is,
which one is the bridge to the missing paragraph. BM25 reads the question but not P1. The P1 hop
reads P1 but not the question. The fusion adds the two lists without either choosing the entity.
Typed relations would supply exactly this. For "in which year was the director of *Titanic*
born?", a relation `Titanic —directed by→ James Cameron` singles out one of the many entities of
*Titanic*'s paragraph, where the hop weighs Cameron, DiCaprio and Winslet alike by rarity. A second
ingredient of GraphRAG-style systems is linking an entity to its own article, rather than to every
paragraph that mentions it. The hop has neither. This reading is an interpretation of dev
diagnostics, not a measurement of relations or of any GraphRAG system.

## Limitations

1. **The question extractor is the laptop pin, not the Phase 9 digest (deviation 11.1).** Model,
   revision, labels, parameters, weights and library versions are the Phase 9 ones; the torch build
   is `+cpu` instead of `+cu126`, which the configuration digest covers. On 1,000 re-extracted
   corpus paragraphs, 998 records were identical and 2 differed by one entity each. The corpus
   index itself is Phase 9's, unchanged.
2. **GLiNER ran slower on `test-11` than on dev** (median 0.698 s against 0.388 s), almost
   certainly because of other load on the laptop during that hour, not because of the questions.
   Since no pass ran, the figure reports nothing. It would have been the D7 GLiNER latency.
3. **The explanation is post hoc.** The diagnostic above was designed after the gate had fired, on
   the same dev questions. It describes the mechanism; it does not test an alternative.
4. **Dev is heavily reused.** The 7,405 validation questions have now been fitted on by Phases 10
   and 11. They remain valid for selection, not as held-out evidence.
5. **Latency and memory were not measured** for any Phase 11 configuration; the candidate-set
   sizes are the only cost evidence.

## Next research decision

Phase 11 closes with `DEV_STOP`. P10-C (Dense + BM25 + the query-blind Entity Hop) stays the line's
best system, and `test-11` stays reserved and unopened.

The diagnostic moves the open question. Reach is no longer the question; the measured bottleneck is
**choosing the bridge entity among P1's**. Two directions address it, recorded in
[`research_roadmap.md`](../research_roadmap.md), in order of cost:

- **cheap:** use the question to choose which of P1's entities to hop from, for example by the
  similarity between the question and the sentence of P1 where each entity appears. This is a form
  of the roadmap's query-aware hop (7.2), and it isolates the selection hypothesis without building
  any relation;
- **expensive:** typed relations between entities, and linking each entity to its own article
  (roadmap 8.1, 8.2), the path towards GraphRAG/HippoRAG-style retrieval, at the extraction cost of
  5.2M paragraphs.

A separate, cost-only finding may be worth keeping: a DF cap of 1,000-10,000 cuts the hop's
candidate sets 7-24× with dev Full Support within ±7 questions of P10-C. Its latency effect is unmeasured.

Which direction comes next is the author's decision.
