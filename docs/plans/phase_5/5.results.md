# Phase 5 results: navigating by entities and concepts read from the text

> Produced by T16. Every figure below is read from an on-disk artifact, named beside it with the
> command that regenerates it. Nothing here re-runs a measurement: the hop run, the traces and the
> gate were written once by `uv run cer navigate`, which refuses a second run, and the few figures
> not stored in an artifact (fragmentation, reachability) are pure read-only functions over the
> frozen node index, named where they are used.

## Verdict

**The gate returned `NAMES_ONLY`.** It was declared in [`5.spec.md`](5.spec.md) HU-7 before any
number existed, computed by code from the per-question scores, and it is in
`data/navigation/gate-decision.json` with its three tests, the reported-only fourth, and an empty
`anomalies` list.

**Entities navigate, and the margin is not small.** A hop from `p1` over shared entity nodes finds
the paragraph dense missed at **0.6579 hit@10 per question** against the frozen comparator's — BM25
on the question — **0.4441**. The paired sign test is 56 wins, 24 losses, 72 ties, **p = 0.000226**.
That is the largest hit@10 of any second hop measured in this project, on the population the Phase 4
diagnostic defined.

**Text-derived concepts are the weakest hop this project has measured.** The concept-only arm scores
**0.0592 hit@10** and **0.1743 hit@100** per question — below the pooled-embedding concept hop that
Phase 4 already ruled out (0.0789 and 0.3684 on the same questions), and far below simply reading
further down the dense list (0.3026 and 0.6974). Against the comparator it is 5 wins, 66 losses,
81 ties, p ≈ 1. This is the arm that carries the hypothesis, and it is last.

**Adding concepts to entities makes the joint hop worse, which is the failure the gate was built to
detect.** E + C scores 0.4803 hit@10 against entity-only's 0.6579. The test "EC beats E" is **1 win,
29 losses, 122 ties**; the test "EC beats BM25-on-question" does not pass either (41 / 37 / 74,
p = 0.367). The concept nodes do not add a signal the names lack; they add breadth that demotes the
paragraph the names had already put in the top 10.

**The mechanism is measured, not inferred.** The entity hop is selective — a median of **34.5**
paragraphs receive a positive score from `p1`, 173.3 on average, **0.90% of the pool** on the mean —
and it lands what it finds at
**median rank 2**, with 102 of its 116 hits inside the top 10. The concept hop is broad (median 248
candidates) and cannot reach much: only **47 of the 158 missing paragraphs share a single concept
node with `p1`**, against 118 for entities. The union inherits the breadth (median 391.5 candidates,
11.3x the entity arm's) without inheriting reach: on the 108 paragraphs both arms find, **EC ranks
72 of them worse, 7 better and 29 the same**, and **23 fall out of the top 10** that entity-only had
them in.

**The headline is a depth-10 result, and it does not survive to depth 100.** At hit@100 the entity
hop scores 0.7434 per question — **below BM25 with `p1`'s text (0.7533)**, the "follow the names"
hop the Phase 4 closure already named as the strongest deep second hop. The entity arm is not
finding paragraphs the older hops cannot reach; it is finding them *sooner*. Its ceiling is
reachability: 118 of 158 missing paragraphs share an entity node with `p1`, and the arm finds 116 of
those 118. Beyond that ceiling no depth helps.

**So the stop is narrow and it is not a null result.** Names are good navigation nodes on this
benchmark — a positive finding, recorded as one — and concepts read from the text are not. That
closes the concept hypothesis for this operationalization on this benchmark, and leaves untouched
everything §7 of [`4.1_research_line_closure.md`](../phase_4/4.1_research_line_closure.md) already
listed as open. §"The scope of the stop" below says exactly which.

### The depth question, stated honestly

The gate measures hit@10. At hit@100 the ordering changes and the headline is qualified:

| Hop | q@10 | q@100 | Gain from 10 → 100 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Node hop, entity | **0.6579** | 0.7434 | +0.0855 |
| BM25 with `p1`'s text | 0.4507 | **0.7533** | +0.3026 |
| BM25 on the question | 0.4441 | 0.7171 | +0.2730 |
| Continue the dense list | 0.3026 | 0.6974 | +0.3947 |
| Node hop, entity + concept | 0.4803 | 0.7434 | +0.2632 |

**The entity hop is nearly saturated at depth 10.** It finds 102 of its 116 hits inside the top 10;
the remaining 90 ranks buy it 14 more paragraphs. Every other hop gains three to four times as much
between the two depths, and at depth 100 the entity hop is **second, behind BM25 on `p1`'s text**
(0.7434 against 0.7533 per question; 0.7342 against 0.7405 per paragraph).

**The ceiling is reachability, and it is measured.** A missing paragraph can only be ranked by an arm
if it shares at least one node with `p1`. Over the 158 missing paragraphs (read-only over the frozen
index and pilot; a shared node needs df ≥ 2 by construction):

| Arm | Missing paragraphs sharing ≥ 1 node with `p1` | Found @100 | Share of its own ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| entity | **118** of 158 (0.7468) | 116 | **98.3%** |
| concept | 47 of 158 (0.2975) | 28 | 59.6% |
| entity + concept | **126** of 158 (0.7975) | 116 | 92.1% |

The entity arm is within **two paragraphs** of everything it can possibly reach. No deeper ranking,
no better scoring and no tie-break can move it: for 40 of the 158 missing paragraphs, `p1` and the
gold share no entity name at all.

**What this means for the verdict.** The entity hop's contribution is precision at shallow depth, not
coverage. Read as "a reader who will look at ten more paragraphs", it is the best hop in the project
by 21 points. Read as "the set of paragraphs an entity graph can reach at all", it is slightly worse
than running BM25 on `p1`'s text, which needs no extraction, no node index and no 26 USD. Both
readings are true and the gate only measured the first.

**No significance test is computed at depth 100.** The spec forbids re-running the gate with a
different metric, and hit@100 is a reported figure that decides nothing (HU-7). The ordering above is
descriptive. The same restriction applies to the comparison against `bm25-follow(p1)` at depth 10,
where the entity hop leads by 0.2072 per question: the comparator was frozen as `bm25-question`
precisely so that no rival could be chosen after the numbers were seen, and that freeze is honoured
here even though it would have been the easier rival.

## The mechanism, from the traces

`data/navigation/traces.json`, bound to the hop-run digest, **260 traces**: one per (question, arm,
missing paragraph) found within depth 100 — 116 entity, 28 concept, 116 entity + concept, exactly the
`found@100` counts above. Every trace's node contributions sum to its recorded score (checked over
all 260: zero mismatches).

The traces were read by the rule D10 fixed in advance: **the first five pilot questions by id in
which each arm found a missing paragraph**, plus **every case where EC found a paragraph E did not**.
Nothing else was read.

> *Note on a field name.* HU-6 calls the per-node quantity `contribution`; the artifact writes it as
> `weight`. With binary incidence the two are the same number — a shared node contributes exactly its
> rarity weight — and the sums check out, so this is a naming difference between spec and artifact,
> not a missing field.

### Why entities navigate: the five entity traces

| Question | The bridge, in one line | Found at | Score | Nodes shared with `p1` |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `5a712beb…e61c` | Golden Globe actor in *Little Fugitive* | rank **5** | 8.26 | `peter dinklage` (8.26) |
| `5a718467…e864` | Manufacturer of the Ford CD2 platform's inspiration | rank **1** | 33.01 | `mazda capella` (8.77), `mazda 626` (8.49), `mazda` (8.08), `ford` (7.68) |
| `5a72944c…c351` | Who brought the Red Sox to City of Palms Park | rank **1** | 15.65 | `mike greenwell` (8.77), `boston red sox` (6.88) |
| `5a72f74a…af59` | Comedian born 21 March 1962 in *Sleepless in Seattle* | rank **7** | 8.26 | `rosie o'donnell` (8.26) |
| `5a74106b…289e` | Where Sachin Warrier's employer is headquartered | rank **4** | 8.77 | `tata consultancy services` (8.77) |

**The path is legible in every one**: `p1` names a person, a company or a car; the paragraph that
answers the second hop is the one that names the same thing. Three of the five are carried by a
**single** node, which is the shape the scoring makes possible: the maximum weight any shared node
can carry is `log(1 + 19366/3) = 8.773`, the weight of a node appearing in exactly two paragraphs, so
one rare name is enough to put a paragraph first. Across all 116 entity traces the mean is **1.46
shared nodes** and the mean node weight **7.85** — near the 8.773 ceiling. The entity arm wins on
rare names, not on many names.

This is also why it is selective: 96,416 distinct entity nodes, of which only **16,702 (17.3%)**
appear in two or more paragraphs and can therefore connect anything at all. The rest are dead weight
that costs nothing.

| `5a77e62b…dfea` — birthplace of the musician behind *The Motion Makes Me Last* | rank 1 | 7.39 | `ambient music` (7.39) |

**Two of these five are what the hypothesis predicted**: `ambient music` and `black metal` are
topical nodes, not names, and they connect two paragraphs a name could not. The concept space does
what it was built to do — occasionally.

**The other three show why it is not enough.** `tribe` and `nickname` are not concepts of a bridge;
they are generic nouns that any number of paragraphs share, and they put the gold at rank 38 and rank
3 respectively by accident of how few competitors happened to share them. The concept vocabulary is
dominated by exactly this kind of node: the most frequent concepts are `actor` (727 paragraphs),
`single` (688), `director` (640), `studio album` (639), `album` (620), `song` (561), `film` (535).
They carry weights of 3.3 to 3.6 — a third of a rare name's — but they are shared by hundreds of
paragraphs at once, and several of them together outrank one exact name.

And the arm cannot reach far enough to make up for it: **only 47 of 158 missing paragraphs share any
concept node with `p1`**, against 118 for entities. A hop it cannot reach is not a ranking problem.

### What happens to the joint arm

The union scores a candidate by summing over all shared nodes of both types, so **EC can never lower
the gold paragraph's own score** — it raises every competitor's more. The five EC traces on the same
questions as the entity table above say it exactly:

| Question | E rank | EC rank | E score | EC score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `5a712beb…e61c` | 5 | **80** | 8.26 | 8.26 (unchanged) |
| `5a718467…e864` | 1 | 1 | 33.01 | 33.01 (unchanged) |
| `5a72944c…c351` | 1 | 1 | 15.65 | 29.23 (two concepts added) |
| `5a72f74a…af59` | 7 | **62** | 8.26 | 8.26 (unchanged) |
| `5a74106b…289e` | 4 | 6 | 8.77 | 8.77 (unchanged) |

In three of five the gold's score does not move at all and its rank collapses, because the candidate
field grew around it. That is the concentration figure in narrative form: **median 34.5 positive
candidates for E, 391.5 for EC — 11.3x**.

## The extraction: what it cost and what it produced

`data/extraction/extraction-0107de3ae9b4e4a3.json`, written by `uv run cer extract`.

| Model / prompt digest | `claude-sonnet-5` / `0107de3ae9b4e4a3` |

| Input tokens / output tokens | 14,850,200 / 2,221,302 |
| **Failures** | **8**, all of kind `bounds` |

**Deviation 5.1 was load-bearing.** The measured cost, 25.98 USD, is **above the original 25 USD
ceiling** the deviation replaced: without the checkpoint decision the phase could not have run at
all. It is also 21.6% below the estimate that motivated the raise, and below even the estimate
without the output margin (27.14 USD). The sample over-predicted in both directions, as the deviation
warned it might: **−1.5% on input tokens and −7.9% on output tokens** against the sample means of
779.4 and 124.7, consistent with the sample's paragraphs being 6.5% longer than the corpus's on
average.

**One precision about the recorded cost.** The 19,346 batch calls priced from the recorded token
totals at batch rates come to **25.9567 USD**; the artifact's `actual_usd` is 25.9848. The difference,
0.028058 USD, is exactly half the sample's recorded standard-rate cost of 0.056116 — so the aggregate
prices the 20 cached sample paragraphs at the batch factor although they were billed synchronously at
full rate. The true out-of-pocket is therefore about **26.013 USD**, 0.028 USD more than the artifact
says. Both figures sit far below the ceiling and the discrepancy changes nothing; it is recorded
because the artifact is the record.

**The 8 failures.** All are `bounds` — an answer with more than 50 items in a list or an item longer
than 120 characters — and the spec forbids repairing them, so those 8 paragraphs carry no node of any
type and are unreachable through any arm. **None of the 158 missing gold paragraphs is among them**
(checked read-only against the index). **One pilot `p1` is**: one of the 152 questions starts its hop
from a paragraph with no nodes and can therefore find nothing in any arm. Two further `p1`s have no
entity node and one has no concept node. Six questions give the entity arm zero positive candidates.

**Two structural facts decide more than the means do.**

1. **Most nodes can never connect anything.** A node in one paragraph cannot be shared. Only
   **16,702 entity nodes (17.3%)** and **12,704 concept nodes (31.1%)** appear in two or more
   paragraphs. The entity namespace is the more fragmented of the two by this measure and still the
   better navigator, because the nodes that *do* repeat are exact names.
2. **At least one paragraph hit the 50-item cap** on entities (`nodes per paragraph — max: 50`), so
   the declared bound bit rather than merely bounding. Eight further answers exceeded the bounds
   outright and were recorded as failures instead of being truncated, as HU-2 requires.

### The synonymy limitation, and the direction it works in

Normalization v1 is NFKC, diacritic stripping, casefold, quote and separator folding, leading article
removal and whitespace collapsing — and **deliberately no plural folding** (D6). What it cannot
resolve stays as two nodes. Counted on the frozen index:

- **448 concept forms** appear together with their own `+s` plural (`award` / `awards`, `armed force`
  / `armed forces`, `alcoholic beverage` / `alcoholic beverages`), and **336 entity forms** do.
- Paraphrase pairs survive intact: `sitcom` **and** `situation comedy`, `tv series` **and**
  `television series`, `movie` **and** `film`, `footballer` **and** `football player` are all
  distinct nodes of the index.
- **1,547 forms exist in both namespaces** — `academy award`, `adaptation`, `air force` — and by the
  spec's own rule (separate namespaces) they are two nodes that never connect.

**This works against the concept arm, and the direction is not a matter of opinion.** A merge that
normalization fails to make can only *remove* a shared node from a pair of paragraphs; it can never
add one. So every unmerged variant is a hop the concept arm might have had and did not. And the loss
falls harder on concepts than on names: a proper name has one canonical spelling that the prompt and
the normalizer reproduce, while a general notion has many equally natural wordings, which is exactly
what the 448 plural pairs and the four paraphrase pairs above show.

**The magnitude is a declared gap, not a filled one.** The counts of unmerged variants are measured;
how many of the 158 missing paragraphs a perfect merge would have recovered is **not**. Resolving it
would need concept embeddings, a synonym table or an ontology — all three ruled out by the spec's
anti-goals for this pilot. What can be said is bounded: even a merge that lost nothing could not lift
the concept arm above its reachability ceiling without *adding* shared nodes, which merging alone
does not do — it redistributes 40,846 concept nodes into fewer, and only the pairs that become shared
by the merge turn into new reach.

## Declared gaps

Written as gaps rather than filled, as the project requires.

1. **No significance test at depth 100, and none against any hop but the three pre-registered ones**
   (plus the reported-only C-vs-BM25). The spec forbids re-running the gate with another metric,
   threshold or comparator. Every depth-100 ordering in this report is descriptive.
2. **The magnitude of the synonymy loss is not measured** — only the count of unmerged variants. See
   §"The synonymy limitation".
3. **The extraction is not reproducible.** Current models reject `temperature`, so re-extracting a
   paragraph may return different nodes. The cached archive is the record, and the artifact carries
   that note itself; everything downstream of it is deterministic.
4. **One pilot `p1` has no nodes** because its extraction failed, so one of the 152 questions is
   unfindable by every arm. It is 0.66% of the population and was not excluded — excluding a question
   after the numbers existed is exactly what HU-1 froze the population against.
5. **No system-level figure exists for this phase**: no Full Support, no context budget, no latency,
   no test split. That is the spec's anti-goal, not an omission — this phase measures one navigation
   step. A comparison at equal budget was Phase 6's, and the gate did not open it.
6. **The entity arm's 40 unreachable paragraphs were not characterized.** It is measured that `p1`
   and the gold share no entity node for 40 of 158; *why* — a bridge through a description rather
   than a name, an extraction that missed the name, a normalization split — is not.

## The scope of the stop

Mirroring how [`4.1_research_line_closure.md`](../phase_4/4.1_research_line_closure.md) §9 scoped the
previous verdict, and for the same reason: a bounded negative result is worth more than a broad one.

### What this pilot establishes

> On the HotpotQA dev bridge failures of this project — 152 questions where dense has read its top-10
> and missed a gold paragraph — a single hop from dense's first paragraph over **concepts extracted
> from the text of each paragraph by an LLM** and normalized deterministically finds the missing
> paragraph at 0.0592 hit@10 and 0.1743 hit@100 per question. That is below every other second hop
> measured in this project, including the pooled-embedding concept hop the previous research line
> already closed as negative. Adding those concepts to a hop over **entities** extracted the same way
> makes the joint hop worse than entities alone, on 29 questions against 1.

And, as a positive finding recorded at the same prominence:

> Entities extracted from the text are the strongest shallow second hop this project has measured:
> 0.6579 hit@10 per question against BM25-on-the-question's 0.4441 (56 wins, 24 losses, 72 ties,
> p = 0.000226), and 18 missing paragraphs placed in the top 10 that neither baseline reaches. The
> advantage is in precision at shallow depth: at depth 100 the arm is behind BM25 on `p1`'s text, and
> its ceiling is that 40 of 158 missing paragraphs share no entity name with `p1` at all.

### Which hypothesis this closes

The proposal's §69 asks whether a corpus can be represented by **interpretable concepts discovered
from the corpus itself** and whether retrieval can expand through them. The first research line
tested one reading of "concept" — dictionary atoms over pooled paragraph embeddings — and closed
negative, explicitly noting that the reading it had not tested was concepts **read from the text**.

**This pilot tested that second reading, and it is the weaker of the two.** The concept arm loses to
the pooled-embedding concept hop it was meant to improve on, at both depths, on the same questions.
So the closure's §7 item 1 — *"concepts extracted from the text — by an LLM, NER or term extraction —
rather than directions found in a pooled embedding"* — is no longer open in the form this pilot
tested it.

### What it does not close

- **The benchmark.** HotpotQA bridge questions are built around an entity named in one paragraph and
  described in another. That an entity hop wins on them is close to a property of the benchmark, and
  the Phase 4 closure said so before this pilot ran. **A corpus whose bridges are thematic rather
  than nominal was never tested**, and this result says nothing about one. Type C questions (§72)
  remain the master plan's declared gap, and no standard benchmark provides them.
- **The hop.** One hop, from one origin, over binary incidence, with one declared scoring form and no
  fitted parameter. Multi-round expansion, a different origin, weighted incidence, a candidate set
  that reserves budget for new paragraphs — none of these were measured, by the spec's anti-goals.
- **The extractor and the prompt.** One model, one frozen prompt, one schema of two node types. The
  spec's own anti-goals excluded relation and attribute nodes (`born in`, `played for`), which is the
  node type most likely to carry a bridge that is neither a name nor a topic.
- **Normalization.** Synonymy that v1 cannot resolve is measured and declared against the concept
  arm. A better merge would raise the concept arm from a floor that is 5 to 11 times below every
  non-concept hop at depth 10, and 3.7 to 4.3 times below at depth 100; it is not plausible on this
  evidence that it would close that gap, but it was not tested.
- **Everything §7 of the closure lists that this pilot did not touch**: a representation finer than
  one pooled embedding per paragraph, a stronger dense model or a reranker as the baseline, and
  response-level metrics.

### What the master plan does with it

`NAMES_ONLY` is not GO. **Phase 6 is not opened**, the founding decisions table is **unchanged** — it
changes only on GO, and only for what a pilot verified — and
[`0_master_plan.md`](../0_master_plan.md) records the stop with the scope above.

```
data/pilot/pilot.json                                  uv run cer pilot

data/nodes/nodes-8e59854118ae5f4d.json|.npz            uv run cer nodes
data/navigation/hop-run.json                           uv run cer navigate
data/navigation/traces.json                            uv run cer navigate
data/navigation/gate-decision.json                     uv run cer navigate
data/diagnostics/second_hop-dev.json                   uv run python scripts/second_hop_diagnostic.py
```

The stages run in the order `pilot → extract --sample → extract → nodes → navigate`, on top of the
Phase 1-3 pipeline (`fetch → build → embed → induce → select`) whose artifacts this phase reads
without touching. `cer navigate` **refuses to run a second time** while `hop-run.json` exists, which
is why the gate cannot be recomputed on a second measurement.

Figures not stored in an artifact, and how they were obtained read-only over the frozen files:

| Figure | How |
|---|---|
| Fragmentation per type | `fragmentation(load_node_index("data/nodes"))` — also printed by `cer nodes` |
| Continuity with the diagnostic | `diagnostic_mismatches(load_hop_run("data/navigation"), <diagnostic>)` → `[]` |
| Reachability ceilings, node coverage of pilot paragraphs, synonymy counts, trace aggregation | Read-only passes over `nodes-*.json|.npz`, `pilot.json` and `traces.json`; no artifact was written or modified |

Nothing in this phase is stochastic (`provenance.stochastic: false`). The one non-deterministic step
is the extraction itself, which is why its output is versioned rather than regenerated.
