## Problem and objective

**Problem.** Every held-out result of this line compares its systems against BGE-small Dense +
BM25 with fixed fusion weights. That control is weaker than what the literature uses cheaply: a
zero-shot cross-encoder, a model that reads the question and one candidate together and
reorders the first-stage list. The line never measured it. The stored rankings say how much room
that leaves (exploratory, gold-informed, computed on 2026-09-30 from the artifacts of Phases 10,
14, 15 and 16):

| Corpus | System | Full Support @2,048, measured | All gold units inside the system's fused top-100, exploratory |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| MultiHop-RAG, 2,255 | P10-B | 587 | 1,160 |
| | P14 | 513 | 1,145 |
| | union of the four top-100 | | 1,298 |
| MuSiQue validation, 2,417 | P10-B | 524 | 900 |
| | P14 | 761 | 1,317 |
| | union of the four top-100 | | 1,410 |
| HotpotQA dev, 7,405 | P10-C | 4,801 | 932 failures with both gold in the fused top-100 (review of 2026-09-29) |

A perfect reorder of what the systems already retrieve would nearly double the result. Two
readings follow, both hypotheses until measured: the order, not the reach, is the bottleneck;
and the Entity Hop's job under a judge is candidate generation. On MuSiQue P14 puts the whole
gold set inside its top-100 for 417 more questions than P10-B; on MultiHop-RAG for 15 fewer.

**Question.**

> With every retriever frozen and nothing fitted, how much of that room does a zero-shot judge
> close on each corpus, at what cost, and does the Entity Hop still add evidence a judge can use?

**Objective.** One pass of two declared judges over the candidates the four systems already
retrieved, on the three corpora; the metrics the literature reports, computed for every system
with and without a judge; and a paper that states what the line established, negative results
included, beside the published figures of other systems. The goal of the phase is a publication.

This phase adds no retriever and changes none. It adds a reordering step on top of stored
rankings, as a declared variant, and it writes.

## Decisions

### D1 — The pool and the reorder rule

- For each question of each set, the pool is the union of the fused top-100 of P10-A, P10-B,
  P10-C and P14. Each judge scores every (question, unit) pair of the pool once.
- **A system under a judge** is that system's own fused top-100, sorted by the judge's score,
  descending, ties broken by the system's original rank. Units beyond the system's top-100 never
  enter: the test is what each system's pool contains, and the judge only reorders it.
- **The union pool under a judge**, the whole pool sorted by score, is reported as a fifth line,
  descriptive: it is the best pool any of the four systems can offer and the natural reference
  for the successor project. It is not a system of this line and enters no preregistered
  comparison.
- HotpotQA dev fused lists are rebuilt from the Phase 10 and Phase 14 component lists with the
  frozen weights, exactly as the D4 integrity check of Phases 15 and 16 does. MuSiQue and
  MultiHop-RAG fused lists are read from the stored `rankings-*.jsonl.gz`.
- The pool's unit texts come from the corpus files of Phases 9, 15 and 16, read-only.

### D2 — Two judges, declared, no selection

- J-light and J-strong are both run on every set and both reported in every table. Neither is
  chosen over the other, and no third judge is tried inside this phase whatever the figures.
- Both are used zero-shot: no fine-tuning, no threshold, no calibration, no prompt beyond the
  pair itself, no fusion of the judge's score with the first-stage score. The judge's order
  replaces the system's order inside the top-100; that is the whole variant.
- Model identity: name, pinned revision, parameter count, maximum sequence length, precision on
  the pod, library versions, recorded in the scoring manifest, as the Phase 9 extractor's
  configuration is.
- Determinism: batch composition does not change scores beyond floating-point noise; the
  reorder breaks ties by original rank, so the same scores always give the same order. The
  scoring of each set is one pass; a rerun writes a distinct file and never overwrites.

### D3 — The sets, the gold and the integrity check

- The three sets and their gold are the ones recorded in Phases 10/14 (HotpotQA dev), 15
  (MuSiQue) and 16 (MultiHop-RAG). Nothing is remapped.
- Before any pair is scored, the code reproduces P10-C at 4,801 / 7,405 and P14 at 5,224 /
  7,405 on HotpotQA dev from the stored lists, and P10-B / P10-C / P14 at 524 / 669 / 761 on
  MuSiQue and 587 / 522 / 513 on MultiHop-RAG from the stored rankings, replayed through the
  same budget packing. A miss stops the phase (`DATA_STOP`).
- HotpotQA dev is the official HotpotQA fullwiki dev set, the split the literature reports on,
  so its figures are the comparable ones. `test-11` is spent and stores no rankings; it is not
  used.
- **Declared in advance:** the weights of P10-C and P14 were fitted on HotpotQA dev in Phases 10
  and 14. Their HotpotQA figures, with or without a judge, are in-sample for those weights; the
  judges themselves are fitted on nothing. MuSiQue and MultiHop-RAG are the frozen transfer.

### D4 — Nothing is fitted, generated or trained

No weight, threshold, depth, prompt or judge is chosen on any of the three sets. No model
generates text anywhere in the phase: no query rewriting, no hypothetical passages, no
decomposition. Nothing is trained or distilled.

### D5 — Preregistered comparisons and descriptive labels

Full Support @2,048 tokens, two-sided exact McNemar on the discordant questions, α = 0.05, per
set and per judge. There is no gate: the phase publishes whatever comes out. The labels below are
written by code so that the result is read the same way on every set.

1. **J(P14) against J(P10-B):** does the hop's pool still add evidence once a judge orders it?
   Label per set and judge: `HOP_ADDS_UNDER_JUDGE` (wins > losses, p < 0.05),
   `HOP_HURTS_UNDER_JUDGE` (losses > wins, p < 0.05), `HOP_NEUTRAL_UNDER_JUDGE` otherwise.
2. **J(P10-B) against P10-B:** what the judge adds to the standard recipe.
3. **J(P14) against P14, J(P10-C) against P10-C, J(P10-A) against P10-A:** the same for each
   system of the line.
4. **J-strong(P10-B) against J-light(P10-B):** the step from the cheap judge to the strong one.

Every comparison states wins, losses, ties, the delta in points and the exact p. Secondary
comparisons decide nothing; the phase has no terminal state other than `DATA_STOP` (D3) or a
cost stop (D8).

### D6 — Comparable metrics, computed for every system with and without a judge

From the stored and the reordered lists, per set:

- **Gold recall @k units**, k in {2, 5, 10, 20, 100}: the share of a question's gold units
  inside the first k units, averaged over questions.
- **Full support @k units**, same k: every gold unit of the question inside the first k. On
  HotpotQA this is the paragraph-pair recall the multi-hop retrievers report.
- **nDCG@10** with binary relevance over gold units, the BEIR metric.
- **Full Support @2,048 tokens** stays the metric of record; the budget curve at 512, 1,024,
  2,048 and 4,096 tokens is reported as in Phases 10-16.
- **The judge ceiling**, as a diagnostic: questions with every gold unit inside the system's
  top-100 and inside the union pool, per set (the table above, recomputed by the stage).

The units of k are corpus units (whole paragraphs), never tokens or fixed-size chunks; the
paper says so wherever a published figure uses another unit.

### D7 — The published figures of other systems

The paper carries, per benchmark, a table of figures published by other work on the same
questions: trained multi-hop retrievers (for example MDR, Baleen), LLM-in-the-loop methods
(for example IRCoT, HippoRAG), the BM25 + cross-encoder zero-shot recipe of BEIR, and the
MultiHop-RAG paper's own pipelines. Each row states the source, the metric as published, the
split, the corpus and its size, and the unit, with a note whenever any of those differs from
ours. A figure that cannot be verified against its paper at writing time is not included. The
table is context for the reader; the paper never presents a published figure as a paired
comparison with ours.

### D8 — Cost, recorded and capped

Pairs scored, wall seconds, throughput, GPU model and attributable USD per judge and per set;
the session total as invoiced. The rented session stops and the author is asked if it reaches
**5 USD**.

### D9 — The paper

- **Form.** A full article in English, about 8 pages plus references, LaTeX source under
  `docs/paper/`. Every number in every table and figure is written by a script from the
  artifacts under `data/`; no figure is typed by hand. The build is reproducible from the
  repository.
- **Content.** The Entity Hop line, Phases 6 to 17, as the spine: the mechanism, its cost, its
  gains at FullWiki scale and on MuSiQue, its regression on news, and what the judges show about
  candidate generation. The concept-space negative result of Phases 2 to 4 in one short section.
  Measured cost throughout. A limitations section that carries at least D3's in-sample note,
  the size of each corpus, the one-hop design against 2-4 hop questions, the LLM-written queries
  of MultiHop-RAG, and the zero-shot judges' training domain.
- **Position.** Untrained, no LLM at query time, cheap: the paper's systems are placed in D7's
  tables under that description, never as competitors of trained or LLM-based systems.
- **Authorship.** The author as sole author, with no affiliation ("independent researcher" where
  a field is mandatory). AI assistance in code, analysis and drafting is disclosed as each venue's
  policy requires.
- **Anonymity.** The public repository names the author. For double-blind venues the submission
  points to an anonymous mirror of the repository, and the paper's text avoids anything that
  identifies it.

### D10 — Where to send it

The phase produces a dated shortlist of four to six venues (conferences, journals or workshops),
each with its submission deadline, page format, review model (blind or open), policy on AI
assistance and on preprints, all verified on the venue's site at writing time. An arXiv preprint
is proposed as the first step. The author chooses; the phase does not submit.

## Data contracts

- **`data/phase17/pool-<set>.jsonl.gz`** and **`pool.json`**: per question, the pool's unit ids
  with the systems that listed each and their ranks; the digests of the source rankings and
  corpora; the sizes.
- **`data/phase17/scores-<judge>-<set>.jsonl.gz`** and **`scoring-<judge>.json`**: one score per
  pair; model pins, precision, versions, truncations, seconds, throughput, GPU, USD.
- **`data/phase17/reordered-<judge>-<set>-<system>.jsonl.gz`**: the reordered top-100.
- **`data/phase17/integrity.json`**, **`outcome.json`**, **`metrics.json`**: D3, D5, D6.
- **`docs/paper/`**: LaTeX source, `tables/` generated, `figures/` generated, `venues.md`,
  and the script that writes them.

Every artifact is JSON, JSONL or NPZ. No pickle. The pools carry unit ids, not texts.

## Restrictions

- **R1.** Rented compute only for the judges' scoring, on RunPod, driven through the API as in
  Phase 16. No paid API for models.
- **R2.** Phase 9-16 artifacts and caches are read-only. Phase 17 writes only under
  `data/phase17/` and `docs/paper/`.
- **R3.** Nothing is fitted, selected, generated or trained on any of the three sets.
- **R4.** The four systems and their rankings are frozen; the judges are used as declared.
- **R5.** The paper contains no number that a script did not write from an artifact.

---

## Anti-objectives

Phase 17 does **not**:

- add or change a retriever, a component, a weight or `α`;
- fuse a judge's score with a first-stage score, calibrate it or threshold it;
- try a third judge, or drop one of the two whatever its figures;
- let a model generate text at query time: no query rewriting, no hypothetical passages, no
  decomposition, no answers;
- train, fine-tune or distil anything;
- open `test-11` or any new corpus;
- report the union pool under a judge as a system of the line;
- present a published figure as a paired comparison with ours;
- build the game prototype (it goes to the successor project);
- submit the paper (the author does).

## Limitations, declared in advance

1. **The judges are zero-shot models trained on English web question answering (MS MARCO).**
   Their behaviour on news, on MuSiQue's composed questions and on HotpotQA is a transfer too.
2. **The pool is bounded at 100.** Gold outside every system's top-100 (about four questions in
   ten on MuSiQue and MultiHop-RAG, exploratory) is unreachable by any reorder; the judge
   results bound what better ordering can give, not what better retrieval could.
3. **HotpotQA dev is in-sample for P10-C's and P14's weights** (D3). The judge figures on it
   are still informative because the judge is fitted on nothing, but the paper says so.
4. **The metric of record packs whole paragraphs into a token budget**; the comparable metrics
   count units. Neither equals a chunk-level metric of a paper that cut the corpus differently.
5. **The cost of a judge is real at query time**: the paper reports it beside every figure, and
   the line's systems are never claimed to match a judge's quality at their cost.
6. **A paper is a claim under review.** Its venue, and whether it is accepted, are outside this
   phase.
