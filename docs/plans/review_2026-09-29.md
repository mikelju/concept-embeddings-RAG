# Review of 2026-09-29: why the gain over Dense + BM25 is small, and what was decided

> An exploratory note, not a phase. Everything measured here is **dev** (the 7,405 HotpotQA
> validation questions), **gold-informed** and **exploratory**: it explains, it selects nothing,
> and no new retriever was run. The scripts and their outputs live under `data/phase14/review/`,
> not versioned, and are identified below by SHA-256. Nothing in `test-10` or `test-11` was
> retrieved on or read (one exception is declared under "Integrity notes"). The author's
> decisions at the end were taken on 2026-09-29 after reading this material.

Every figure is labelled **measured** (read from a recorded artifact or computed by a script
listed below over recorded artifacts), **derived** (arithmetic on measured values),
**literature** (read in a paper on 2026-09-29, with its link) or **interpretation**.

## 2. Where P10-C loses on dev (measured)

Script `anatomy.py`. Inputs: the Phase 10 dev lists (digest `d2e5c2d6…b7e9`), the Phase 9
questions and token counts. Fusion and context filling are the repository's own `fuse_lists` and
`fill_context`. Before anything else the script reproduced the recorded dev counts exactly: Dense
4,125, P10-B 4,536, P10-C 4,801 of 7,405.

**The seed.** P1 (Dense's first paragraph) is a gold paragraph for 5,733 questions (77.4 %).

| Situation | Questions | P10-C supported |
|---|---:|---:|
| P1 is gold | 5,733 | 71.5 % |
| P1 is not gold | 1,672 (22.6 %; 37.3 % of the failures) | 41.9 % |
| both gold in Dense's top 10 | 3,585 | 98.9 % |
| exactly one gold in Dense's top 10 | 3,330 | 36.2 % |
| no gold in Dense's top 10 | 490 | 10.6 % |

**The 2,604 failures split into reach and ordering.**

| Failure kind | Failures | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| a gold paragraph is in none of the three component lists (Dense top 100, BM25 top 100, hop list) | 1,063 (40.8 %) | reach: no reweighting or reordering of the current lists can support them |
| both gold in the union of the three lists, badly placed | 1,541 (59.2 %) | ordering: a perfect reranker of the union would support all of them (ceiling 6,342, 85.6 %) |
| … of which both gold already in the fused top 100 | 932 | a perfect reranker of the fused top 100 would support them (ceiling 5,733, 77.4 %) |

No dev gold pair exceeds 2,048 tokens. In the 932 fused-top-100 failures the better-placed gold
sits at median fused rank 1 and the other at median rank 48, while the 2,048-token context holds
25.2 units on average over all questions and 23.8 in these 932 (gold paragraphs average 99
tokens). The typical failure is one gold found
and the second buried.

**Where the 4,310 gold paragraphs outside Dense's top 10 sit.**

| Category | Gold paragraphs | Share reaching the P10-C context |
|---|---:|---:|
| in no list | 1,111 (25.8 %) | 0 % |
| hop only | 721 (16.7 %) | 26.5 % |
| Dense 11-100 only | 455 (10.6 %) | 14.5 % |
| BM25 only | 454 (10.5 %) | 33.9 % |
| Dense + BM25 | 625 (14.5 %) | 76.3 % |
| Dense + hop | 322 (7.5 %) | 49.4 % |
| BM25 + hop | 230 (5.3 %) | 68.3 % |
| all three | 392 (9.1 %) | 90.1 % |

**The hop and the seed.** Of the gold paragraphs outside Dense's top 10, the hop lists 55.0 %
(first 5: 19.8 %) when P1 is gold and 15.8 % (first 5: 5.1 %) when it is not. A wrong P1 also
makes a reach failure 3.6 times as likely (32.5 % against 9.1 % of questions).

**The hop's score is coarse.** Over the 7,293 non-empty hop lists, the median number of distinct
scores among the first 10 entries is 3; 1,071 of the 7,095 full lists (15.1 %) have their first
10 entries tied at one score, and 537 lists (7.4 %) are flat throughout, so min-max fusion makes
the hop abstain there.

**Question type (proxy).** Questions whose answer is `yes` or `no` (458, a proxy for comparison
questions) are supported at 95.4 % by Dense alone and by P10-C alike. The remaining 6,947
questions are at 62.8 % (Dense 53.1 %) and hold 1,058 of the 1,063 reach failures.

## 3. How far a "fact link" from P1 reaches (measured, yardsticks)

Two reach diagnostics were run over the same 4,310 missed gold paragraphs. Each also computes a
gold-informed ceiling with the Phase 13 construction: the reached gold items are moved to the top
of the hop list with the list's top score, fused with the unchanged Dense and BM25 lists at
0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2, Full Support @2,048 against 4,801.

### 3a. Wikipedia hyperlinks, as a yardstick only

Script `links.py`. The FullWiki source archive, downloaded in deviation 8.1 and the input of the
Phase 9 corpus (`data/phase8_1/source/…withlinks-abstracts.tar.bz2`), carries `text_with_links`
on every record; `corpus/fullwiki.py` keeps only title and sentences,
so no phase has used the links. The archive was streamed once, read-only (620 s, 771 MB peak);
all 5,233,329 records matched their corpus unit id in order; 83.2 % of the 23.4 M links resolve to
a corpus title (the rest are mostly redirects, not followed).

| Group | n | P1 → gold | gold → P1 | either |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all missed gold | 4,310 | 53.7 % | 11.9 % | **62.8 %** |
| in none of the three lists | 1,111 | 44.5 % | 4.1 % | 48.5 % |
| P1 is gold | 2,510 | 87.4 % | 13.1 % | 96.9 % |
| P1 is not gold | 1,800 | 6.8 % | 10.3 % | 15.2 % |

P1's out-degree is median 5 (p90 11, max 56); its in-degree median 1 (p90 37, max 14,181); the
union median 8, against the entity hop's median 5,504 candidates. Ceilings: links alone, either
direction, +1,384; links plus the hop's listed items, +1,421; out-links only, +1,272; in-links
only, +128. The two gold paragraphs of a dev question link first → second in 79.9 % of the
questions, which is how HotpotQA built its bridge questions (Yang et al. 2018, §2, literature:
pairs (a, b) sampled from a graph whose edges are hyperlinks from a's first paragraph).

**The author excluded hyperlinks as a component on 2026-09-29:** Wikipedia is an example corpus,
and the knowledge bases the technique is meant for (engineering documentation, regulations,
client records) carry no such links. The links stay a yardstick for "P1 mentions something the
second paragraph is *about*", nothing more.

### 3b. Title mention, a link-free string rule

Script `titles.py`. "P1's text mentions the gold unit's title" (and the reverse), with titles
normalized (lower case, no diacritics, trailing parenthetical dropped) and whole-word matching.
No link is read.

| Group | n | P1 → gold | gold → P1 | either |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all missed gold | 4,310 | 46.1 % | 12.6 % | **55.6 %** |
| in none of the three lists | 1,111 | 33.4 % | 3.6 % | 36.9 % |
| P1 is gold | 2,510 | 73.6 % | 11.9 % | 82.1 % |
| P1 is not gold | 1,800 | 7.8 % | 13.4 % | 18.5 % |

The table and the ceilings below use **all titles**. 617 items (14.3 %) have a one-word gold
title, the noisy case. Each configuration with its own reach, ceiling and candidate count:

| Configuration | Reach ("either") | Ceiling | Titles P1 mentions per question (units behind them), median / p90 |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| all titles | 55.6 % | +1,187 (P1 → gold only: +1,070) | 47 / 81 (315 / 576) |
| multi-word titles only (one-word, stopword and number titles excluded) | 47.1 % | +993 | 8 / 17 (22 / 60) |

The candidate count is the number of distinct corpus titles found among the word n-grams (up to
6) of P1's text, and the units behind them, per question. Only the multi-word configuration has a
neighbourhood comparable to the hyperlink out-degree (median 5).

**Reading (interpretation).** Most of what the hyperlinks showed survives without them: the
entity hop links a *mention to every other mention* (the GLiNER node "united states" has a
document frequency of 189,579), while what reaches the second gold paragraph is the link from a
mention in P1 to the unit that is *about* that mention. On this corpus that unit is identified by its title. Whether a
corpus has such units is a property to declare, not to assume; see §7.

## 5. Phase 14 on dev (measured, `data/phase14/fit.json`, commit `8a3694b`)

The relevance-ordered hop (candidates scored by `(1 − α) · r̂ + α · ŝ`, the rarity sum and the
cosine to the question each min-max normalized over the question's candidates) passes its dev
gate: α = 0.75 at 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2 gives **5,224 / 7,405** against the bar of 4,835 and P10-C's
4,801, with 520 questions won and 97 lost. Of the gold paragraphs that enter the context in the won
questions, 18 were in Dense's top 10, 260 in Dense's ranks 11-100 and 242 beyond Dense's top 100.
The hop's first-5 count over the 2,651 bridge-sharing gold paragraphs goes from 590 (α = 0) to
1,207 (α = 0.75). The single held-out pass on `test-11` is the Phase 14 branch's to run, on the
author's explicit authorization, and to record in `phase_14/14.results.md`; it had not run when
these diagnostics were computed.

## 6. Why the gain over Dense + BM25 was small (interpretation)

1. **The hop finds the neighbourhood but cannot order it.** It reaches 61.5 % of the missed gold
   through a shared entity (2,651 of 4,310, derived from Phase 13's screen population) and lists
   1,665 of them within its depth-100 list, at median position 11, among thousands of
   candidates; the rarity score carries no information about which entity is the bridge or which candidate
   answers the question. Phase 14 is the first change that puts such information into the score,
   and it is the first to pass a dev gate in four attempts.
2. **The benchmark's bridge is "about-ness", not co-mention.** The second gold paragraph is the
   one *about* something P1 names (§3). A raw entity form shared by thousands of paragraphs is a
   noisy proxy for that.
3. **A wrong P1 has no remedy.** 22.6 % of questions, 37.3 % of failures, and the hop's reach
   collapses there.
4. **The easy questions are already solved.** The yes/no proxy group is at 95 % with Dense alone;
   the remaining difficulty is concentrated in the other 6,947 questions, mostly bridge questions
   whose second paragraph is related to the question by a fact, not by wording, where BM25
   already covers the cases that name the bridge.
5. **The fusion reads the hop badly.** Coarse, tied scores leave the order to a unit-id hash and
   make 7.4 % of the lists abstain; a continuous score (Phase 14) removes this by construction.

## 7. What each lever needs from the corpus (interpretation)

The author's target corpora are engineering documentation, safety and labour regulations,
client profiles and database-held records, not Wikipedia. Each lever depends on a corpus
property that must be declared.

| Lever | Needs from the corpus | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| second-hop query reformulation (vector of question + P1 text against Dense, untrained) | nothing | general; the closest to the original iterative-retrieval hypothesis; the only lever that reaches context sharing no name with P1 |
| zero-shot cross-encoder over the fused top 100, scoring (question + P1, candidate) | nothing | general; precision, not reach; a model in the online loop, to be declared, applied to every system alike |
| ordering structural candidates by the question (Phase 14) | some structural candidate generator | general as a principle |
| stronger or domain-adapted Dense | nothing | general |
| multiple seeds | nothing | general; cost depends on the hop's neighbourhood size |
| the current entity hop (mention to mention) | an extractor of names or domain terms | general, weak in ordering, hub-prone |
| hop to the unit *about* the entity (title, glossary entry, record, first-sentence subject) | definitional units | conditional; where absent it adds nothing (an empty list contributes nothing in the fusion) and a gain must be reported as corpus-dependent |
| the corpus's own reference structure (links, "see article 12", document ids, keys) | explicit references | conditional and common in technical corpora; Wikipedia hyperlinks are one instance, excluded here |
| canonicalization | aliases and abbreviations of the domain | conditional; matters more in enterprise documentation than in Wikipedia, and by glossary rather than by string rule |

## 9. Integrity notes and limitations

- **Dev is heavily reused.** The 7,405 validation questions have been fitted by Phases 10-14 and
  looked at with gold-informed ceilings here and in Phase 13. They remain valid for selection,
  not as held-out evidence.
- **Ceilings are not systems.** They read the gold to build the hop list and bound what a signal
  could add; no candidate retriever was run on dev by this review.
- **String rules.** The title diagnostic is whole-word matching on normalized text; it can match a
  substring of a longer name and merges titles that differ only by their parenthetical. Raw
  titles are unique in the corpus (5,233,329 distinct), but the rule matches normalized titles,
  of which there are 4,788,513 for 5,233,329 units, so some units share a normalized title.
- **The reach through links or titles is partly a benchmark property.** HotpotQA built its bridge
  questions from first-paragraph hyperlinks (§4), so 79.9 % of dev gold pairs are linked by
  construction. That is one reason the author excluded links as a component and asked for a
  cross-benchmark validation.
- **One rule slip, declared.** While preparing `anatomy.py`, the agent that ran it printed
  `data/phase10/reproduction.json` and `data/phase10/outcome.json`. The latter holds aggregate
  `test-10` figures (the McNemar record, success counts and latencies), all already published in
  `phase_10/10.results.md`. Nothing from either file
  was used, and nothing was fitted or selected on it. `test-11` was never touched.
- **The diagnostics were run by delegated agents** under written rules (read-only, dev only,
  reproduce the recorded counts first, scratchpad only). Each report was checked against its
  output file. The scripts record no code commit; the repository was at `8a3694b` with the
  other session's uncommitted Phase 14 work in `cli.py` and its test, which none of the scripts
  import.
- **Nothing in the repository changed** because of the diagnostics. The archive was streamed
  read-only.
