The answer to the phase question: **yes.** Scoring the Entity Hop's candidates by a mix of their
rarity overlap with P1 and their BGE similarity to the question retrieves the full two-paragraph
evidence more often than P10-C at FullWiki scale. At the primary budget it gains on 317 questions
for every 72 it loses. **P14 is the line's new best system.**

## D9 — latency, reported, not gated

`Retriever.retrieve` wall time per question, question vector served from its cache; Dense scoring,
BM25, the hop and fusion, excluding question encoding; all four systems in one process, one after
another, on the laptop. For P14, `sim` is the one `candidate_similarity` call per question
(gathering the `C(q)` rows and their dot products), timed inside `retrieve`.

| System | Mean | Median | p95 | Max | Label |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| P10-A Dense | 154.3 ms | 153.5 ms | 174.3 ms | 4,408.3 ms | measured |
| P10-B Dense + BM25 | 194.2 ms | 193.6 ms | 222.3 ms | 290.8 ms | measured |
| P10-C Dense + BM25 + Entity Hop | 215.4 ms | 210.9 ms | 249.5 ms | 3,658.4 ms | measured |
| P14 `retrieve` without `sim` | 365.6 ms | 393.7 ms | 515.8 ms | 1,248.7 ms | measured |
| P14 `sim` only | 25.7 ms | 4.0 ms | 123.4 ms | 510.6 ms | measured |
| **P14 total (`latency_ms`)** | **391.3 ms** | 407.8 ms | 592.6 ms | 1,313.6 ms | measured |

Mean ratios (written by code): P14 / P10-C **1.82** with `sim` (1.70 without); P14 / P10-B **2.02**
(1.88 without). The P10-A and P10-C maxima are single slow questions, as recorded. P14 has no such
outlier (max 1.3 s), but its p95 is 2.4 times P10-C's (derived).

**Interpretation, not a decomposition.** `sim` itself is small (a mean of 25.7 ms, 128.5 s over the
pass). Most of P14's extra 176 ms over P10-C (derived) is outside `sim`, in the hop's work over the
whole candidate set: the relevance-ordered hop keeps, normalizes and orders all of `C(q)` (up to
hundreds of thousands of rows), where the Phase 9 hop only partitions its top 100. That breakdown
was not timed separately. The absolute figures are not comparable with Phase 10's pass (P10-C
446 ms mean on `test-10`): a different pass, process and code revision.

## Limitations

Declared in the spec in advance (1-5), then found in the run (6-8).

1. **Dev has been fitted by Phases 10-13 and looked at again with oracle ceilings.** The 7,405
   validation questions remain valid for selection, not as held-out evidence. The ceilings chose
   this phase's form (the pure tie-break excluded, the candidate side chosen) but measured no
   similarity ordering, so no `α` was seen on dev before the fit.
2. **`sim` is Dense's own signal.** Part of the gain may re-weight what Dense already ranks: on dev
   260 of the 520 won paragraphs were in Dense's ranks 11-100. D8 reports it and cannot separate
   the causes.
3. **Train questions, a fine-tuned encoder.** BGE-small saw HotpotQA train during fine-tuning, and
   `test-11` comes from train. The new term uses the same vectors, so `sim` may be optimistic on
   `test-11` in the way Dense is; P10-C shares that bias through its Dense component. Dense reads
   `test-11` at 57.04 %, 1.33 points above its dev 55.71 % (derived), inside Phase 10's probe
   bound (+2.89 pp, 5-point margin). Absolute figures are not comparable with validation-set
   figures in the literature.
4. **The ceilings are loose.** +1,257 places every reachable gold paragraph at the top of the hop
   list with the list's top score. P14's +423 on dev is a third of it; no real signal was expected
   to approach it.
5. **The normalization is per question and over all candidates.** One extreme candidate stretches
   a term's range and compresses the rest; the `α` grid absorbs part of this, not all.
6. **The D8 split on `test-11` is bounded, not the one specified** (deviation 14.1): the pass kept
   metrics, not rankings.
7. **Latency is one host, one run, one brute-force CPU implementation.** The extra time outside
   `sim` is attributed to the full-candidate-set work by reading the code, not by a timing.
8. **One configuration, one benchmark.** P14 was selected on HotpotQA dev and tested on HotpotQA
   train questions. Whether the gain holds on another multi-hop benchmark (roadmap 7.1) is not
   measured.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Established (measured, held-out):**

- Ordering the Entity Hop's candidates by a mix of rarity and question similarity
  (`α = 0.75`) beats P10-C by **+4.90 pp** Full Support @2,048 on 5,000 held-out train questions
  (317 wins, 72 losses, p = 9.0e-38), and Dense + BM25 by +9.02 pp.
- The query-blind hop's **order** was a bottleneck, with its reach unchanged: the same candidate
  set, the same seed, the same fusion weights, only the hop's scores changed. On dev the hop's first-5 bridge
  count doubles (590 → 1,207).
- Phases 12 and 13 used the question to **remove** candidates or seeds and lost; Phase 14 uses it
  to **reorder** and wins. That is consistent with Phase 13's hypothesis that the hard filter, not
  the signal, was what failed (interpretation).
- P10-C's own gain over P10-B replicates on a second held-out draw (+4.12 pp).

**Not established:**

- how much of the gain is re-weighting Dense's own ranking rather than new evidence (dev: about
  half the won paragraphs were in Dense's 11-100, half beyond its 100; `test-11`: not separable);
- whether it holds beside a stronger Dense, on another benchmark or outside Wikipedia;
- whether a cheaper implementation keeps the gain at P10-C's latency (P14 is 1.82× P10-C's mean);
- anything about reach: the candidate set is the Phase 9 one, and 40.8 % of P10-C's dev failures
  are out of reach of all three lists (review of 2026-09-29).
