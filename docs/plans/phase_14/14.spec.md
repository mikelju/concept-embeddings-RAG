## Step 0 — Reach ceilings and the cost of canonicalization (done, 2026-09-28)

Before anything else in this phase, the author asked whether widening the hop's **reach** by
canonicalizing entities (roadmap 8.1) is worth more than **ordering** what it already reaches.
Step 0 decides nothing by itself. It ends in an author decision.

**Ceilings** (measured, exploratory, dev, gold-informed; `phase_13/13.results.md`, "Reach
ceilings"). There is no ground truth for "same entity", so string rules stand in for a
canonicalizer: **strict** (one form is a full word sequence of the other) and **lenient** (the
forms share a content word). A real canonicalizer should fall between the two. The ceilings sit
on top of the perfect-ordering ceiling C4 (+1,257):

| | Newly reachable gold paragraphs (of 1,659 that share no node with P1) | Extra Full Support on dev over C4 |
|---|---:|---:|
| strict | 305 | +157 |
| lenient | 620 | +282 |
| P1 names the gold's article title (a *linking* proxy) | 270 | +167 |

At the ceiling, ordering what the hop reaches is worth 4-8 times what widening its reach adds.
Widening also multiplies the candidate sets: the median set grows from 5,168 to 23,152 (strict)
or 166,613 (lenient) on a 500-question dev sample (measured).

**Cost of canonicalization.** These are **projections**, not measurements. The global sizes
below are measured: 6,092,750 entity nodes, 14,999,300 strict pairs and at most 27.9 billion
lenient pairs.

| Route | LLM? | Where | Time | Money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. String rule (strict), applied offline or at query time | no | laptop | minutes; the pair count took under 5 min, and Phase 9 built the entity index in 282 s | 0 USD |
| B. BGE vectors of the 6.09 M forms, then cosine over the 15.0 M strict pairs, threshold fitted on dev | no | rented GPU for the encoding | ~26 h on the laptop at Phase 13's 64.7 texts/s, or under the 38 min Phase 9 took for 5.2 M paragraphs on the GPU | under 0.5 USD attributable (Phase 9: 0.47 USD for its encoding), plus pod overhead |
| C. LLM judges the 15.0 M strict pairs, forms only | yes | Batch API | hours to a day of batch processing | about 410 USD with Claude Haiku 4.5, 825 USD with Claude Sonnet 5 |
| D. As C, with one sentence of context per mention | yes | Batch API | same | about 1,200 USD with Haiku 4.5, 2,400 USD with Sonnet 5 |
| E. LLM over the lenient pairs | yes | — | — | infeasible without blocking: on the order of 10^5-10^6 USD |

- **Assumptions behind C-D.**
  - Batches of 100 pairs per request, with about 25 input and 6 output tokens per pair for forms
    only, and about 130 input tokens per pair with context.
  - Batch API prices, half the list price: Haiku 4.5 at 0.50 / 2.50 USD per million input /
    output tokens, Sonnet 5 at 1 / 5 USD.
  - A pilot of about 1,000 pairs, costing cents, would measure the real token counts.
- **An LLM is not required to test canonicalization.** Route A tests the idea at no cost. An LLM
  mainly adds aliases the string rule misses ("JFK") and rejects false merges ("Paris" with
  "Paris Hilton"). Its value cannot be known before route A is measured.
- **Restricting the LLM to the P1s of the evaluation sets is not a valid system**, because it
  would depend on the benchmark. An LLM at query time would be a declared variant.
- **Paid API.** Routes C-E would need a paid API and routes B-D a rented GPU. The rest of this
  spec excludes both (R1). Either would be the author's decision in its own spec.

**Decision (the author's, 2026-09-28).** Candidate-side ordering first: this spec runs as
written. **Canonicalization follows as Phase 15**, once Phase 14 closes, starting with route A
(the strict string rule, on the laptop, no LLM). Phase 15 gets its own spec; nothing of it runs
inside Phase 14.

## Problem and objective

**Problem.** P10-C (Dense + BM25 + the frozen Phase 9 Entity Hop, 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2) is the line's
best system: 66.50 % Full Support @2,048 on `test-10`, 4,801 / 7,405 on dev. Phases 11, 12 and 13
tried to improve the hop **on the seed side**, choosing which of P1's entities to follow, and all
three stopped at their dev gate.

The hop reaches every paragraph that shares an entity with P1 (median 5,504 candidates per dev
question, max 395,922). It orders them only by the rarity of the shared entities. Ties are broken
by unit id, an arbitrary hash, and the hop never looks at the candidate itself. Phase 11 measured
that 1,290 of the 1,665 gold paragraphs the hop lists are tied with other candidates.

The Phase 13 ceilings (`phase_13/13.results.md`, "Ceilings"; exploratory, dev, gold-informed)
bound each lever by replacing the hop list inside P10-C at its frozen weights:

| Ceiling | Full Support @2,048 on dev vs P10-C's 4,801 |
|---|---:|
| perfect seed choice | +119 (219 won, 100 lost) |
| perfect soft weight on the bridge entities | +639 (641 won, 2 lost) |
| perfect tie-break | **+0** |
| perfect reorder of the hop's candidate set | **+1,257** |

Two findings shape this phase:

- **The candidate side has the most room.** Putting the reachable evidence at the top of the hop
  list is worth up to +1,257 questions on dev. This is an upper bound built from the gold, not an
  estimate.
- **A pure tie-break is worth exactly zero here, by construction.** The fusion min-max normalizes
  the hop list and gives an absent unit 0. A tie-break that leaves scores unchanged only decides
  which members of the tie group crossing the cut enter the list, and that group holds the
  list's lowest score, which normalizes to 0. Any question signal must therefore change the
  **score** the fusion reads, not only the order within ties.

**Question.**

> Does ordering the Entity Hop's candidates by a score that mixes their rarity overlap with P1
> and their BGE similarity to the question retrieve the full multi-hop evidence more often than
> P10-C at FullWiki scale?

This is roadmap 7.2's original extension, `relevance(candidate, Q)`, which specs 12 and 13
excluded.

**Objective.** One candidate family, fitted on dev. The single configuration dev selects is
measured once on `test-11`:

- against **P10-C**, which decides the label;
- against **P10-B** (Dense + BM25), with its own preregistered test, because it is the reference
  the community understands.

## Decisions settled before implementation

### D1 — Candidate similarity

- `sim(c) = cos(BGE(q), BGE(c)) = v_q · v_c`, the dot product of two L2-normalized vectors.
- `v_c` is the candidate's row in the Phase 9 passage cache; `v_q` is the question vector Dense
  already uses:
  - dev: the Phase 9 question cache, key `4894aceb0406c63a`;
  - `test-11`: the Phase 11 question cache, key `3ab80134c058052d`.
- **Query vector: the question alone.** Nothing is encoded. A query built from the question and
  P1 (MDR style) is a different question and is left to the roadmap.
- One function computes `sim` in float32 from the gathered candidate rows, on dev and on
  `test-11` alike. The pass does not reuse Dense's own score vector, so that dev and held-out
  scores come from the same arithmetic.
- **Integrity check (a gate).** For every dev question, the recomputed `sim(P1)` must equal the
  score Dense recorded for P1 in the Phase 10 dev lists within 1e-5. A miss means the wrong vectors
  or question vectors: the phase stops and goes to the author.
- **Candidate set: all of them, no cap.** Every candidate the Phase 9 hop reaches is scored.

### D2 — The relevance-ordered hop

For a question `q`, let `C(q)` be the Phase 9 hop's candidates before its depth cut, `r(c)` the
Phase 9 rarity score (the sum of rarity weights over the entity nodes `c` shares with P1) and
`sim(c)` from D1.

- **Scale.** Each term is min-max normalized over `C(q)`:
  - `r̂(c) = (r(c) − min r) / (max r − min r)`;
  - `ŝ(c) = (sim(c) − min sim) / (max sim − min sim)`;
  - a term whose maximum equals its minimum over `C(q)` is 0 for every candidate.
- **Score.** `score_α(c) = (1 − α) · r̂(c) + α · ŝ(c)`, with `α ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}`.
- **List.** `C(q)` ranked by `score_α` descending, then unit id ascending, cut at depth 100. Each
  listed unit carries `score_α` as the score the fusion reads.
- **`α = 0` is the Phase 9 hop, exactly.** At `α = 0` the stage returns the unchanged Phase 9
  hop list and its rarity scores, not the normalized `r̂`. This keeps P10-C's third component
  bit for bit. The min-max fusion is invariant to that affine rescaling in exact arithmetic, but
  not necessarily in floating point.
- **`α = 1`** orders `C(q)` by similarity alone, with the entities acting only as a filter: Dense
  restricted to P1's entity neighbourhood, outside `read(q)`.
- **Unchanged:** P1, `read(q)`, the candidate set, depth 100, no padding. The relevance order can
  lift a candidate from below Phase 9's cut into the list, and changes its fused score.
- **The pure tie-break is not a grid point.** The ceilings show that it cannot move a question
  under this fusion (see the problem).

### D3 — Laptop reproduction gate

Before the fit, the new code at `α = 0`, `0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2` must reproduce P10-C's dev count
**exactly: 4,801 / 7,405**, with 0 differing questions against the Phase 10 dev lists, and a hop
list identical to Phase 10's `entity-hop` list for every dev question. A miss stops the phase and
goes to the author.

### D4 — The candidate family and the dev fit

P14 is `weighted` fusion with min-max normalization per component, the scheme P10-C used, over
three components in fixed order: Dense, BM25, the relevance-ordered hop at `α`.

- **Grid:** 5 values of `α` × the 66 weight triples of multiples of 0.1 that sum to 1: **330
  configurations**.
- **The grid contains P10-C exactly:** `α = 0`, `0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2`.
- **Objective:** Full Support @2,048 over the 7,405 dev questions.
- **No screen.** The reproduction gate is followed directly by the grid.
- **Ties** are broken by:
  1. gold recall @2,048;
  2. the smaller `α`, the one closest to P10-C;
  3. the larger `w_dense`;
  4. the larger `w_bm25`.

### D5 — Dev gate

The selected configuration must reach **at least 4,835 / 7,405** dev Full Support @2,048, that is
P10-C's 4,801 plus 34 questions.

The margin rescales Phase 11's 37 questions for 1,430 configurations to this grid, as in Phases
12 and 13, since the expected selection gain of the best of K noisy configurations grows as
√(ln K):

`37 × √(ln 330 / ln 1,430) = 33.06`, rounded up to 34.

Below the bar, the phase stops with `DEV_STOP` and `test-11` stays unopened.

### D6 — Primary endpoint and label

Full Support @2,048 on `test-11`, P14 against P10-C: one two-sided exact McNemar test (binomial on
the discordant questions, p = 1/2), α = 0.05.

```text
CANDIDATE_RELEVANCE_SUPPORTED      wins > losses and p < 0.05
CANDIDATE_RELEVANCE_REGRESSION     losses > wins and p < 0.05
CANDIDATE_RELEVANCE_NOT_SUPPORTED  otherwise
DEV_STOP                           D5
```

The label is written by code. Nothing else can change it. `test-11` is opened in a single pass,
only after the author authorizes it explicitly.

### D7 — Secondary comparison, preregistered

P14 against P10-B on the same questions and metric, with the same exact McNemar test, α = 0.05.
It is reported with wins, losses, delta and p, and it **cannot change the label**. The report also
gives P10-C against P10-B on `test-11` (descriptive): these are the first `test-11` figures of
either system.

### D8 — Overlap with Dense, reported, deciding nothing

`sim(c)` is Dense's own score. On dev, 1,154 of the 2,651 bridge-sharing gold paragraphs are
already in Dense's ranks 11-100, where the fusion gives them Dense weight. Roadmap 7.2 asks
whether the hop's gain comes from **not** asking Dense's question twice. The phase keeps those
candidates in the hop and reports, descriptively:

- **the dev curve by `α`:** the best dev Full Support at each `α`, over its 66 weight triples;
- **the hop list by `α`:** at each `α`, how many of the 2,651 bridge-sharing gold paragraphs the
  hop lists in its first 5 and in its depth-100 list, against Phase 9's 590 / 1,665;
- **where the gains come from**, on dev for the selected configuration and on `test-11` for P14
  against P10-C. For every question won and every question lost, each gold paragraph whose
  coverage within the 2,048-token budget changed is classified by its Dense rank: 1-10, 11-100,
  or beyond 100.

### D9 — Latency, reported, not gated

Each system's `retrieve` wall time per question is measured in the same pass on the laptop, as in
Phase 10:

- it covers Dense scoring, BM25, the hop and fusion, and excludes the BGE question encoding;
- for P14, computing `sim` over `C(q)` is new query-time work: it is **timed separately**, and
  reported beside the `retrieve` figure and summed with it;
- the report gives mean, median, p95, max and the ratios P14 / P10-C and P14 / P10-B.

## User stories

### HU-1 — The relevance-ordered hop lists on dev

**As** the author, **I want** the hop list of every dev question at every `α`, built once from
fixed inputs, **so that** the fit reads fixed lists.

- [ ] P1, `read(q)` and the Dense and BM25 lists come from the Phase 10 dev lists, digest-checked,
      not re-run.
- [ ] The Phase 9 passage vectors are verified against the digest `embedding.json` records before
      use.
- [ ] The D1 integrity check passes for every dev question, or the phase stops.
- [ ] Candidate-set sizes per question are recorded (median, p90, max), with seconds, peak memory
      and host.

**Projection, not a measurement.**
- **Candidate pairs.** The dev candidate sets hold 204,990,886 (question, candidate) pairs, a mean
  of 27,683 per question.
- **Scoring time.** Gathering those rows and taking their dot products is memory-bound. The
  planning figure is 10-30 min.
- **Hop time.** Phase 13 built its hop lists in 741 s, giving a planning figure for dev lists of
  about 20-45 min in all.
- **Memory.** Dense's cache is a compressed NPZ, so it cannot be memory-mapped. It loads whole:
  8.04 GB of float32, as it did for Phase 10 on this laptop. With the entity index, the planning
  peak is about 16 GB of 31.6.

## Restrictions

- **R1.** No rented compute and no paid API. Everything runs on the laptop.
- **R2.** Phase 9-13 artifacts, dev lists and caches are read-only. Phase 14 writes only under
  `data/phase14/`.
- **R3.** Nothing is fitted or selected on `probe`, `test-10` or `test-11`. `test-10` is spent: it
  is neither retrieved on nor read again, not even descriptively.
- **R4.** P10-B and P10-C keep their frozen weights. The hop keeps P1, `read(q)`, its candidate set,
  its rarity weight and depth 100.
- **R5.** Similarity uses the Phase 9 Dense vectors and model. Any other encoder is a different
  phase.

---

## Anti-objectives

Phase 14 does **not**:

- encode anything: no new passage, question, window or `question + P1` vector;
- change the candidate set: no cap, no DF cap, no new seeds, no seed filter or seed weighting;
- use the Phase 13 window signal, which stays a candidate for a later phase (soft seed weighting,
  ceiling +639 on dev);
- exclude Dense's top 100 from the hop, or add any other overlap rule to the grid;
- add relations, canonicalization, entity linking, a second hop or graph propagation;
- run GLiNER, an LLM or a cross-encoder at query time;
- refit P10-B or P10-C, or try a second candidate family after a `DEV_STOP`.

## Limitations, declared in advance

1. **Dev has been fitted by Phases 10-13, and looked at again with oracle ceilings.** The 7,405
   validation questions remain valid for selection, not as held-out evidence. The ceilings read
   the gold to choose this phase's **form**: they excluded the pure tie-break and pointed to the
   candidate side. They did not measure any similarity ordering, so no `α` was seen on dev before
   the fit.
2. **`sim` is Dense's own signal.** A gain may partly re-weight what Dense already ranks; D8
   reports how much, descriptively, and cannot separate the two causally.
3. **Train questions, a fine-tuned encoder.** BGE-small saw HotpotQA train during fine-tuning,
   and `test-11` comes from train. Phase 10's probe bounded the effect on Dense (+2.89 pp, within
   its 5-point margin). The new term uses the same vectors, so `sim` may be optimistic on
   `test-11` in the same way. P10-C, the reference, shares that bias through its Dense component.
4. **The ceilings are loose.** +1,257 places every reachable gold paragraph at the top of the
   hop list with the list's top score. No real signal is expected to approach it.
5. **The normalization is per question and over all candidates.** A single extreme candidate
   stretches a term's range and compresses the rest; the grid over `α` absorbs part of this, not
   all.
