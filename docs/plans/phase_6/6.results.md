## The question this phase asked

> **Can an entity-navigation second hop replace the BM25 component of the existing dense+BM25
> system without reducing retrieval quality?**

The question is a **replacement** question, and it was framed that way before any measurement: the
decision procedure is a non-inferiority test of B against A at a preregistered margin, guarded by
a sign test of B against dense and an assay-sensitivity check of A against dense. It was never a
superiority test, and §"Reported beside the decision" below keeps the superiority reading where it
belongs — outside the decision.

### In plain words

- **B improves on dense.** It supports 103 questions dense does not and loses 12 that dense
  supports; the sign test passes overwhelmingly.
- **A also improves on dense**, 85 against 30. This is the assay-sensitivity check: it proves the
  experiment is capable of detecting a real second-component effect on this population, so a pass
  for B is not an artefact of a measurement too blunt to see anything.
- **B is not inferior to A** by more than the preregistered margin. The lower limit of the
  difference sits above −δ, so the hypothesis "B is worse than A by more than half of what BM25
  buys over dense" is rejected.
- **Therefore the Entity Hop satisfies the Phase 6 replacement criterion for BM25**, under this
  protocol, this corpus and this population. That sentence is the state the artifact records; it is
  not read off any single aggregate.

## Diagnosis of the mechanism

The bridge-like subgroup — questions with at least one gold paragraph outside `read(q)`, the first
10 units of `D(q)` — is computed by the frozen rule and **decides nothing**
(`readout-test.json` → `subgroup`):

| Split | Subgroup size | Full Support: dense | A | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dev (600) | **152** — exactly the Phase 5 pilot question set (`pilot_comparison.equal: true`, both difference lists empty) | 39 | 75 | 82 |
| test (1,400) | **321** | 76 | 140 | **176** |

On the subgroup the ordering is the same as overall and the spread is wider: B supports 176 of 321
where dense supports 76.

Where B's wins come from, computed read-only over `traces-test.json` and the three
`outcomes-*-test.json` (see §"Provenance and reproducibility" — these four numbers are not stored
in an artifact):

- **Of the 103 questions B supports and dense does not, all 103** have at least one unit of origin
  `entity` in B's 2,048-token context — a paragraph the entity hop proposed, not one dense had
  already placed in that context.
- **67 of those 103** depend on entity-introduced units *only*: they carry no `dense-reorder` unit
  at all, so the win cannot be attributed to reordering dense's own list.
- **85 of the 96** questions B supports and A does not carry at least one `entity` unit.
- Of B's 1,246 supported questions overall, 879 carry at least one.
- `traces-test.json` holds 3,657 traces over 976 questions: **3,328 of origin `entity`** and 329
  `dense-reorder`.

Against that, the units B brings in from outside dense's top-100 list are *fewer* than A's
(`diagnostics-test.json` → `aggregates`): B **1.52** on average (max 14), A **2.82** (max 15). B
therefore wins more while disturbing the dense list less, which is what a hop conditioned on
dense's own first paragraph should look like, and is the opposite of what a system winning by
sheer breadth would look like.

**This supports the reading that the gain comes from the entity hop itself rather than from merely
reordering paragraphs dense had already retrieved.** It is a mechanism reading, not a second
decision.

Other diagnostics of the hop on test (`diagnostics-test.json` → `aggregates`, 1,400 questions,
scheme `weighted`, budget 2,048): candidate list size mean 48.9 / median 33 / max 100, with **486**
questions reaching the depth limit of 100; positives mean 191.3 / median 33 / max 2,245; **96**
expansions empty; **72** with a single candidate; **338** where the candidate scores are flat, so
the weighted scheme abstains; bottom tier mean 31.9; overlap with dense's ranks 11–100 mean 5.03
(max 64); **3** questions whose `p1` has no entity node and **0** whose `p1` is among the eight
failed extractions.

rule: budget 2,048, four groups, first 5 by ascending qid): "B succeeds where A fails" (96

## Limitations and scope

1. **One benchmark.** HotpotQA distractor, the frozen 19,366-unit pool of Phase 1. Nothing here
   transfers to another corpus without measurement.
2. **A multi-hop / bridge diagnostic.** The population rewards exactly the move the entity hop
   makes: reaching a second supporting paragraph linked to the first by a shared name. The
   bridge-like subgroup is 321 of 1,400 test questions, and it is where the spread is widest.
3. **Raw Phase 5 entities, with no canonicalization.** The node index is the frozen one: 96,416
   entity nodes, 82.7% of entity forms occurring in a single paragraph. Aliases and naming variants
   are *not* merged. The result is what raw extracted names buy; it is not evidence about what
   canonicalization would add, in either direction.
4. **This does not demonstrate the Type C claim** of the proposal — satellite knowledge not
   explicit in the query. That needs a different evaluation population, as
   [`0_master_plan.md`](../0_master_plan.md) already records.
5. **This does not validate the general concept-embeddings architecture.** What is measured is a
   second-stage candidate generator over entity nodes read from the text, fused with dense.
6. **The result is specific to the frozen protocol**: this metric (Full Support), this budget
   (2,048), this margin (δ = 55/2800), this α, this method and this population. A different choice
   of any of them is a different experiment, and by the rules of this phase cannot be run on the
   same test split.
7. **Scope deliberately excluded**, unchanged from the spec: concept nodes, canonicalization,
   relation/attribute nodes, multi-round expansion, and a three-way `Dense + BM25 + Entity`
   combination.

## Relation to the earlier phases

- **Phase 4 closed the first research line with a negative, bounded result**: concepts *induced
  from pooled paragraph embeddings* did not help — the diffusion walk returned dense's own top-100
  reordered. That verdict stands exactly as
  [`4.1_research_line_closure.md`](../phase_4/4.1_research_line_closure.md) scopes it, and it is
  about a **different representation** from the one measured here. Phase 6 neither reopens nor
  contradicts it, and this positive result must not be read as softening it.
- **Phase 5** extracted entities and concepts from the *text* and measured one-hop navigation on
  the 152-question bridge diagnostic. Its gate returned **NAMES ONLY**: entities are a strong
  second-hop signal (0.6579 hit@10 against 0.4441 for the frozen comparator), text-derived concepts
  are the weakest hop the project has measured. Phase 5 explicitly did **not** establish an
  end-to-end improvement.
- **Phase 6 closes that gap for the entity signal only**: the Phase 5 signal integrates end to end,
  inside a complete retrieval system, measured at equal token budget against the real dense+BM25
  control, and it replaces the BM25 component under the frozen protocol without loss of quality.
  Concepts — of either kind — are not part of this result.

the record. Every one of them carries a sha256 digest that its loader verifies, and every test
artifact carries `freeze_digest = d03ccfe4…`, binding it to the freeze committed in `d320729`.

## Conclusion

**Phase 6 is complete, and its recorded state is `ENTITY_REPLACEMENT_SUPPORTED`
(`data/replacement/decision.json` → `state`, route `null`, no anomalies, no open question).** Under
one protocol frozen and committed before the test split was opened, and read exactly once, a second
hop over entity names extracted from the corpus text replaces BM25 in the dense hybrid without
reducing retrieval quality: B improves on dense (103 wins to 12, p = 1.676e-19), the control A also
improves on dense so the experiment is demonstrably sensitive (85 to 30, p = 1.430e-07), and B is
non-inferior to A at the preregistered margin δ = 55/2800 (b = 96, c = 60, lower limit +0.011135 >
−0.019643, p = 3.780e-07). That B also *exceeds* A by 2.57 points of Full Support is recorded as
descriptive and decides nothing: the phase asked whether the entity hop could take BM25's place, and
the answer recorded in the artifact is that it can — on HotpotQA, with the raw uncanonicalized
entities of Phase 5, at roughly seven times A's retrieval latency. Nothing here establishes the
Type C claim, validates the wider concept-embeddings architecture, or reopens the negative result
Phase 4 recorded for concepts induced from pooled embeddings, which concerned a different
representation. What comes next is the author's to decide.
