## Why it did not help

**Derived from the design, not a new measurement.** Under D2, a candidate's score is the sum of
rarity weights over *all* the entities it shares with P1, whatever `m` is; the seeds only decide
which candidates are admitted. So for every `m`, the hop list is P10-C's full hop *ranking*
(before its depth-100 cut) restricted to the candidates that hold a seed, then cut at 100. Every
candidate keeps its score and its relative order; the list refills to depth 100 from below
P10-C's cut (at `m = 1`, 152,051 listed units in 3,516 questions are outside the `m = all` list),
and the fusion's min-max normalization of the hop component changes with the list. Filtering can
raise a gold paragraph only by removing competitors ranked above it, and it drops the gold
outright whenever none of its bridge entities is a seed.

**Measured.** The net effect on the 2,651 bridge items is negative at every `m`: the first-5 count
falls from 590 to 568, 541 and 429, and Full Support follows. How many bridges the filter dropped
and how many it promoted was not computed separately; that the drops dominate is the natural
reading of the net counts (W puts a bridge first 40.78 % of the time and among its first 3
entities 75.52 %), not a measured split.

**Interpretation, a hypothesis.** The phase converted a good but imperfect *ranking* of entities
into a hard *filter* on candidates. A ranking that is right four times in ten is valuable as a
weight and harmful as a gate. What the screen shows is a signal worth using softly: raising the
candidates that share the best-scored entities without removing the rest. The spec excluded that
form ("weight seeds softly"), so it remains untested; this phase says nothing measured about it.

## Ceilings (exploratory, dev, gold-informed)

Computed after `DEV_STOP`, at the author's request, before the next phase is specified. They
bound how much room each lever of the hop has. **They read the gold paragraphs to build every
list, so none is a system, and they select nothing.** No candidate-to-question similarity was
computed. Script and output: `data/phase13/ceilings/`, not versioned (sha256 `ceilings.py`
`ddd81b45…09bb4`, `ceilings.txt` `ef4bc0ba…e8224`, `ceilings.json` `3a5a58a4…de0b`). It reads
the Phase 10 dev lists (`d2e5c2d6…b7e9`) and the Phase 9 entity index (`0cf28b3f…baaa`), both
read-only, and took 497.7 s on the laptop.

**Method.** Each ceiling replaces every dev question's hop list inside P10-C. The ceiling list is
then fused with the unchanged Dense and BM25 lists at the frozen 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2 weights, and
scored on Full Support @2,048. The items are the 2,651 of the screen (`phase13.screen_population`),
in 2,540 questions. A question with no item keeps P10-C's hop list under every ceiling. Checks:
rebuilt from the entity index, the P10-C hop list equals Phase 10's for all 7,405 questions, and
the Phase 12 per-item oracle is reproduced at 2,055 in the list and 1,131 in the first 5.

| Ceiling | What the oracle does | Score the fusion sees |
|---|---|---|
| C1 perfect seed | hops only from the union of the question's bridge entities | the seeded hop's own rarity sums |
| C2 perfect soft weight | keeps every P1 entity, orders by bridge-entity weight first, then P10-C's score | `1e6 × bridge sum + P10-C score` (order checked monotone: 0 violations) |
| C3 perfect tie-break | P10-C's scores, each item first inside its tie group, then the cut at 100 | P10-C's, unchanged |
| C4 perfect reorder of the candidate set | every item the hop reaches goes to the top of the hop list | the list's top score for items, P10-C's for the rest |

### Derived: why a pure tie-break is worth exactly zero here

C3 is +0, with no question moved either way, although it lifts 308 items into the hop's first 5.
This follows from the fusion, not from the oracle:

- the weighted fusion min-max normalizes the hop list per question and gives an absent unit
  `ABSENT_FLOOR = 0.0` (`retrieval/fusion.py`);
- a tie-break that leaves scores unchanged can only change which members of the tie group that
  crosses the cut enter the list, and that group holds the list's lowest score, which normalizes
  to 0, the same value an absent unit gets;
- inside the list, reordering tied units changes no fused score, and `fuse` breaks fused ties by
  unit id.

So under P10-C's fusion, any rule that only reorders within the hop's ties, including cos(q, c)
as a tie-breaker, cannot change a single question. To act, the question signal has to change the
**score** the fusion reads.

### Interpretation (a reading, not a measurement)

- **The candidate side has the largest room.** Putting the reachable evidence at the top of the
  hop list is worth up to +1,257 questions on dev, about 38 times the usual bar. It is an upper
  bound built from the gold, not an estimate of what any similarity can reach.
- **Soft weighting keeps most of what a perfect seed choice buys and loses almost nothing.** C2
  gains 641 and loses 2. C1, the hard seed filter, gains 219 but loses 100. At the ceiling, as in
  Phase 13's measured fit, removal is what costs.
- **A pure tie-break has no room at all** in this fusion (derived above). A candidate-side phase
  has to use the similarity as part of the hop's score, not only to break its ties.
- **Overlap with Dense is large.** 44 % of the items (1,154 / 2,651) are already in Dense's
  ranks 11-100, where the fusion already gives them Dense weight. A hop scored by cos(q, c)
  re-asks Dense's own question for those. The ceilings do not say how much of C4's room lies in
  the other 56 %.

### Reach ceilings: what widening the hop could add (exploratory, dev, gold-informed)

Computed next, at the author's request, to weigh entity canonicalization (roadmap 8.1) against
candidate-side ordering before the next phase. Script and output: `data/phase13/ceilings/`, not
versioned (sha256 `canonical.py` `d395c3fd…2f2b`, `canonical.txt` `e938f80a…0436`,
`canonical.json` `3f792bbd…578a`), same read-only inputs, 243.9 s.

There is no ground truth for "these two forms are the same entity", so **these are string
proxies, not an oracle**. They are applied to the gold paragraphs outside the Dense top 10 that
share **no** entity node with P1:

- **strict:** some P1 entity form and some gold entity form, normalized, where one is a full
  contiguous word sequence of the other ("cameron" in "james cameron");
- **lenient:** the two sides share a content word (stopwords and 1-character words excluded);
- **title:** P1's sentences mention the gold paragraph's article title, with the parenthetical
  disambiguator dropped. This is a proxy for entity *linking*, not canonicalization.

A canonicalizer should land between strict (misses aliases) and lenient (merges "Paris" with
"Paris Hilton"). Each fused ceiling is C4 plus the newly reachable items, all at the top of the
hop list with the list's top score, fused at 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2. Rebuilt this way, C4 gives 6,058
again.

**Derived.** On top of perfect ordering, widening the reach adds +157 (strict) to +282 (lenient)

+1,257. At the ceiling, the ordering lever is roughly 4-8 times the reach lever.

**Interpretation (a reading).** The reach ceilings assume the new paragraphs land at the top of
the hop list. A canonicalized hop would add them to larger candidate sets, where today's ordering
already leaves 1,075 of the 1,665 listed gold paragraphs outside the hop's first 5. Reach without
a better ordering would likely bury the new evidence the same way. This favours candidate-side
ordering first, with canonicalization after it; it is a reading of ceilings, not a measurement of
either system.

## Limitations

1. **Dev has now been fitted by Phases 10-13.** The 7,405 validation questions remain valid for
   selection, not as held-out evidence.
2. **The screen's population and bridge definition come from Phase 12's post-hoc diagnostic** on
   the same dev questions. The screen tested one fixed signal and chose nothing, but the
   population was not defined blind.
3. **The bridge set is lenient.** Any P1 entity the gold also holds counts, generic ones included,
   so the 40.78 % is a hit rate against that definition, not proof that the entity is the one the
   question needs.
4. **Windows are cut from normalized text** (lower-cased, no diacritics, separators as spaces).
   Normalization also drops a leading `the`, `a` or `an` and edge punctuation from the whole
   sentence, so a window at a sentence start can lose that word. BGE-small lower-cases anyway; the
   effect of the rest was not measured.
5. **Window vectors were encoded on the laptop CPU**, dev question vectors on Phase 9's rented GPU.
   The numeric difference was not measured.
6. **Latency was not measured for P13**, because no held-out pass ran.
7. **Code for the pass exists only in part.** S4 added `OnlineWindows`, needed only by a pass, and
   S1 added pass-only constants. They were written before the gate fired, cannot open `test-11` on
   their own, and are exercised only by their unit tests.

## Next research decision

Phase 13 closes with `DEV_STOP`. P10-C stays the line's best system, and `test-11` stays reserved
and unopened.

**Measured.** An entity-level signal exists: the window score puts a bridge entity first for
40.78 % of the 2,651 bridge-sharing gold paragraphs, against 19.01 % for the best trivial rule and
20.07 % for Phase 12's sentence signal restated per entity. Used as a hard seed filter, it lowers
the hop's first-5 count at every `m` (429-568 against 590) and Full Support with it.

**Interpretation, a hypothesis.** Phases 12 and 13 both used the question to *remove* part of what
the query-blind hop finds. Phase 13 suggests that the removal, not the signal, is what fails. The
question the line now faces is how to use an imperfect entity ranking without dropping what it
gets wrong — for example as a weight on the hop's score. That form was excluded by this spec and
is untested. Which direction comes next — that, typed relations for P1's entities (roadmap 8.2),
or something else — is the author's decision.
