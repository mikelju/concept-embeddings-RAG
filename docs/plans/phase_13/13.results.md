## Why it did not help

**Derived from the design, not a new measurement.** Under D2, a candidate's score is the sum of
rarity weights over *all* the entities it shares with P1, whatever `m` is; the seeds only decide
which candidates are admitted. So for every `m`, the hop list is P10-C's full hop *ranking*
(before its depth-100 cut) restricted to the candidates that hold a seed, then cut at 100. Every
candidate keeps its score and its relative order; the list refills to depth 100 from below
P10-C's cut (at `m = 1`, 152,051 listed units in 3,516 questions are outside the `m = all` list),
and the fusion's min-max normalization of the hop component changes with the list. Filtering can
raise a gold paragraph only by removing competitors ranked above it, and it drops the gold
outright whenever none of its bridge entities is a seed.

**Measured.** The net effect on the 2,651 bridge items is negative at every `m`: the first-5 count
falls from 590 to 568, 541 and 429, and Full Support follows. How many bridges the filter dropped
and how many it promoted was not computed separately; that the drops dominate is the natural
reading of the net counts (W puts a bridge first 40.78 % of the time and among its first 3
entities 75.52 %), not a measured split.

**Interpretation, a hypothesis.** The phase converted a good but imperfect *ranking* of entities
into a hard *filter* on candidates. A ranking that is right four times in ten is valuable as a
weight and harmful as a gate. What the screen shows is a signal worth using softly: raising the
candidates that share the best-scored entities without removing the rest. The spec excluded that
form ("weight seeds softly"), so it remains untested; this phase says nothing measured about it.

## Limitations

1. **Dev has now been fitted by Phases 10-13.** The 7,405 validation questions remain valid for
   selection, not as held-out evidence.
2. **The screen's population and bridge definition come from Phase 12's post-hoc diagnostic** on
   the same dev questions. The screen tested one fixed signal and chose nothing, but the
   population was not defined blind.
3. **The bridge set is lenient.** Any P1 entity the gold also holds counts, generic ones included,
   so the 40.78 % is a hit rate against that definition, not proof that the entity is the one the
   question needs.
4. **Windows are cut from normalized text** (lower-cased, no diacritics, separators as spaces).
   Normalization also drops a leading `the`, `a` or `an` and edge punctuation from the whole
   sentence, so a window at a sentence start can lose that word. BGE-small lower-cases anyway; the
   effect of the rest was not measured.
5. **Window vectors were encoded on the laptop CPU**, dev question vectors on Phase 9's rented GPU.
   The numeric difference was not measured.
6. **Latency was not measured for P13**, because no held-out pass ran.
7. **Code for the pass exists only in part.** S4 added `OnlineWindows`, needed only by a pass, and
   S1 added pass-only constants. They were written before the gate fired, cannot open `test-11` on
   their own, and are exercised only by their unit tests.

## Next research decision

Phase 13 closes with `DEV_STOP`. P10-C stays the line's best system, and `test-11` stays reserved
and unopened.

**Measured.** An entity-level signal exists: the window score puts a bridge entity first for
40.78 % of the 2,651 bridge-sharing gold paragraphs, against 19.01 % for the best trivial rule and
20.07 % for Phase 12's sentence signal restated per entity. Used as a hard seed filter, it lowers
the hop's first-5 count at every `m` (429-568 against 590) and Full Support with it.

**Interpretation, a hypothesis.** Phases 12 and 13 both used the question to *remove* part of what
the query-blind hop finds. Phase 13 suggests that the removal, not the signal, is what fails. The
question the line now faces is how to use an imperfect entity ranking without dropping what it
gets wrong — for example as a weight on the hop's score. That form was excluded by this spec and
is untested. Which direction comes next — that, typed relations for P1's entities (roadmap 8.2),
or something else — is the author's decision.
